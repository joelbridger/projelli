**Medicare and IRMAA.** This is exactly the right concern to raise. IRMAA (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount) is a surcharge added to Medicare Part B and Part D premiums when your modified adjusted gross income exceeds certain thresholds. For 2024, the first surcharge kicks in above $206,000 for a married couple filing jointly. Based on our current projections, a $48,000 conversion keeps you well below that level, so we do not expect any IRMAA exposure this year. We track this carefully in Holistiplan and will flag it if anything changes. Once Susan's pension income begins in 2026 and Robert's Social Security starts in 2030, IRMAA management becomes a more active part of the annual planning conversation.

Hi Robert,

Good question on the tax. The short answer: do NOT withhold from the converted amount itself. Withholding from an IRA conversion is treated as a distribution, which reduces the amount that actually ends up in the Roth and can create penalties if you are under 59.5 (you are not, but the principle matters). The right approach is to pay the estimated tax separately from non-retirement funds, typically by making a Q4 estimated tax payment. We will add that to your action items for the fall alongside the conversion itself.

The estimated $11,500 is based on the current projection and will be refined when we run the final conversion calculation in October.

Glad to hear you are handling the beneficiary update this week. Please take a screenshot or print the confirmation page at Schwab so we have a record. I will note the completion in your file once you confirm.
