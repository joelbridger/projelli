Would a backdoor Roth make sense for them, and what should I watch out for?
From your files

Their 2024 income put the Webbs above the direct Roth contribution limit, and they still hold about $96k in a pre-tax 401(k) that hasn't been rolled over yet. 4 5

General guidance

A backdoor Roth lets higher earners fund a Roth indirectly — contribute to a traditional IRA, then convert it. The big thing to flag for them: the IRS pro-rata rule counts existing pre-tax IRA money toward the conversion, so a large pre-tax balance can make most of the converted amount taxable. That's why the sequence matters — rolling that old 401(k) into a new employer plan first (instead of an IRA) can keep the backdoor clean.

General knowledge, not from the Webbs' files. Contribution limits and rules change — confirm current figures before you advise.
