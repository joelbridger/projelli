Local-first vs cloud AI: a tradeoffs guide for attorneys, CPAs, and consultants.

Updated 2026-04-27 · 11 min read

The short version. Local-first AI keeps your data on your machine and uses your own AI provider key. Cloud AI keeps your data on the vendor's servers and bundles inference into the subscription. Each has real trade-offs. Local-first wins on privacy, cost, and longevity. Cloud wins on convenience, multi-device sync, and lower setup friction. For attorneys, CPAs, and consultants specifically, the decision usually comes down to how much of your client work and confidential files you're willing to put on someone else's database.

Both architectures work. Both have legitimate use cases. The mistake is using "local-first" as a virtue word and "cloud" as an insult, or the other way around. Each is the right answer in different circumstances. This page maps which is which.

Quick definitions

Cloud AI workspace: A web or desktop app where your conversations, files, and projects live on the vendor's servers. The AI is bundled into the subscription. Examples: Notion AI, ChatGPT Plus, Claude.ai Pro, Reflect, Mem.ai, Tana.

Local-first AI workspace: A desktop app where your conversations and files live on your machine in standard formats. You bring your own API key for the AI provider. Examples: Keepance, Cursor with BYOK, Continue.dev, Obsidian with AI plugins.

There's a third architecture, fully local AI, where the model itself runs on your machine via Ollama or LM Studio. We'll touch on it; the bulk of this comparison is local-first vs cloud.

When cloud AI is the right answer

Cloud AI is genuinely better in three specific situations.

1. You work across many devices and need real-time sync

If you flip between a laptop, an iPad, and a phone all day and need every change to appear on all three within a few seconds, cloud AI wins. Local-first with Dropbox or iCloud as the sync layer works, but adds a 10-30 second sync delay and occasionally requires a "reopen the file" step. For a professional who lives in their browser tabs, cloud's smooth multi-device experience is worth the trade-off.

2. You collaborate with a team in real-time

Multi-user editing on the same document, comments, mentions, presence indicators. Cloud workspaces (Notion, Google Docs) own this. Local-first tools are deliberately single-user. If your work involves editing the same doc with another person at the same time, cloud is the right shape.

3. You want zero setup friction

For a non-technical user who just wants to type and have AI help, ChatGPT.com is genuinely easier. No API key, no desktop install, no configuration. The cost is privacy, longevity, and lock-in, but the convenience is real and not everyone wants to make that trade.

When local-first is the right answer

Local-first wins in five situations that map well to attorney, CPA, and consultant workflows.

1. The content is sensitive enough that vendor exposure matters

Client files, legal memos, tax work papers, case strategy notes, engagement letters, confidential analysis. Anything you'd be uncomfortable seeing in a court filing or a vendor's data breach report. For attorneys, ABA Formal Opinion 512 makes this concrete: cloud AI tools can create privilege waiver issues. For tax professionals, IRC §7216 makes unauthorized disclosure of client data a federal criminal misdemeanor. Local-first keeps the authoritative copy on your machine; the only data that ever touches a server is the specific text you choose to send for inference.

2. You want to own your archive over years

Two years into a serious solo practice, you'll have hundreds of client conversations and work-product drafts. Searchable across all of them. Linkable between them. With cloud, this archive lives in the vendor's database and is theirs to retain, change format on, or sunset. With local-first, the archive is a folder of Markdown files. You can grep it. You can move it. You can read it in 20 years.

3. You're cost-sensitive and your usage is below subscription thresholds

The flat $20/month for ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro pays off only if you use AI heavily and consistently. Most independent professionals have lumpy usage, $0 some weeks and $50 of API tokens in others. Local-first BYOK matches this lumpiness. You pay for what you actually use.

4. You're legally or professionally uncomfortable with vendor-controlled client data

This isn't just a preference, it's a professional obligation for many practitioners. Attorneys have duty-of-confidentiality rules. CPAs have IRC §7216. Consultants have NDA obligations. Local-first is the only architecture that keeps client data off the vendor's servers entirely. If you're in this group, it's not a close call.

5. You're already in the local-first ecosystem

If your editor is Obsidian, your password manager is on your machine (1Password local vault), your documents live in a folder-based system, and your habits are already "data on my device", a local-first AI workspace fits the existing pattern. Adding a cloud workspace creates a friction point in an otherwise consistent setup.

Things people get wrong about both

Myth: cloud AI is always more "powerful"

Not really. The model is the same. Claude in ChatGPT-style cloud chat is the same Claude available via the Anthropic API in a local-first BYOK app. The interface differs; the inference is identical. "Powerful" usually means "polished UI", which is a real thing but not a model capability.

Myth: local-first is always more private

Mostly true, with one caveat. The data on your machine is more private than data on a vendor's server. But the data you send to the AI provider for inference is sitting on the AI provider's logs (usually for 30 days, sometimes longer). Local-first reduces your exposure surface; it doesn't eliminate it. To eliminate it, you need fully local AI (Ollama / LM Studio), which trades model quality.

Myth: local-first is hard to set up

It's slightly harder than ChatGPT.com. The whole setup is: sign up at the AI provider's developer portal (3 minutes), generate a key (1 minute), paste it into the desktop app (30 seconds). Five minutes total. Less time than installing Zoom for the first time. People exaggerate this friction.

Myth: cloud AI can't be private

Some cloud AI tools have strong privacy guarantees. Notion AI is contractually clear that customer data doesn't train models. Anthropic's Claude.ai has improved its consumer-tier policy. Cloud isn't automatically a privacy disaster; you just have to read the actual terms, which most users don't.

Most independent attorneys, CPAs, and consultants end up with: local-first for client work and confidential content (Keepance or similar), cloud for team docs (Notion or Google Docs), and ChatGPT.com or Claude.ai for disposable lookups. Three tools, each playing to its strength.

What about local AI (Ollama, LM Studio)?

The third architecture: the AI itself runs on your machine. No internet required for inference. Open-weight models (Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek) running via Ollama or LM Studio.

Trade-off: model quality. In 2026, open-weight models are competitive with cloud frontier models on many tasks but lag on others. For long-form strategic synthesis, deep reasoning, and very long context, cloud frontier models are still better. For drafting, code, and short-form tasks, open-weight is often good enough.

The realistic 2026 stack for a privacy-conscious attorney or CPA: local-first BYOK for cloud-AI-quality work, plus Ollama configured as a fallback provider for the most sensitive content. Keepance supports both side-by-side; route per conversation based on the sensitivity.

The decision framework, in two sentences

If your work is mostly individual client work or confidential content that you need to own and protect, pick local-first. If your work is mostly real-time team collaboration on shared documents, pick cloud.

The right answer for a solo attorney, CPA, or consultant is almost always local-first for client work and confidential content, with cloud tools alongside for team-collab and disposable-query slots.
