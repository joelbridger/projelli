Where Copilot genuinely wins

If you already pay for Microsoft 365, Copilot is a relatively inexpensive add-on and it fits naturally into how you already work. It can draft a real Word document, build a PowerPoint from an outline, summarize an Outlook thread, and do it all inside the apps you have open every day. That's a real advantage, and it's why most consultants and corporate knowledge workers already have it.

The no-training guarantee in the enterprise tier is also real. Microsoft contractually commits not to use your tenant data to train its models. Your data stays inside Microsoft's infrastructure, which for many organizations is a level of security they've already cleared in their vendor-approval process.

And if you need a polished .pptx or a formatted .docx quickly, Copilot delivers in a way Keepance currently does not. Keepance produces Markdown and a basic export. If the deliverable is a finished, branded document, Copilot (or Gamma for decks) is ahead.

Where the architectures diverge

The core difference is where your work lives when it's done. With Copilot, your documents live in OneDrive and SharePoint, your email stays in Exchange Online, and the AI interactions happen inside Microsoft's infrastructure. That's fine for most office work. For a lawyer whose client communications and draft filings have to stay privileged, a CPA handling return data under IRC Section 7216, a consultant under a strict no-AI-upload NDA clause, or an RIA managing Reg S-P vendor oversight, "inside Microsoft's tenant" is still a vendor's servers, not your machine.

Keepance imports your email and stores it locally, encrypted on your machine. It keeps every AI conversation as a plain Markdown file in a folder you control. With a local model running on your machine via Ollama, nothing leaves at all, including the queries you run over your imported mail. That's a different architecture, not just a different privacy policy, and it's the one that satisfies the strictest confidentiality requirements in each vertical.

The email contrast is sharp. Copilot's Outlook integration is powerful precisely because it reads and summarizes your mail in Microsoft's cloud. Keepance's email import works the other direction: your Outlook, IMAP, or Gmail is pulled onto your machine, indexed locally, and searched from there. The AI never reads your mail on a remote server. If you use a cloud API key for AI queries over your mail, the prompt goes to that provider, but your stored mail never leaves your disk.

Who adds Keepance even if they have Copilot

A lot of professionals end up with both. Copilot for the everyday Office work. Keepance for the client-facing drafts, confidential analysis, and anything that involves work product they'd rather not route through any vendor's cloud. The profiles that add Keepance specifically:

  • Attorneys handling privileged communications, draft motions, and client files. Federal courts are actively examining whether cloud AI use defeats privilege (see US v. Heppner, S.D.N.Y., Feb 2026). A local model in Keepance is the cleanest answer. (Informational, not legal advice; verify with your bar counsel.)
  • CPAs and EAs whose client return data is protected under IRC Section 7216. A cloud API key, including Copilot, is a third-party disclosure. A local model in Keepance removes it. (Informational, not tax advice.)
  • Independent consultants under NDAs with explicit no-AI-upload clauses. Enterprise Copilot still uploads to Microsoft. Only a local model honors a blanket no-upload clause literally.
  • Independent RIAs and financial advisors who became subject to the SEC's Reg S-P vendor-oversight requirement as of June 2026. With Keepance on a local model, there is no AI vendor in the data path to oversee. (Informational, not compliance advice; verify with your compliance counsel.)

When to pick Copilot

  • You're already paying for Microsoft 365 and want AI inside Word, Outlook, and Teams with minimal friction.
  • You need real .docx and .pptx output, not Markdown.
  • Your organization has already cleared Microsoft as a vendor and the contractual no-training guarantee is sufficient for your confidentiality requirements.
  • Your main use case is drafting emails and meeting summaries in Outlook, where Copilot's deep integration genuinely shines.

When Keepance is the better fit

  • Your work requires that client data stay on your machine, not a vendor's cloud, whether because of privilege, regulation, or contract.
  • You want your email imported, stored, and searched locally, not read by a remote service.
  • You want profession-specific practice templates (legal, tax, consulting, advisor) that go deeper than general-purpose Office AI.
  • You want your AI conversations to be files you own, on your disk, not ephemeral interactions inside cloud apps.
  • You want the option to run a local model so that nothing, including your email queries, leaves your machine at all.
