Keepance vs Tana.

Tana is a cloud outliner built around supertags, fields, and structured nodes. It's powerful, polished, and beloved by people who think in graphs. Keepance is a local-first Word-native workspace with profession-specific practice packs for attorneys, CPAs, and consultants. A professional might love either one; they're different shapes for different brains. The honest split: Tana for "I want to build a knowledge graph and query it." Keepance for "I want my confidential client work in real .docx files I own, with matter-scoped AI recall."

Where Keepance wins

1. Local-first ownership and confidentiality

Tana's data lives in their cloud. Export is available but the working format is theirs. Keepance's data lives on your machine in Markdown. For attorneys, CPAs, and consultants building a long strategic record of client work, the compounding-archive value over multiple years tilts toward Keepance, and the confidentiality requirements may make local-first non-negotiable.

2. Profession-specific practice packs with structured AI interview

Tana has templates (it's actually one of its strengths) but they're general-purpose, user-built. Keepance ships 15 practice packs designed for professional work, each running as a guided AI interview that produces a finished doc. Client intake, engagement letter, matter strategy, financial projections, weekly review.

3. Your key, your provider, nothing in the middle

Tana's AI is bundled into the subscription. Keepance is BYOK: you bring your own Claude / OpenAI / Gemini key and switch per conversation. Your API key lives in your OS keychain and prompts go straight from your machine to the provider. You see exactly what the AI costs, you choose the model per conversation, and sensitive client content never passes through Keepance's servers.

4. Simplicity for documents that aren't graphs

Tana's outliner-plus-supertags model rewards a specific working style. If you're writing engagement letters and matter memos more than building knowledge graphs, the data model is overhead. Keepance's Word-native model fits document-shaped professional work more naturally.

Where Tana wins

1. Structured queries over your knowledge graph

Tana's signature feature is the ability to define a "supertag" (e.g., #person, #project, #meeting) and then query across all instances ("show me all #project where #status = 'active' and #owner = me"). This is a real superpower for users who think in structured data. Keepance has no graph view or structured query; it focuses on document-shaped work with matter-scoped cited recall over your full archive.

2. Networked-thinking polish

Tana's UX for outlining, breadcrumbs, indents, transclusion is the most polished in the category. If you're already a Roam/Logseq/Workflowy user, Tana feels like the natural next step.

3. Mobile

Tana has native iOS and Android apps. Keepance is desktop-only.

4. Real-time collaboration

Tana Pro supports multi-user editing. Keepance is single-user.

Migrating from Tana to Keepance

  1. In Tana, export your workspace as Markdown
  2. The export contains your nodes converted to nested bullet lists in Markdown
  3. Drop into a new Keepance workspace folder
  4. Tana's supertag fields will appear as inline tags or YAML frontmatter (depending on export options); Keepance treats both as searchable text
  5. Tana's Markdown text content imports cleanly; Tana-specific supertag fields will appear as plain text

The biggest adjustment: you lose the structured-query feature. If your Tana workflow depends heavily on supertag queries, Keepance will feel less expressive. If your Tana workflow is mostly outlining + AI assistance, the migration is straightforward and you'll barely notice.
