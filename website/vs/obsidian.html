The real distinction

Obsidian's core promise is that it ships an editor, and the community ships everything else. That's a genuinely good architecture for a personal knowledge base, and it's why Obsidian has grown for years without a single feature that the community couldn't build. But the cost of that architecture is consistency. Smart Connections passed 786,000 community downloads by January 2026 and its stats are on obsidian-stats, which is impressive, but it's still a plugin maintained by one developer that can break with any Obsidian core update. The Copilot for Obsidian plugin reaches 100,000+ users and ships weekly, which is also impressive, but it's a separate codebase with a separate release cycle.

Keepance takes the opposite bet. One polished app, four providers built in, the AI is the primary input method, and every chat conversation produces a real Markdown file in your folder. Where Obsidian's AI plugins treat AI as an enhancement to writing, Keepance treats the conversation as the first draft. You can still edit the file by hand afterward, the same way you would in Obsidian. The difference is in what starts the document. As of v2.0 you can also paste an image into the chat, drop in a PDF for Claude to read natively, hit [Compress] when a long chat overflows the context window, and click "Read aloud" to hear an answer through a local Piper sidecar. Each of those is a separate Obsidian plugin if you want them.

Both Keepance and Obsidian are local-first, which matters a lot for anyone with confidentiality obligations. The important distinction is in how AI is handled. Obsidian's AI plugins typically require you to enter your own API key, which is good, but the plugin routes your prompt to the AI provider through its own code and update cycle. Keepance routes your prompt directly from your machine to the provider endpoint. Nothing goes through Keepance's servers (there are none in this path). For attorneys working under privilege, CPAs bound by IRC Section 7216, and consultants under NDA, the local-first data posture is table stakes, and Keepance makes it explicit by design: documents on your disk, API key in your OS keychain, prompts going straight to the provider.

The second distinction is profession-specific practice packs. Obsidian has a thriving community templates ecosystem, but the good ones (Linking Your Thinking, PARA, various zettelkasten kits) are for personal knowledge work. There is no "run a client intake, produce an engagement letter draft, save it as ENGAGEMENT_LETTER.md" flow anywhere in the Obsidian ecosystem. Keepance ships 15 of those, designed for the specific document types attorneys, CPAs, and consultants actually write. See the blog for what each one actually produces.

The third distinction is the MCP server. Keepance's workspace is exposable to Claude Desktop, Cursor, Zed, and any other MCP-compatible client via a one-click .mcpb install. Obsidian does not ship a first-party MCP server; community projects approximate it but none are production-ready as of April 2026.

When to pick Keepance

  • You want a local-first AI workspace but you've never been willing to spend a weekend assembling Obsidian plugins.
  • You're an attorney, CPA, or consultant who writes the same types of documents repeatedly and wants templates for each.
  • You want chat conversations saved as files automatically, not as ephemeral UI state.
  • You want Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, and Ollama all in one app without four plugins.
  • You want your workspace available to Claude Desktop, Cursor, or Zed via MCP out of the box.
  • Your work involves confidential client information that should stay on your machine, not in a vendor's cloud.

When to pick Obsidian

  • You already have a working Obsidian vault with plugins you trust, and you're editing daily.
  • You want to model a second brain with a rich plugin ecosystem (dataview, tasks, graph view, canvas).
  • You want a free tool forever with a paid sync add-on, with no software purchase required.
  • You need mobile sync today. Obsidian has first-party iOS and Android apps; Keepance is desktop-only.
  • You're happy to assemble your own AI stack from community plugins and prefer that flexibility.

The honest caveat

Obsidian's community could ship a "Professional Practice Workflows" plugin this weekend that closes most of the gap between them and Keepance. That's the nature of its architecture. Obsidian's plugin ecosystem also dwarfs Keepance's. There are 1,500+ Obsidian community plugins. Keepance does not have a plugin ecosystem. Keepance's bet is that a polished, single-developer product with AI as the primary input beats an assemble-it-yourself plugin stack for the specific audience of attorneys, CPAs, and independent consultants who handle confidential client information and need local-first guarantees, even if a community plugin eventually approximates the feature set. If you're a power user who enjoys assembling your own stack, Obsidian remains the right call.
