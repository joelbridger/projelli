Both lean on AI, both have search across your archive, both are single-user. The split: where the data lives and what you're optimizing for. Mem optimizes for "I don't want to manage folders." Keepance optimizes for "I want my confidential client content as files I own."

Where Keepance wins

1. Ownership and longevity

The deeper your archive, the more it matters that your notes are in a format you control. Keepance's notes are Markdown files in a folder. They survive Keepance the company. They're greppable, version-controllable, backup-able by any tool. Mem's are in their cloud; export is available but the working format is theirs.

2. Profession-specific practice structure

Mem's pitch is "AI organizes for you." That works well for general note-taking. Keepance's pitch is "structured workflows that produce professional documents." For client intake drafting, engagement letter synthesis, matter strategy, the structured approach with explicit practice packs is more useful than auto-organization.

3. Your key, your provider, nothing in the middle

Mem bundles inference into the subscription. That means Mem's key, Mem's model selection, and your content going through Mem's infrastructure to reach the AI. With Keepance, you bring your own Claude, OpenAI, or Gemini key. It lives in your OS keychain. When you send a prompt, it goes from your machine straight to the provider. Keepance is not in the data path. For attorneys working under privilege, CPAs handling client returns under IRC §7216, or consultants with NDA-bound client work, that's not a preference. It's often the threshold question: can this content land in a vendor's database without your knowledge or consent? With Keepance, it can't. Your files stay on your machine. Keepance is not in the data path. Your prompts go straight to Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google via your own key, and for the most sensitive matters you can route to a local Ollama model where nothing touches any external server.

4. Privacy posture for confidential client information

Mem's notes live on Mem's servers. Keepance's notes live on your hard drive. Mem's cloud means the company can do AI processing across your full archive (which is the feature) but also means the data sits in their database. For attorneys, CPAs, and consultants handling privileged or confidential client information, that data sitting in a vendor's database is a structural risk, not a matter of taste.

5. Multi-provider flexibility

Mem provides whatever AI they currently bundle. Keepance supports Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, and local Ollama side-by-side. You pick the model per conversation; you can route the most sensitive client content to local Ollama and general work to Claude.

Where Mem.ai wins

1. Automatic organization

If you genuinely don't want to manage folders or tags, Mem's "just write, AI organizes" is real and elegant. You drop notes in, the AI surfaces related ones, you find what you wrote about a topic without having to remember where you put it. Keepance requires more intentional structure (folders, matter organization, file naming).

2. Mobile-first capture

Mem's iOS app makes capturing thoughts on the go genuinely easy. Keepance is desktop-only; mobile capture means another tool (Apple Notes, Drafts, Obsidian Mobile) that exports to your Keepance workspace later.

3. Onboarding friction is lower

Mem: sign up, paste card, start writing. Keepance: download, install, sign up at AI provider, generate API key, paste, start writing. Both are short; Mem's path is more familiar to non-technical users.

4. AI-curated daily summaries

Mem's "today's relevant notes" surfacing is a polished experience. Keepance has full-text search and matter-scoped cited recall, but no AI-curated daily feed.

Migrating from Mem.ai to Keepance

  1. Mem.ai supports plain-text export of all your notes
  2. Download the export and unzip into a folder
  3. Open Keepance and pick that folder as your workspace
  4. Mem's automatic tags don't directly map; consider keeping them as `#hashtag` text in your notes (Keepance's search will index them) or restructuring with folders
  5. Get an Anthropic / OpenAI / Gemini API key, paste into Keepance, start chatting against your imported archive

The biggest adjustment: you go from "Mem organizes my notes" to "I organize my documents manually with folders and matter structure." Some users love this; some find it more work. Worth a 14-day trial of Keepance alongside Mem before committing.

Frequently asked questions

What does Mem.ai do?

Mem.ai is a cloud-based notes app that uses AI to automatically organize your notes, surface related content, and answer questions across your full archive. The pitch is "organize itself" rather than asking you to manage folders or tags.

How is Keepance different from Mem.ai?

Keepance is local-first: notes live on your machine in Markdown files, you bring your own AI key, and the app has profession-specific workflow packs. Mem.ai is cloud-first: notes live on Mem's servers, AI is bundled, and the focus is automatic organization rather than structured professional workflows.

Can I import Mem.ai notes into Keepance?

Mem.ai supports export to plain text. The text files can be dropped into a Keepance workspace folder. Mem's automatic-tag system doesn't have a direct equivalent in Keepance; you'll either keep tags as #hashtags in the file content or rebuild structure with folders.

Which one is more private?

Keepance is more private by architecture: your data lives on your hard drive and the app has no servers in the data path. Mem.ai stores your notes on their servers and processes them through AI; their privacy policy is reasonable but the data does live on a vendor's infrastructure.

Does Mem.ai have profession-specific practice packs?

No. Mem's structure is general-purpose notes with AI organization layered on top. Keepance ships with 15 practice workflow packs designed for professional work: client intake, engagement letter, matter strategy, financial projections, weekly review, and more.

Can I use both at the same time?

Yes. Some professionals use Mem for general note-taking on mobile and Keepance for structured client and matter docs on desktop. They serve different parts of the workflow.
