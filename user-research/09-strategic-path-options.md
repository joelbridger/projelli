# 09 — Four paths: how much of this research to act on

*Requested by Jameson after the 2026-07-10 research synthesis. Four fully-built-out options,
from least to most ambitious. Each path answers the same five questions: what we act on from
the research, what we build, how the story we tell changes, what has to be true for this
path to be the right one, and how we'd know it's working or failing. Evidence IDs (E-###)
point to the ledger (01). Relative sizes only (S/M/L/XL), no time promises.*

**The dial these paths turn.** The research produced three tiers of evidence:
1. **Proven pains** — things she actually does and suffers (scattered truth, the many-hands
   relay, template lock-in, scheduling chase). Safest to act on.
2. **Co-authored solutions** — ideas that came out of the conversation between her and
   Jameson (the triage coach, reports, quick capture). Real signal, unvalidated shape.
3. **Aspirations** — her hope that Lantern replaces Wealthbox and Jump entirely (E-110).
   The most exciting and least reliable tier.

Conservative acts on almost nothing yet. Standard acts on tier 1. Aggressive acts on tiers
1 and 2. Extra-aggressive acts on all three, including the aspiration.

---

# PATH 1 — CONSERVATIVE: "Validate before you build"

## The idea in one sentence
Treat the session as hypotheses only: fix words, merge what's already finished, run the
follow-up research, and change nothing strategic until strangers confirm it.

## What we act on from the research
- The copy fixes (B5): "firm" not "practice" (E-001), kill the "exported from RightCapital"
  framing that confused her twice across two sessions (E-002/E-003), remove law-firm
  connector names from advisor screens (E-008), benefit-first wording (E-005/E-006).
- The intake merge decision (B4) — the work is already built and verified; merging it is
  housekeeping, not new ambition.
- The full follow-up research plan (07): 8–12 paid interviews with strangers, usability
  probes, her recruiting offer.
- Everything else — including the "Source of Truth" reframe — waits for replication.

## What we build
Almost nothing new. Optionally one throwaway demo (the umbrella-policy scenario) used ONLY
as a research stimulus in interviews, not as a product commitment.

## How the story changes
It doesn't. Current positioning, current flagship (Client Map), current roadmap stand.

## What has to be true for this to be the right path
- The single-participant risk outweighs the value of moving now.
- The market window is forgiving — Jump and others won't close the template/source-of-truth
  gaps while we study them.
- The team's constraint is direction-confidence, not momentum.

## Pros
- **Lowest risk of building the wrong thing.** Every concern in the counteranalysis
  (spouse bias, interviewer leading, n=1) is fully respected.
- **Cheap.** Small copy diffs plus interviews; nearly all engineering capacity stays on
  hardening/demo work already in flight.
- **Sets up better decisions.** In a few weeks you'd re-run this same four-path choice with
  8–12 more voices behind it.
- **No positioning whiplash.** The website, demo scripts, and board story stay stable.

## Cons
- **It ignores the strongest finding.** "What's current, with proof" was behavior-grounded,
  repeated, and matches rails we already have — waiting on it costs the one clear win.
- **The research momentum decays.** She handed us a recruiting path and two demo scenarios;
  sitting on them wastes warm leads and her goodwill.
- **The board's own stance says the opposite.** The 2026-06-25 CEO decision was "all-in on
  first users" — this path produces zero user-facing progress while we study.
- **Validation without a demo is weaker.** Interviewing strangers about "would you want
  dated answers?" is far less reliable than showing the umbrella demo and watching. The
  research plan itself (07 §5) says probes need prototypes.
- **False safety.** Doing nothing is also a bet — on the status quo roadmap, which this
  research actively challenged (map staleness, Word emphasis, briefs never requested).

## Failure mode
Three weeks pass, the interviews confirm what the session already said, and we've spent the
window learning what we knew. Meanwhile the product's flagship language (living map) keeps
selling the thing the research flagged as a trust risk.

## Cost / reversibility / signals
- **Cost:** S. **Reversibility:** total.
- **Working:** interviews replicate the top themes fast; a sharper Path-2 decision follows.
- **Failing (kill signal):** recruiting stalls or interviews drag past a few weeks with no
  decision — at which point escalate to Path 2 anyway.

# PATH 2 — STANDARD: "The Source of Truth release" (the synthesis recommendation)

## The idea in one sentence
Act fully on the proven pains: make Lantern the place an advisor asks "what's current on
this client, with proof?", and the safest path from a meeting to their own template, their
own tasks, their own CRM — while research runs in parallel to gate the rest.

## What we act on from the research
- **Tier 1 (proven pains) — build:** dated, cited, conflict-aware answers (★B1, E-066/067/
  104/128); meeting notes in the firm's own template with per-item approve-and-route into
  Wealthbox tasks, plus the internal-only "inside scoop" lane (★B2, E-032/050-052); CRM
  write depth — assignees, workflows, custom fields (B3, E-049/092); intake merge + first
  real pilot (B4); all copy fixes (B5); slide OCR so their PowerPoint decks become readable
  (E-100–E-102).
- **Tier 2 (co-authored) — prototype only:** triage coach, scheduling due-list, verified
  1099 sending, practice reports (C1–C4) as cheap mockups tested in the follow-up interviews.
- **Messaging shift:** flagship language moves from "a living Client Map" to "current,
  dated, cited answers whenever you ask" (00 insight 6). Compliance/privacy stays as the
  gate-opener; capability becomes the headline (00 insight 5).
- The follow-up research (07) runs in parallel and gates every tier-2 build.

## What we build (scope)
B1 (M) + B2 (M/L) + B3 (S/M) + B4 (S) + B5 (S) + slide OCR (S/M). Roughly one focused
release. All of it sits on rails the gap analysis confirmed exist (06).

## How the story changes
Same product category (private AI workspace beside your CRM), sharper promise: *"Ask
anything about a client — get the current answer, dated and cited. End a meeting — get your
notes in your format and your tasks in your CRM, approved by you."* Demoed with the two
scenarios she designed (umbrella policy; HSA/benefits).

## What has to be true
- The what's-current question-class is frequent beyond her firm (open question D1 — but the
  build is cheap enough that partial generality still pays).
- Template lock-in is common enough that fidelity is a wedge, not custom work per firm (D2).
- We can carry dates through retrieval without wrecking answer quality (engineering risk,
  believed small per 06).

## Pros
- **Every build traces to behavior-grounded evidence.** Nothing rides on primed enthusiasm.
- **Highest evidence-per-dollar.** The gap analysis sized these as the shortest paths on
  existing rails; the "one primitive, five needs" date layer pays five times.
- **It attacks Jump where Jump already lost this firm** (template→tasks, E-030/032) instead
  of where Jump is strong (bot notetaking).
- **Trust-compounding.** Dates-on-answers and approval routing deepen the product's existing
  trust story rather than adding surface area — consistent with the board's simplicity rule.
- **Reversible messaging.** The reframe is words and demo scripts, not architecture; if
  stranger-testing says "map" language sells better, we can walk it back cheaply.
- **Research still runs.** Tier-2 bets get validated before they're built; the paths stay
  open.

## Cons
- **It may under-reach.** If the research is more right than we can prove yet, tier-2 items
  (the daily triage surface especially) are where daily-active usage lives — Path 2 delays
  the stickiest feature.
- **The reframe has switching costs now.** Website, demo videos, board narrative, and the
  onboarding tour all say "Client Map" today; changing the spine of the pitch mid-GTM has
  real coordination cost even if each edit is small.
- **Still n=1.** We'd be building tier-1 items on one participant's pains. Mitigated (they
  are behavior-grounded and cheap), not eliminated.
- **Two-front focus risk.** A release theme + a research program at once needs coordination
  discipline; the research could quietly starve.

## Failure mode
The umbrella demo lands flat with stranger advisors — "nice, but I'd just call the client" —
and the release theme turns out to be an ops-role pain, not an advisor-buyer pain. (This is
exactly what the parallel interviews are designed to catch early, before B2's larger spend.)

## Cost / reversibility / signals
- **Cost:** M overall. **Reversibility:** high (features are additive; messaging is words).
- **Working:** pilot advisors answer "what's current" via Ask instead of hunting (and click
  the dates); a pilot firm accepts generated notes as "ours" without edits; intake completes
  a real household.
- **Kill signals:** interviews fail to replicate the source-of-truth pain (rework theme);
  template ingestion turns out to be per-firm consulting work (drop B2's template half,
  keep task routing).

# PATH 3 — AGGRESSIVE: "Own the advisor's day"

## The idea in one sentence
Everything in Path 2, PLUS build the co-authored tier on conviction — triage, scheduling,
safe sending, practice reports, firm process answers — so Lantern isn't just where you ask
questions, it's the surface an advisor opens every morning.

## What we act on from the research
- Tiers 1 AND 2. The hedges and provenance caveats on tier 2 (skeptic §4a/4b) are consciously
  overridden by conviction: the pains under them (dumb red task lists E-119, the scheduling
  cascade E-080–086, the 1099 nail-biter E-094, judgment trapped in two heads E-122) are
  real even where the solution shapes were co-authored.
- **Build:** morning triage proposal over CRM tasks + calendar (C1, M); scheduling due-list
  with drafted outreach and batch approve (C2, M); verified-recipient sending + account
  registry (C3, S/M each); whole-practice Ask exposed + engagement/practice reports (C4,
  S/M — deliberately reversing the "CRM-shaped, skip it" scope-out, 06 #6); firm process
  assistant ("how do we do X here", O9, M); quick capture to CRM (O8, S).
- **Strategic posture:** Lantern becomes "the intelligence layer over your whole stack" —
  the goal is to make Jump irrelevant at small RIAs and make Wealthbox feel like the dumb
  database behind Lantern.

## How the story changes
From "a private AI workspace" to **"the first thing you open every morning."** The demo
stops being a Q&A moment and becomes a day: morning triage → pre-meeting recall → meeting →
your-template notes → approved tasks → the system chases the scheduling and the documents.

## What has to be true
- The tier-2 pains generalize AND our solution shapes are close enough to right without
  prototype validation (this is the big bet — the research explicitly could not confirm it).
- A 6-person firm will let a new tool orchestrate its task list and client outreach drafts
  (deeper workflow penetration than answering questions — a bigger compliance and habit ask).
- The team can ship roughly 2× Path 2's surface area without quality erosion — against the
  repo's own history of "more surface = more trust-breaker hunting" (the July QA cycles).

## Pros
- **If the research is right, this is the sticky product.** Q&A gets used weekly; a triage
  surface gets used daily. Daily-active is what makes "leading advisor AI" credible.
- **Covers the whole emotional arc of the research** — not just "find the truth" but "get
  through the day without dread" (E-053), which is the deepest thing she said.
- **Blocks the incumbent's counter-move.** If Jump ships template fidelity next quarter,
  Path 2's wedge narrows; Path 3's breadth (scheduling, sending, triage, process answers)
  is much harder to fast-follow.
- **The engagement report and triage give the manager and ops personas reasons to champion
  the product** — Path 2 mostly serves her role and the advisor.

## Cons
- **It builds unvalidated shapes.** The triage coach arrived inside Jameson's pitch; manager
  reports she floated with "I don't know what exactly we would do with that" (E-124). Path 3
  spends real engineering on tier-2 confidence levels the skeptic lane rated medium-low.
- **Surface-area explosion vs the simplicity stance.** The board's 2026-06-29 decision was
  win on simplicity; six new surfaces risks recreating the "connect 60 things" heaviness we
  criticize in Jump — the failure mode the research participant herself punishes (E-038/039
  overwhelm, battery drain).
- **Trust exposure multiplies.** Outreach drafts, triage advice, and reports are all
  surfaces that can be visibly wrong or stale — her named one-strike trust killers (E-072).
  Path 2 has two such surfaces to harden; Path 3 has seven.
- **Compliance-adjacent features get heavier.** Client outreach drafting and engagement
  cadences edge toward marketing/communication territory that firms' compliance officers
  scrutinize (and that the 07-08 session taught us is the buying gate).
- **Opportunity cost against GTM.** The CEO stance is first-users-first; Path 3 is a big
  build program. More building, later selling — the exact pattern the June evaluation
  ("~zero traction") warned about.

## Failure mode
Six surfaces ship at "pretty good"; none at "trustworthy"; a pilot advisor hits one stale
report and one wrong triage suggestion in week one, and the product's core trust story —
the thing the research says is everything — takes the hit. (The mitigations: stagger the
tier-2 features behind flag gates; ship each only after its own mini-pilot.)

## Cost / reversibility / signals
- **Cost:** L overall (2×+ Path 2). **Reversibility:** medium — features can be flagged off,
  but "the morning surface" positioning is harder to retreat from than a Q&A promise.
- **Working:** pilot users open Lantern before Wealthbox in the morning (measurable in
  local analytics); scheduling batch-sends replace the manual cascade at a pilot firm.
- **Kill signals:** triage suggestions get dismissed >half the time; outreach drafts stall
  in compliance review at pilot firms; QA finds trust-breaker-class bugs in the new surfaces.

# PATH 4 — EXTRA-AGGRESSIVE: "Replace Wealthbox"

## The idea in one sentence
Act on her aspiration (E-110): build Lantern into the firm's actual system of record —
contacts, households, notes, tasks, workflows, team coordination — so a small RIA runs on
Lantern and cancels Wealthbox (and Jump).

## What we'd be acting on from the research
- Tier 3: "I've been kind of hoping that you would just replace Wealthbox and combine a lot
  of softwares into one... an assistant, a source of truth, amazing searchability" (E-110).
- Her belief that Wealthbox is technically simple ("a way to display a database of people...
  pretty basic", E-111) and her prediction that Jump becomes a full CRM within five years
  (E-112).
- The genuinely broken things a rebuild could fix BY DESIGN rather than by patch: template
  changes that don't propagate to 40 open workflows (E-098/099), dumb equal-weight task
  lists (E-119), keyword-brittle search (E-019), unsearchable email (E-020), six-people-six-
  styles data entry (E-108), information-stored-as-tasks (E-074/075).

## What we'd build (honest scope, from the gap analysis)
Everything in Paths 2+3, PLUS a real CRM core, none of which exists today (06):
- Contact/household/relationship database as first-class records (today `Matter` is a
  single container; 06 #21). **L**
- A task system: assignees, due dates, recurrence, priorities, notifications. **L**
- A workflow engine with templates AND propagation-to-open-instances (the fix for their
  named defect — this would be a genuine better-than-Wealthbox moment). **L**
- Team-wide multi-user operation as the DEFAULT: presence, notifications, shared queues —
  all under the end-to-end-encryption architecture, where every collaboration feature is
  harder by design (the E2EE relay can never read content; the firm tier exists but was
  built for shared matters, not for being six people's all-day operating system). **XL**
- Reports, imports/migration from Wealthbox (with years of their history), admin/permissions,
  and the long tail a system of record carries (API for other tools — note Jump writes INTO
  Wealthbox; at this firm WE would have to receive what Jump sends or replace Jump too,
  which this path does). **L–XL**
- **Total: XL — a second product's worth of build, most of it far from our differentiators.**

## How the story changes
Completely. We stop selling "the private AI beside your CRM" and start selling **"cancel
your CRM."** The buyer conversation changes from "add a tool" (one advisor can champion it)
to "rip out the firm's operating system" (a firm-wide, compliance-gated, migration-scared,
everyone-must-switch decision). Pricing, onboarding, support, and sales cycle all become
system-of-record-shaped: longer, heavier, stickier.

## What would have to be true
- Her aspiration generalizes: many small RIAs actively want ONE system, enough to endure a
  CRM migration (the research cannot support this — it's one spouse-invested sentence,
  contradicted by her own "Wealthbox is integral" testimony, E-106–109).
- We can build and support a CRM core to system-of-record reliability while ALSO being the
  best AI layer — with a team that today measures itself on trust-breaker lockdown for a
  much smaller surface.
- The E2EE architecture can carry always-on six-person coordination without becoming the
  bottleneck (unproven at this usage intensity).
- We out-execute both Wealthbox (defending its base) and Jump (her own prediction says Jump
  is headed here with far more funding and distribution — meeting Jump head-on in the CRM
  arena is fighting their next move on their timeline).

## Pros (the honest case FOR it)
- **The end-state prize is the biggest.** System-of-record products have the deepest moats,
  the highest switching costs in OUR favor once installed, per-seat expansion, and pricing
  power. If the category consolidates (her Jump-becomes-CRM prediction), the consolidator
  wins big.
- **Some of their worst pains are only truly fixable by owning the layer.** Template
  propagation to open workflows, judgment-aware task lists, and unified search across notes/
  email/tasks are structurally awkward as bolt-ons and natural as a rebuilt core.
- **The consolidation appetite is real at some level.** Small firms hate tool sprawl; "one
  bill, one login, one place" is a perennial seller — and our local-first/E2EE privacy story
  would be a genuinely differentiated CRM posture nobody in the category has.
- **We'd stop depending on a potential competitor's API.** If Jump does become a CRM and
  Wealthbox tightens its ecosystem, the "intelligence layer on top" position gets squeezed
  from both sides. Path 4 is the hedge against being a feature of someone else's platform.
- **Fixing their named defects by design** (propagation, dumb tasks) would create marquee
  "only we do this" moments that pure AI features can't match.

## Cons (why the synthesis recommended against)
- **The evidence base is one hopeful sentence from the founder's wife.** The skeptic lane's
  full discount applies: aspiration, not evaluation; contradicted by her own dependence
  testimony; she is not the buyer; and the firm ignores Jump features that don't fit their
  habits today (E-030/031) — behavior that predicts CRM-migration refusal, not adoption.
- **It fights the switching costs the research proved.** The clearest adoption finding was
  that this firm wouldn't even change a NOTE TEMPLATE to gain automation (E-032). A CRM
  migration is that decision times a hundred.
- **It abandons the wedge for a war.** Paths 2–3 attack where incumbents are weak and we are
  strong (private AI, template fidelity, cited truth). Path 4 attacks where Wealthbox is
  entrenched, where Jump's roadmap (per her prediction) is headed with more money, and where
  our differentiators (local-first, E2EE, cited AI) matter least to the buying decision.
- **XL build on a team whose bottleneck is verification.** The July history is instructive:
  every new surface generated trust-breaker hunts; the whole current strategy ("lock down
  trust-breakers, get real users") exists because surface area outran hardening once already.
  A CRM core is the largest surface expansion possible.
- **It resurrects "all-in-one," which the market punishes.** The needs-discovery evidence
  (external, multi-source) explicitly found practitioners scorning all-in-ones ("went with
  Advyzon and now regret it") and the board's stance is simple-and-focused. Path 4 makes us
  the thing our own positioning attacks.
- **GTM regression.** We'd trade a champion-led, land-small motion for a rip-and-replace
  enterprise-style sale — with zero paying customers today. It maximizes time-to-first-
  revenue at the exact moment the board stance is first-users-first.
- **Migration is the graveyard.** Years of contacts, notes, tasks, open workflows, and
  integrations (Jump writes into Wealthbox; Calendly; email logging) must move flawlessly —
  a single lost note violates the product's core promise. The research's own "nail biter"
  standard (E-094) applies to us at migration scale.

## A hybrid worth naming (if the ambition appeals)
The credible version of Path 4 is **sequenced, not immediate**: run Path 2/3, and build the
few CRM-core primitives that ALSO serve the layer strategy (households/relationships, an
account registry, a real task model for triage) as typed local records. That keeps a
"quiet CRM option" alive — if pilots later show firms asking to cancel Wealthbox on their
own, the migration path exists. This is "earn the right to replace," versus declaring it now.

## Failure mode
A year of building a CRM core nobody migrates to; the AI edge stagnates while Jump ships
template fidelity and Wealthbox ships a better AI tab; the demo gets heavier; the first-
users goal recedes. The company becomes an underfunded CRM vendor with a great privacy
story.

## Cost / reversibility / signals
- **Cost:** XL. **Reversibility:** low once announced — "we're your system of record" is a
  promise you can't quietly retract, and migration customers can't be stranded.
- **Working (if attempted):** a pilot firm VOLUNTARILY asks to move off Wealthbox after
  living with Paths 2/3 features; migration fidelity proves lossless on a real book.
- **Kill signals:** pilot firms decline migration even when offered free; Jump announces CRM
  features (validating the war we'd be walking into with less money); the task/workflow core
  slips while trust bugs accumulate.

# My recommendation (advisory — the decision is yours)

**Path 2, with Path 3's two best items pulled forward as prototypes and Path 4's hybrid
seed planted quietly.** Concretely: ship the Source of Truth release (Path 2 in full);
prototype the triage coach and the scheduling due-list during the same cycle and let the
stranger interviews vote (so Path 3 remains one decision away, informed); and when building
B1–B3, model households, accounts, and tasks as proper typed records (the Path 4 hybrid
seed) so the replace-Wealthbox door stays open without betting the company on it now.

Why: the research's own quality tiers point there. Path 2 is the only path whose every build
is backed by behavior-grounded evidence; Path 3 becomes right the moment the interviews
replicate tier-2 pains (weeks away, not quarters); Path 4's prize is real but its evidence
today is one aspirational sentence standing against the strongest adoption finding we have —
a firm that wouldn't change a note template to gain automation. "Earn the right to replace"
converts Path 4 from a bet-the-company decision into a sequence of small, reversible ones.

**What would change my recommendation:** if 3+ of the next 10 stranger interviews
spontaneously ask for consolidation ("I wish this replaced my CRM") the way she did — or if
a pilot firm asks to move off Wealthbox unprompted — the hybrid seed graduates to a real
Path 4 evaluation. If the interviews fail to replicate the source-of-truth pain, drop back
toward Path 1 and rethink the wedge itself.
