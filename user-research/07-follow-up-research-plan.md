# 07 — Follow-up research plan

*What the 2026-07-10 session cannot answer, and how to answer it. This session was a
hypothesis generator of unusual quality — one information-rich, atypical, non-neutral
participant. Nothing in it is market validation. The participant herself offered the
recruiting path (XYPN Facebook group, her alumni network, ~$100 gift cards, a colleague who
works across 4–5 advisory offices) and volunteered to make introductions.*

## 1. Unanswered questions (ranked by how much they gate product decisions)

1. **Does "latest and greatest" cited inquiry reach wallet-out?** She called source-of-truth
   inquiry "hugely valuable for our firm" — but she is not the buyer, and no price was ever
   discussed. The June evaluation flagged this same unknown; it remains open.
2. **Is the two-audience meeting output (client-facing template + internal color) a general
   need or a JBW artifact?** The "inside scoop" pain had real frequency evidence ("this happens
   all the time") but the shape of the fix depends on firm culture.
3. **How common is template lock-in?** JBW refused to flex their note template even when Jump
   said flexibility would unlock automation. If most firms are similarly "married," then
   faithful-template rendering + template→tasks is a durable moat; if JBW is unusual, it's a
   custom-work trap.
4. **Do process-oriented staff (the "Seattle" persona) reject the open-ended Ask interface?**
   She predicted her own teammate wants the opposite interaction style. If true generally, the
   product needs a browsable/structured complement before firm-wide adoption works.
5. **Who actually decides at a 5–10 person RIA, and what does the advisor (not ops) evaluate
   on?** This session is an ops/client-service lens. Andy/Chris (advisors) and Jessica
   (manager) would weight things differently — she said so explicitly.
6. **Is scheduling toil general?** Her cascade (client list → who hasn't booked → right
   Calendly link → service tier → manual Outlook 40% of the time) is detailed and real — but
   firms using different booking stacks may not share it.
7. **Does the stale-map fear generalize into "won't adopt maintained artifacts at all"?** Her
   staleness-kills-trust warning is a design constraint if general.
8. **Frequency calibration for the flagship demo scenarios.** By her own account the
   umbrella-policy change is a ~once-per-several-years event per client. Is the *class* of
   question ("what's current on X, given documents + emails disagree") daily-frequency even if
   each instance is rare? Need diary-style evidence, not recall.
9. **Phone-call capture reality.** Half of advisor calls at JBW aren't captured; inbound is
   awkward everywhere. How do other firms handle it, and does anyone pay for a fix?
10. **Does privacy/local-first matter to the WANT, or only to the PERMISSION?** The 2026-07-08
    session says it's the gate; this session (compliance bracketed) shows zero capability pull
    for it. Both can be true — test where it belongs in the pitch order with strangers.

## 4. Questions for other advisors (neutral phrasings — no product pitch until the end)

Workflow/current behavior (Mom-Test style):
1. "Walk me through the last client meeting you had — from the week before it to the week
   after." (Listen for: prep sources, notes flow, who touches what.)
2. "The last time you needed to know whether a client's [insurance/benefits/account] info was
   current — what did you actually do?" (Latest-and-greatest, unprompted.)
3. "Tell me about the last time information didn't make it to the person who needed it."
   (Handoffs, dropped balls, emotional stakes.)
4. "What happens to everything said in a meeting that doesn't end up in your notes?"
5. "How do meeting notes differ between what the client sees and what you keep internally?"
   (Two-audience — do NOT suggest "inside scoop.")
6. "Show me (or describe) your task list right now. How do you decide what to do today?"
7. "How does a client's annual meeting get scheduled at your firm, start to finish?"
8. "When did you last send something sensitive to a third party (accountant, attorney)? How
   did you make sure it went to the right person?"
9. "What's the most tedious recurring thing in your role that you'd pay to never do again?"
10. "If your CRM disappeared tomorrow, what would you actually miss?"

AI/trust attitudes (after workflow, before pitch):
11. "Where does AI currently touch your practice, officially or unofficially?"
12. "If an AI drafted something client-facing, what would need to be true before you sent it?"
13. "Would you rather approve every AI action, or review a log afterward?" (Test her
    approval-over-log preference for generality.)

Only after all the above: 60-second Lantern description, then "what would you use first,"
"what would stop your firm," "what would you expect it to cost."

## 5. Usability-test tasks (once prototypes exist)

1. **Latest-and-greatest inquiry:** seed a workspace where a policy document and a later email
   disagree; task: "find the current coverage amount." Success = uses Ask, trusts the dated
   timeline answer, clicks a citation to verify. Measure: time-to-confidence, whether dates
   were noticed, whether they double-check manually anyway.
2. **Meeting review flow:** play a mock meeting recording; task: produce the notes you'd send
   the client and whatever you'd keep internally, then get them to the CRM. Success = the
   include/exclude review feels faster than their current flow; internal items never land in
   the client artifact; approval gate is understood without explanation.
3. **Template fidelity:** give them THEIR firm's note template; measure whether the output is
   accepted as "ours" without edits (the Jump-failure test).
4. **Task routing:** from reviewed notes, assign one item to a colleague with context. Watch
   for whether "assignee + destination" matches their mental model.
5. **Staleness perception probe:** show a Client Map where one fact is outdated but dated, vs
   one undated. Measure trust reaction — does visible dating rescue trust or not?

## 7. Revised interview guide note

The 2026-07-10 session's biggest methodological lessons, folded into the guide above:
- **Bracketing compliance changed everything we heard.** Run future sessions both ways
  (half with compliance bracketed, half not) to locate privacy's true place in the pitch.
- **Don't offer artifact shapes ("a map," "a report," "an approval list").** Ask what they'd
  do/need; let them shape the artifact. Most leading moments in this session were shape-offers.
- **Don't pitch before minute 45.** The richest material came from "what do you hate doing"
  and "walk me through it" — not from feature reactions.
- **Frequency-check every pain on the spot** ("when did that last happen? how often?") — the
  session left several vivid pains (wrong-Roth-IRA, umbrella update) without frequency data.
