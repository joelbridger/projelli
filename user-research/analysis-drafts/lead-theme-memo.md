# Lead analyst memo — candidate themes from full familiarization read (pre-lane reconciliation)

*Written by the lead (Fable) after personally reading both transcripts end to end, BEFORE
reading any subagent lane output. Purpose: independent theme candidates to test against the
lanes, so lane conclusions don't anchor the lead. These are hypotheses, not findings.*

## Candidate themes (to test against the evidence ledger)

T1. **"Latest and greatest" / source-of-truth reconciliation is the center of gravity.**
The umbrella-policy scenario (file says $1M, later email says $2M — she wants a short dated
timeline with citations), Philip's recurring "what is the source of truth" question, the
"scavenger hunt," "spill the tea," the HSA/benefits example, and her closing statement that
source-of-truth inquiries would be "hugely valuable for our firm." This is HER framing,
recurring unprompted across both parts. It is the strongest product signal in the session
and matches Ask/Client Map but demands cross-source (file+email+CRM+meeting) RECONCILIATION
WITH DATES, not just retrieval.

T2. **Information reaches its destinations through too many human hands, and everyone lives
in fear of being the hand that drops it.** The notes→template→her→Philip→RightCapital chain
("a lot of hands"), Teams color never landing in the CRM, SOS messages ("where is so-and-so's
kid going to college"), "horrible fear that we are going to be the one that drops the ball,"
1099-to-wrong-accountant "nail biter," wrong-Roth-IRA story. Pain is BOTH efficiency AND
emotional load. Note: the fix she wants is not autonomy — it's fewer, safer, verified handoffs.

T3. **Advisor verification is a non-negotiable law of the workflow (and the design center for
any write).** Only the human who was in the meeting can green-light content derived from it;
review must be cheap (checkbox include/exclude, approval lists) because reviewer bandwidth is
the scarcest resource; approval strongly preferred over audit-log-after-the-fact. AI content
that is long, wrong, or mis-formatted = "battery drain" that made a senior advisor abandon
Jump output entirely and rewrite from memory. Trust collapses on staleness too (the map that's
out of date "would decrease trust a lot").

T4. **The meeting is the heart; the template is sacred; the loss is the human story.** Their
3-part Jump template is deliberately minimal and clients are trained to it; the firm explicitly
REFUSES to flex the template even when Jump says flexibility would unlock automation
("married to the notes"). What the template loses is the color/"inside scoop" (couple
disagreements, kid's college, end-of-meeting reversals — "the last 10 minutes" is where value
lives). They want that color captured SEPARATELY (internal-only, searchable, in the CRM for
meeting prep) — never sent to clients (liability fear of full transcripts). Two-audience
output (client-facing vs internal) is a hard requirement Jump doesn't meet.

T5. **Wealthbox is the firm's operating system — and its dumbness is the daily grind.**
Everything routes to Wealthbox (notes, tasks, workflows, pinned notes as memory), but: search
is keyword-brittle (401k vs "retirement account at JP Morgan"), email isn't searchable, six
people write in six styles, tasks are dumb sticky notes (overdue reds, spelling task = fraud
task visually), template changes don't propagate to 40 open workflows, advisors don't know
process/template names and misfile requests. Her stated hope: Lantern REPLACES Wealthbox+Jump
eventually (weigh: spouse-invested prediction + she's not the buyer; but the pains are real
and specific). Near-term implication is coexist-and-write-back; long-term implication is the
task/process layer is genuinely weak and might be attackable.

T6. **Proactivity is wanted only where staleness can't hurt: nudges, triage, reports — not
facts.** AI coach over the task list (accepts even the "shaming" workload honesty — "honestly
it would be helpful"), who's-the-hot-client resource reports, engagement-gap report (no
interaction in N months), scheduling nudges (who hasn't booked; whose Calendly link; service
tiers). But structured always-on maps of facts (cash needs) worry her because keeping them
current is exactly the job that already crushes them ("so many places to keep updated" — a new
map = one more place). Design implication: generated-on-demand views from live sources beat
maintained artifacts.

T7. **Client-experience credibility is a driving motive, not just efficiency.** "Never ask a
client the same question twice" landed hardest of all intake features (doctor's-office story;
cringe at CC'd emails asking known things); gifts/kid's-college details; calling clients out of
the blue with remembered context; "clients are trained" expectations; time-back fantasies were
all client-facing (attend meetings, watch recordings, proactive calls). Positioning insight:
she frames value as being a credible, caring firm — "we look like we know you" — not "AI
efficiency."

T8. **Whole-practice memory beats client files: process knowledge and decision history are
part of the need.** "Why did we decide X"; an AI help agent that knows their Wealthbox
conventions ("how do I request a money movement"); compliance-change "tentacles" (audit
everything against a rule change); role-reassignment sweeps. This extends the product's
client-centric model toward a FIRM-level knowledge space.
