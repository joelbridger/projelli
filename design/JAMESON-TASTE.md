# Jameson’s Design Taste

This is a living record of what Jameson responds to in Lantern. It is not a replacement for the charter or design system. It helps when more than one option would otherwise fit.

## Growth rule

Every future blessing, rejection, or meaningful visual correction from Jameson must add one dated entry here and a matching record in `decisions/`. Capture the evidence, not a guess: what he saw or reacted to, what he said or chose, and the design lesson it reveals.

## Recorded reactions and directives

### 2026-06-02 — Light by default; calm over drama

**What he reacted to:** Jameson’s standing UI direction is a polished light theme, not a dark interface. He also prefers low ceremony and direct progress.

**What it reveals:** Lantern should feel like a well-kept desk in daylight: light background, dark readable text, quiet accents, and no unnecessary steps.

**Source:** Workspace `AGENTS.md`, preference history, 2026-06-02.

### 2026-07-05 — Copy must be understandable at a glance

**What he reacted to:** Jameson tightened the communication standard to “explain like Jameson is 10.”

**What it reveals:** UI copy should use everyday words, short sentences, concrete labels, and a clear next action. A user should not need product or technical knowledge to understand a screen.

**Source:** Workspace `AGENTS.md`, preference history, 2026-07-05.

### 2026-07-14 — Keep the full plan visible

**What he reacted to:** Jameson asked for every item to be covered on a master tracking list.

**What it reveals:** He values a complete, visible picture over hidden or forgotten work. In design, this means clear information structure and honest tracking of design debts, not scattered exceptions.

**Source:** `MASTER-TRACKER.md`, opening note, 2026-07-14 (R46).

### 2026-07-15 — The v2 prototype is approved as the visual and interaction reference

**What he reacted to:** “I have thoroughly reviewed the latest prototype and I approve everything to be built.” He left one small carry-forward note: the Repeats checkbox alignment in the task modal.

**What it reveals:** The approved prototype is the baseline, not loose inspiration. Preserve its visual and interaction choices unless a dated decision says otherwise. Small visual details still matter and should be corrected in their proper surface rather than ignored.

**Source:** `MASTER-TRACKER.md`, Design track / A4-A5 design freeze, 2026-07-15.

### 2026-07-15 — A client record should read as one whole

**What he reacted to:** The parity review found that four record sections had become separate top-level cards even though the approved prototype nests them in one “Client profile” card. The difference was deliberately staged for his review as a real design delta.

**What it reveals:** Fragmenting one client story into a collection of competing cards can lose the intended sense of one coherent record. Respect the approved grouping unless a deliberate new decision improves the journey.

**Source:** `MASTER-TRACKER.md`, vision-parity debt entry, 2026-07-15.

### 2026-07-16 — R2: check visual fidelity as the product grows

**What he reacted to:** The second consultant assessment, requested by Jameson, identified that design matching had fallen behind the number of completed features. The adopted response was a second parity sweep and an ongoing rhythm of a sweep after roughly every 15 landed changes.

**What it reveals:** Faithfulness to the approved direction is not a one-time sign-off. Recheck it as the product grows, especially where separate feature work could make the overall experience drift.

**Source:** `CONSULTANT-FABLE-NOTES.md`, R2 and its adopted disposition, 2026-07-17 (assessment dated 2026-07-16).

### 2026-07-16 — R4: real use settles design questions

**What he reacted to:** The same assessment called for a guided Jameson drive of the product because a growing set of design deltas needed real use to settle them. The response records that this drive belongs with Jameson.

**What it reveals:** Screenshots and review are necessary, but real journeys in the app are the final check for whether a choice feels natural. Stage meaningful deltas clearly for that kind of decision rather than quietly normalizing them.

**Source:** `CONSULTANT-FABLE-NOTES.md`, R4 and disposition, 2026-07-17 (assessment dated 2026-07-16).

### 2026-07-16 — D-series: design must be a real product discipline

**What he reacted to:** Jameson directed that UI and design become first-class: every UI feature needs a design source, a separate design review, and tracked design coverage. New screens need a design spec before review; high-stakes new directions need his blessing.

**What it reveals:** “It works” is not enough. The intended experience must be named before building, judged after building, and kept visible as evidence.

**Source:** `CONSULTANT-FABLE-NOTES.md`, “JAMESON DIRECTIVE: UI/DESIGN FIRST-CLASS (D-series),” 2026-07-17; recorded as received 2026-07-16.

### 2026-07-17 — D2: a screen must work by itself and with its neighbors

**What he reacted to:** Jameson required two levels of review: craft and coherence. He explicitly warned that something can be “beautifully crafted but wrong for the whole.”

**What it reveals:** Repeated patterns, navigation, voice, and journey position matter as much as surface-level polish. Do not approve a lone beautiful screen that introduces a new dialect into Lantern.

**Source:** `CONSULTANT-FABLE-NOTES.md`, “D2 AMENDMENT,” 2026-07-17.

### 2026-07-17 — D5-D8: preserve the product’s design memory

**What he reacted to:** Jameson directed the Design Office: one living design source of truth, a charter, a taste record that grows from every blessing, a standing design owner, fresh-eyes testing, and later visual regression.

**What it reveals:** Good taste must survive individual sessions. Lantern’s design knowledge belongs in a maintained shared home, not in any one person’s memory.

**Source:** `CONSULTANT-FABLE-NOTES.md`, “D-SERIES EXTENSION: THE DESIGN OFFICE,” 2026-07-17.

## How to use this record

When choosing between valid options, prefer the one that keeps the interface light, calm, plain-spoken, coherent, and faithful to the approved prototype. If a proposed change conflicts with a stronger or newer decision, record the conflict in `decisions/` and ask Jameson to choose.
