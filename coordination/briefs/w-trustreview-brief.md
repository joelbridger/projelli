## The lens — what you are and are NOT hunting
The calibration example: bot-less recording was fully functional, private, well-built — and still broken at the human level, because *the other people in the meeting had no way to know*. A CFP spotted it in one sentence; four QA personas and 41 filed findings never did. **Your entire job is finding the rest of that class.**

You are NOT hunting: crashes, broken buttons, pixel bugs, data loss (41 findings already filed in `coordination/qa-campaign/BUG-DB.md` — read it so you don't refile). You ARE hunting, on every surface:
1. **Trust asymmetries** — where the app knows/does something the human (advisor, client, compliance officer, colleague) can't SEE or PROVE. Recording-without-notice was one. Where else does the app ask to be taken on faith? (What does "indexed locally" look like to someone who doesn't believe it? Can an advisor *show* a client their data never left? When AI writes something, can the advisor tell what it read to write it — and could they defend it to a compliance officer?)
2. **Moment-of-use reality** — WHEN does each feature fire in an advisor's actual day, and what competes for their attention at that second? (The consent dialog appears at the most loaded moment of the day — right before a client meeting. What else lands at the wrong moment, demands reading when there's no time, or asks for a decision the advisor can't make yet?)
3. **The other people in the room** — every feature that touches a second human (clients in meetings, colleagues in a firm, the recipient of an AI-drafted email, the CRM other staff read). What do THEY experience, and does it embarrass, surprise, or misrepresent the advisor? (An AI-drafted follow-up that reads like AI wrote it. A CRM note a colleague reads without knowing a machine wrote it. What does the client receive/see/sense?)
4. **The compliance officer veto** — walk each capability asking "what makes the firm's compliance officer say NO to the whole product?" (Books-and-records: are AI outputs/meeting notes retained/exportable the way Reg S-P/FINRA-adjacent expectations want? Can the firm demonstrate supervision? Where would an examiner frown?)
5. **Week-one adoption cliffs** — the existing-workflow collisions: an advisor already HAS a CRM discipline, an email style, a meeting routine, a filing habit. Where does the app demand they change a working habit rather than slot into it? Where would they silently stop using a feature after trying it twice? What does the app look like on day 3 when the demo glow is gone — is the daily loop actually shorter than their old way?
