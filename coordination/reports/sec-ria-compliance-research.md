**B. What Consultants Evaluate**
A Synergy/XYPN-style reviewer will mostly ask: “What client data touches this tool, who can see it, how is it protected, what happens if something goes wrong, and how will the CCO prove supervision later?”
They will evaluate: data flow, encryption at rest and in transit, access controls, local storage, key handling, API-key storage, telemetry, subprocessors, AI-provider routing, data residency, retention/deletion, incident response, breach notice timing, business continuity, vendor financial/operational stability, support access, software updates, vulnerability handling, insurance, SOC 2 Type II or equivalent evidence, and the contract/DPA. **C. Why Local-First/BYOK Helps**
The advisor’s concern, “we’re not allowed to put client files into ChatGPT,” is really a data-sharing and supervision concern, not a blanket SEC ban on AI. If a hosted AI service receives, maintains, processes, or is permitted access to customer information, it can become a service provider under Reg S-P, which triggers due diligence, monitoring, incident response, privacy-notice consistency, and breach-notice planning. ([ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-248/subpart-A)) ([ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-248/subpart-A))
A local-first design changes the risk picture because client files can stay on the adviser’s device, not on the software vendor’s server. That reduces vendor access to customer information. BYOK also means the advisory firm controls the AI-provider account and key. But it does not erase the issue: if prompts, client files, or excerpts are sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or another model provider, that provider still needs approval under the firm’s vendor/AI policy. ([ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-248/subpart-A))
Truthful claims we can likely make, if technically true: “Advisor Prep Hero does not store client files on our servers,” “client documents are processed locally by default,” “the firm controls which AI provider is used,” “AI calls are user-initiated,” and “local-only mode can prevent client content from leaving the device.”  
Claims to avoid: “SEC approved,” “compliant by default,” “safe to upload all client files into any AI,” “no vendor review needed,” or “no data ever leaves” unless every mode, telemetry path, crash report, update check, and AI request really proves that. AI-washing enforcement makes substantiation mandatory. ([sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-36))
