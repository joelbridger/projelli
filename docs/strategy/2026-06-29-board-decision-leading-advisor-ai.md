# BOARD DECISION — Advisor Prep Hero competes to be the leading financial-advisor AI (2026-06-29)

**Decided by:** Jameson (Board of Directors), 2026-06-29. **Status: OFFICIAL STANCE going forward.**
This supersedes the "reposition away from advisors / retreat to a narrow wedge" recommendation in the
2026-06-28 competitive and strategy analyses (see "What this supersedes" below). It is the controlling
direction; downstream product, positioning, and roadmap decisions align to it.

## The decision (in the board's words)

**Advisor Prep Hero continues forward to become the leading financial-advisor AI in the space — head-on, not by
retreating to a niche.** We do not have clients yet, and that's understood. The bet is that we can execute
**more simply and more powerfully** by being, from the start, **an AI product — an app — not a note-taking
app.** The shape of the product is the simple, powerful one: *connect your files → ask cited questions →
get a living Client Map.* Clean, clear, AI-first.

## The rationale (the board's firsthand read of the competition)

Jameson signed up for **Jump** (the incumbent, ~$105M-funded, the most-adopted standalone advisor-AI tool)
on a personal account and went through the product and their AI-integration videos directly. The board's
firsthand assessment:

- Jump's AI is **messy, scattered, and hard to access/understand inside the actual product.** It is **not**
  a simple "connect your files and ask questions" experience — it's "connect 60 things, then send forms and
  take notes."
- **Jump is still a note-taking app at its core.** Its AI direction is **fuzzy at best.**
- This is the opening: Advisor Prep Hero wins by being **simpler, clearer, and genuinely AI-first** where Jump is
  bloated, integration-sprawled, and note-taker-anchored.

**Note — this firsthand read aligns with, not contradicts, the competitive report's findings about Jump's
weaknesses:** that report independently concluded Jump's "synthesis" is meeting/CRM-derived, its document
capability is shallow (intake/forms, not deep folder reasoning), and it leads with notetaking. Where the
board diverges is the *recommendation*: the report said "avoid Jump head-on, retreat to a privacy/document
niche." The board's call is the opposite — **attack those weaknesses head-on** with a cleaner, AI-first
product, and go for the category lead.

## What this means going forward (execution implications)

1. **Advisors remain the lead ICP — head-on, not a retreat.** We are trying to win the financial-advisor AI
   category, not carve a defensive niche out of it. (Law/tax stay as adjacent secondary segments behind a
   flag; they are not the play.)
2. **Position as a simple, powerful AI APP — never a note-taker.** The product story is *connect files →
   ask cited questions → Client Map.* Do NOT lead with, or drift toward, meeting-notetaking as the core.
   Notetaking, if ever present, is a feature, never the identity.
3. **Win on simplicity + AI-first clarity, not integration breadth.** Jump's failure mode is "connect 60
   things." Advisor Prep Hero's edge is the opposite: it just works on your files and answers, cleanly. Resist
   feature/integration sprawl that would make us the thing we're beating. (Connectors are sales-enablers in
   service of the simple story, not the story.)
4. **The real assets still matter and reinforce this:** local-first / private-by-design + BYOK, reasoning
   over the actual document + email pile (not just meetings), Word-native AI redline, and the cited Client
   Map. These make the "simple, powerful, AI-first, private" promise credible.
5. **The competitive + strategy analyses are retained as INPUTS, not orders.** The Jump analysis
   (`competitive-analysis/jump-vs-keepance/`) and the 2026-06-28 advisor memo
   (`docs/strategy/2026-06-28-strategic-advisor-memo.md`) remain valuable for *understanding the competitor
   and the market* — but their "reposition away / narrow off advisors" recommendation is **not** the
   direction. Use them to learn where Jump is weak; ignore their retreat counsel.

## What this supersedes
- The Jump competitive report's headline recommendation ("avoid direct financial-advisor competition;
  reposition to a narrower wedge Jump doesn't own"). — Superseded on the *direction*; kept as competitor intel.
- The 2026-06-28 strategic-advisor memo's "narrow hard, treat advisors as one slice, keep attorneys parallel
  as a hedge." — The "get real users / don't fall into the build trap / sell to strangers" discipline still
  stands; the "retreat from leading-advisor-AI ambition" framing does not.

## Still true (not changed by this decision)
- We have **no paying clients yet** — the goal is to *become* the leader, from zero. The "go get real
  first users / validate with real advisors / don't only build" discipline from the memo remains in force;
  this decision sets the *direction* of that effort (lead the category as a simple AI app), not a license to
  build endlessly without users.
