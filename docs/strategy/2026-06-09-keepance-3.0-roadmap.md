# Advisor Prep Hero 3.0 Implementation Roadmap

## Context

Advisor Prep Hero v2.5.1 is a local-first, Markdown-native desktop AI workspace sold as a solo tool. Four-source UX research (synthetic interview + usability test + two deep-research reports) and the resulting vision doc concluded that the most viable Advisor Prep Hero is something sharper and bigger: **the private intelligence layer for a law practice.** It wins on confidential, cited recall across a lawyer's whole history, deepens into litigation document work, produces real Office deliverables, and grows from a solo on-ramp into a firm platform, with a confidentiality architecture the cloud incumbents structurally cannot copy.

This roadmap operationalizes that vision as Advisor Prep Hero 3.0. North star: `~/keepance/docs/research/2026-06-08-ux-attorney-v2.5.1/vision-most-viable-keepance.md` (and the report alongside it). Three board-level decisions were made this session and shape everything below.

**Intended outcome:** one comprehensive 3.0 release that is Office-native, confidentially cited, litigation-deep, and firm-ready, validated by real firms before launch.

### Decisions locked (2026-06-09)

| Decision | Choice | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Firm tier | **Commit to the firm platform** | Advisor Prep Hero becomes a company that operates cloud infra (shared matters, multi-seat, assured/zero-retention tier) and pursues SOC 2 / DPA. |
| Release strategy | **One big 3.0, everything together** | No wedge-first market trickle. Mitigate big-bang risk with internal alphas behind flags + a design-partner firm beta program throughout the build. |
| Word fidelity | **Build fully in-house** | Own the OOXML/track-changes engine end to end. No vendor in the confidential document path. This is the hardest core and is de-risked first. |

### Non-negotiables (from the research, true for every workstream)

1. Office is the atom; the lawyer never sees Markdown.
2. Every AI answer over client data carries a one-click, verifiable citation. No uncited answers over client data, ever.
3. The user can always explain, in one sentence, where their data is and who can see it.
4. Only honest claims: the provider-exposure asterisk is told proactively, not hidden.
5. Matter isolation and privilege/work-product handling are first-class, enforced, and audited.
6. Reuse before rebuild. The foundation is ~60-70% of the way there; do not throw away what works.

> No time estimates anywhere in this plan (per standing preference). Sequencing is by dependency; size is relative (S / M / L / XL); risk is Low / Med / High.

---

## Program shape

One big 3.0, built in dependency-ordered phases, launched together. The two things that make this safe rather than reckless:

- **De-risk the make-or-break bets first (P0 gates).** Three R&D spikes must succeed before the full build is committed: in-house track-changes round-trip, matter-scoped verified-citation retrieval, and the collaboration/identity architecture. If a gate fails, we redesign before spending the big build behind it.
- **Design-Partner Firm program runs the whole way through.** Recruit 5 to 10 firms (use the research screener at `~/keepance/docs/research/.../instruments/screener.md`) into a private beta. They validate each phase on real matters, which simultaneously: (a) is the real-user research Pass B the findings demanded, (b) produces the named-attorney proof and CLE/testimonials the research said gate adoption, and (c) removes the big-bang launch risk by the time 3.0 goes GA.

Branch strategy: a long-lived 3.0 development line off `main`, every workstream behind feature flags, merged continuously, backup-tagged before each risky migration. (All git handled autonomously.)

This roadmap should be committed into the repo (`~/keepance/docs/strategy/2026-06-09-keepance-3.0-roadmap.md`) when execution begins; it is the working north star and will be updated as gates resolve.

## Workstreams

Eight workstreams. Each lists what we reuse (with paths), what we build or replace, the hardest risk, and the key files. Dependencies: A and B and C are the wedge core; D sits on A+B; E wraps them; F is the firm layer; G and H are assurance and fit.

### WS-A: Office-native document engine (in-house) — XL, High risk
**Goal:** Office (Word first, then PDF, Excel, PowerPoint) is the canonical, faithful, round-tripping format with track changes, comments, styles, numbering, tables, and letterhead. The AI operates on the real document structure. Markdown becomes an invisible internal detail at most.

- **Reuse:** `src/modules/workspace/` (WorkspaceService, FSBackend already does binary read/write, PathValidator); `src/utils/spreadsheet-io.ts` (SheetJS xlsx is already high-fidelity); `docx-preview` for read-only render; LibreOffice conversion in `src-tauri/src/commands/fs.rs` (for PDF export); CodeMirror `MarkdownEditor.tsx` retained only for any optional internal text surfaces.
- **Build / replace:** an in-house OOXML engine in Rust (`src-tauri/src/commands/docx/*`, new) that parses and serializes DOCX while preserving revisions (`<w:ins>`/`<w:del>`), comments, styles, numbering, tables, and section properties; a structured document model (JSON) exchanged with the frontend; a new rich document editor that renders revisions inline with author and timestamp and emits revisions on AI edits (replace `src/components/media/DocxEditor.tsx` and the Mammoth path in `src/utils/docx-io.ts`); version history refactored to binary snapshots in a `.versions/` folder with structural diff (`src/modules/versioning/VersionService.ts`, `src/components/editor/DiffViewer.tsx`); the canonical-format flip (new docs default to `.docx`; AI chat artifacts and workflow outputs become Office files; `.aichat` references linked Office files in `src/types/ai.ts`); `editorStore.ts` content becomes binary-capable; autosave in `App.tsx` writes binary.
- **Hardest risk:** faithful track-changes + comments round-trip, in-house. This is the program's single biggest technical bet and is **P0 Spike 1**.

### WS-B: Confidential cited recall (the wedge) — L, Med risk
**Goal:** ask in plain English, get the exact source (email, document, or matter) back with a one-click verifiable citation, privately, across everything ingested.

- **Reuse:** the RAG stack `src-tauri/src/commands/rag/*` (LanceDB + fastembed, the frozen `Hit` wire format); MiniSearch `src/modules/search/ContentIndex.ts`; `src/modules/memory/*` (MemoryService, `@workspace`); `fileContextStore` / `useOpenFileAIContext`; the email index callback in `src-tauri/src/commands/mail/sync.rs`.
- **Build:** index email + Office documents + PDFs (with OCR for scanned filings, new local OCR sidecar) into the vector store with rich metadata (source id, paragraph/page, matter id); extend `Hit` with `source_email_id` / `page` / `matter_id`; a citation-verification layer (confirm the file/paragraph exists and the text matches before render; block uncited answers over client data); a unified verifiable citation format (refactor `CitationParser` and `src/modules/research/SourceCardService.ts`) with click-to-open-source; inject email into chat context (currently synced but not injected).
- **Hardest risk:** citations that reliably resolve back to the exact source across formats; OCR quality.

### WS-C: The trust layer — L, High risk
**Goal:** the user can always explain where data goes; matters are isolated; privilege is first-class; provider exposure is honest.

- **Reuse:** Provider interface `src/modules/models/Provider.ts` + the Ollama local path `OllamaProvider.ts`; append-only `src/modules/audit/AuditService.ts`; `KeychainService` + `src-tauri/src/commands/keychain.rs`; email encryption `src-tauri/src/commands/mail/crypto.rs` + SQLCipher `store.rs`.
- **Build:** (1) **egress indicator** persistent on every AI action showing destination (Local/Ollama, Direct-to-Anthropic/OpenAI/Google, or Assured), in `StatusBar.tsx` / `MainPanel.tsx`; (2) **data map**, a plain-English, client-shareable "where is your data / who can see it" screen (onboarding + settings); (3) **confidentiality spectrum** as a Settings control (Local-only / BYOK-direct / Assured) that constrains the model list to the mode and surfaces honest provider-retention + training-opt-out guidance; (4) **matter model**: a first-class Client/Matter concept with matter-scoped retrieval enforced at index time and query time (so A never leaks into B) plus an active-matter badge; (5) **privilege / work-product**: tag files, blocks, and emails, exclude from retrieval unless pinned, scrub metadata on export; (6) **provenance audit**: move audit to SQLCipher and add events (`retrieval_executed`, `citation_verified`, `privilege_evaluated`, `scope_enforced`, `egress_shown`); (7) **optional encrypted vault** for the workspace, resolving the plaintext-at-rest finding.
- **Hardest risk:** matter isolation enforced at index + query + audit (not just UI filtering), and a data map that is exactly accurate (any inaccuracy kills credibility with this audience).
- **Key files:** `StatusBar.tsx`, `MainPanel.tsx`, `src/settings/schema.ts` + new Confidentiality and Clients/Matters panes, `AuditService.ts`, the rag scope params in `src-tauri/src/commands/rag/*`, mail privilege fields in `mail/model.rs`.

### WS-D: The associate + real deliverables — L, Med risk
**Goal:** deposition/discovery contradiction-finding, timelines, privilege logs, discovery triage, and drafting, all cited and verifiable, output as real Office deliverables.

- **Reuse:** `src/modules/workflow/*` (WorkflowEngine, RunRecordService, InterviewForm); `templates/legal|tax|consulting/*`; Office export from WS-A.
- **Build:** workflows operate on Office docs + cited RAG (WS-A/B); make the contradiction-finder, timeline builder, and privilege-log drafter genuinely strong and cited ("tireless first-year associate, not an oracle"); a privileged-mode gate on workflow execution; matter tagging on outputs; remove the `@draft` flag on legal templates only after design-partner attorney review.
- **Hardest risk:** output quality high enough to trust; validated by design-partner attorneys.

### WS-E: Onboarding and honesty — M, Med risk
**Goal:** a non-technical lawyer reaches a real cited answer without hitting the API-key wall, and understands the data story.

- **Reuse:** `src/components/onboarding/*` (FirstRunWizard, ApiKeyExplainer, ApiKeyTester, ProviderTutorialSteps, FeatureTour), `src/onboarding/samples/*`.
- **Build:** rebuild the API-key step with plain-English BYOK + cost + the Ollama option + the data-map diagram; a genuine "configure later" path; a guided "get your key" wizard; profession-to-model defaults; a first run that lands directly on the wedge (connect email, ask a question, see a cited answer). Acceptance test = the research usability protocol's Task 1 and Task 4.

### WS-F: Firm platform and cloud — XL, High risk
**Goal:** from single-user local to firm platform: identity/auth, per-org licensing and seats, ACL and ethical walls, shared matters, collaboration/sync, and the assured/zero-retention backend.

- **Reuse:** Tauri commands are user-agnostic pure functions (thread identity through them); keychain (move to a per-user namespace); the file watcher (add user identity); the license-validation seam at `licenses.keepance.com`.
- **Build:** user accounts + auth; a license-server rewrite (org, seats, machine binding, revoke) as a new backend service; per-file ACL + ethical walls + file-tree filtering; the collaboration/sync layer (CRDT or server-authoritative, decided in P0 Spike 3); the assured/zero-retention inference backend (a proxy that decrypts per session, forwards to the provider, and never logs request bodies); per-user keychain namespace in `src-tauri/src/commands/keychain.rs`; a new `src-tauri/src/commands/sync.rs`.
- **Hardest risk:** conflict-free collaborative sync, and operating a zero-retention backend without weakening the confidentiality moat. Both architecture-decided in **P0 Spike 3**.

### WS-G: Assurance and governance — M, Med risk
**Goal:** clear firm diligence, tell one consistent public truth, and make extensibility safe.

- **Build:** SOC 2 (the audit trail is evidence; needs a third-party auditor) and a DPA template + legal review and an SLA; reconcile documentation/pricing/version into a single source of truth across `website/`, FAQ, EULA, `CHANGELOG.md`, the repo, and LemonSqueezy, with one `VERSION` constant; MCP/plugin "Privileged Matter Mode" guardrails (disable network extensions, network-scope allowlists, plugin signing) in `src/modules/plugins/*` (PluginAPIBridge, PluginPermissions) and `src-tauri/src/commands/mcp.rs`; honest telemetry documentation.
- **Reuse:** `AuditService` as assurance evidence; `KEEPANCE_BUSINESS_PLAN.md` as the pricing source of truth (reconcile the rest to it).

### WS-H: Fits your stack (integrations) — L, Med risk
**Goal:** sit beside Clio, Outlook, Word, and document-management systems instead of replacing them.

- **Reuse:** the email connectors (`src-tauri/src/commands/mail/*`, Outlook already live); Office round-trip from WS-A.
- **Build:** Clio matter sync; DMS handoff (NetDocuments/iManage export or check-in); a secure review-package export; Word/Outlook add-in surface (later in the phase); these make the "no finishing elsewhere" promise real.

## Phasing (dependency-ordered, converging on one 3.0)

| Phase | Focus | Workstreams | Exit gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | De-risk the make-or-break bets + stand up the design-partner program | A, B/C, F spikes | All three spikes proven; 5-10 firms recruited |
| **P1** | Office-native core + canonical-format flip + onboarding shell | A, E | Import a tracked-change Word doc, edit, export, opens clean in Word |
| **P2** | Confidential cited recall + trust-layer core | B, C | Cited recall over email+docs+matters; matter isolation enforced; egress indicator + data map live |
| **P3** | The associate + real deliverables | D | Design-partner attorneys validate contradiction-finder + deliverables; legal templates un-drafted |
| **P4** | Firm platform | F | Two firms running shared matters with ACLs + the assured backend in private beta |
| **P5** | Assurance, governance, guardrails, integrations | G, H | SOC 2 in progress, DPA ready, docs reconciled, privileged-mode shipped, Clio/DMS handoff working |
| **P6** | Harden + design-partner GA | all | One big 3.0 launch with named proof (testimonials, CLE) |

**P0 spikes (each a hard gate):**
- **Spike 1 (WS-A):** in-house DOCX round-trip preserving and producing track changes + comments. Proof: a Word doc with existing tracked changes imports, the engine adds a new tracked edit programmatically, exports, and Word shows all original + new revisions as acceptable/rejectable.
- **Spike 2 (WS-B/C):** matter-scoped, verified-citation retrieval over a mixed email + document corpus. Proof: a natural-language question returns the exact source with a citation that opens it; a Matter-A query never returns Matter-B content.
- **Spike 3 (WS-F):** collaboration/identity/sync architecture decision (CRDT vs server-authoritative) and the assured zero-retention backend design. Proof: a written architecture decision + a thin prototype of two clients editing one shared matter.

## Cross-cutting

- **Validation and proof:** the design-partner firm program is woven through P0-P6 and is the acceptance authority for the wedge (run the research usability protocol as the formal acceptance test). It is also where the testimonials, CLE, and bar credibility come from.
- **Risk register (top five):** in-house track-changes fidelity (WS-A); collaborative sync (WS-F); operating a zero-retention backend without weakening the moat (WS-C/F); matter-isolation correctness (WS-C); big-bang launch risk (mitigated by the design-partner betas and the internal usability of the wedge before firm layers land).
- **Governance:** the vision doc is the north star; pricing/positioning/brand changes are board-ratified; documentation reconciled to a single source of truth early (WS-G) so the public story stops contradicting itself.

## Verification (how we know each piece works end to end)

Run the app with `npm run tauri dev` (desktop) and exercise real flows; `npm run test` (Vitest) and the Playwright suite for regression; design-partner sessions for real-matter validation. Concrete acceptance checks:

- **Office (WS-A):** open a Word doc containing tracked changes and comments, have the AI redline a clause, export, and confirm it opens in Microsoft Word with every original and new revision intact and individually acceptable/rejectable. Spreadsheet formulas and a PowerPoint deck round-trip without loss.
- **Cited recall (WS-B):** ask "what did [client] say about the deadline" and get the exact email back with a citation that opens the source; confirm no answer renders over client data without a verifiable citation.
- **Trust layer (WS-C):** the egress indicator shows the correct destination for Local, BYOK, and Assured modes; a Matter-A chat never retrieves Matter-B content (checked at index, query, and audit); a privilege-tagged email is excluded from retrieval unless pinned; the audit log records the new events; a non-technical tester can correctly state where their data is after onboarding.
- **Associate (WS-D):** a design-partner litigator confirms the contradiction-finder surfaces real, cited contradictions across a deposition + production and that the Word output is filing-grade.
- **Firm (WS-F):** two users on two machines edit one shared matter with conflict-free sync; an admin allocates and revokes a seat; the assured backend forwards a prompt and provably logs no request body.
- **Assurance/governance (WS-G):** pricing, licensing, and version statements match across website, app, EULA, and LemonSqueezy; Privileged Matter Mode disables all network extensions and the audit proves it.

## Deliberate hard bets (acknowledged, not hidden)

Per the "ignore difficulty" mandate, this plan takes on three things that commonly sink products, on purpose: in-house Word/track-changes fidelity, conflict-free firm collaboration, and operating a zero-retention backend that has to stay true to the privacy moat. Each is isolated into a P0 gate so we prove it before betting the build on it, and each is validated by real firms before 3.0 ships. That is the trade: maximum viability and defensibility, bought with the hardest engineering, de-risked by gating and real-user validation rather than by scoping the ambition down.
