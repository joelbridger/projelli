# 4. The "replace, don't add" positioning platform

*The strategic center of gravity, per Jameson's directive: advisors evaluate new
software by asking "what can this replace?" and refuse to add another tool. This
section (a) checks that insight against the evidence, (b) builds the messaging
architecture on it, (c) does the replacement math an advisor could show a partner, and
(d) states the honest limits of the claim today.*

## 4.1 Is the "replace, don't add" insight true? — Yes, with one sharpening

The wife's-read is externally validated by the most-cited voice in advisor technology.
Michael Kitces, verbatim (AdvisorTech commentary, kitces.com):

> "Any new software tool needs to competitively bid to either replace some other tool
> in the advisors' tech stack, consolidate multiple tools together, or free up advisor
> or staff time to produce net savings after the cost of the software — otherwise
> buying more technology would simply be a drag on the firm's profitability."

And the context that makes it bite:

- A typical independent RIA runs **15–25 software tools** (2025 Kitces Research, via
  Digital Alpha's analysis). The Kitces AdvisorTech map grew from 189 companies in 2018
  to **551 companies across 36 categories** by April 2025 — the supply of "one more
  tool" is effectively infinite, and advisors know it.
- Advisors rate their *whole stack* lower than their individual tools, and fewer than
  1 in 3 report real integration between key systems. The Kitces-derived line:
  "Advisors don't have an AI problem. They have a data problem."
- Practitioner voices, on the record (Financial Planning, Jan 12 2026): "Every fintech
  company wants to be the 'all-in-one' solution, but in reality, most advisors are
  still stitching together three or four different platforms" (Dan Pascone, Tailored
  Wealth). A planner running ~10 tools: "I'm not proud of it… If smarter integrations
  or acquisitions can realistically cut my stack in half, that's meaningful progress"
  (Gitanjali Kumar, Worthique).
- The incumbents are already selling replacement: Fidelity bundles planning + custody
  tech explicitly to cut RIA tool count; Wealthbox bundled a $49/mo AI notetaker into
  the CRM precisely so advisors could cancel a standalone one. InvestmentNews (Jan 9,
  2026, "Is $50 the new $120?"): "the current crowded landscape of standalone AI
  notetakers might not be long for this world."

**The sharpening (from the Codex red-team, and it's right):** tool fatigue does not
mean advisors never add tools — the AI-notetaker category itself went from 1 surveyed
product to 14 with ~43% aggregate adoption in a single year (T3 2026, n=2,906), which
is advisors *adding* by the thousands. What advisors resist is adding **setup, habit
change, and vendor risk without immediate payoff**. So the winning frame is precisely
Jameson's: don't be the 26th tool — be the tool that lets them cancel two or three.
"Replace" is how a new vendor gets *permission to exist* in a saturated stack.

## 4.2 What an advisor can actually cancel when they adopt Lantern

Ground truth = the wave plans + coverage audit, at program completion. Three tiers,
because honesty is the whole game:

**Tier A — clean replacement (say it plainly):**
1. **The AI meeting notetaker** — Jump Meet ($100/mo, $75 small-firm), or Zocks
   ($67–109/mo), FinMate ($95–150/mo), Zeplyn, Fathom/Otter/Fireflies for those using
   generic ones. Waves 1–3 cover capture → notes → tasks → CRM sync → follow-up →
   prep briefs, plus the agenda export, dictation notes, and keyword tracking.
2. **The dictation/transcription service** — Mobile Assistant-style dictation (now
   owned by Jump) and generic transcription subscriptions; Wave 3's dictation +
   import path covers it.
3. **The "ChatGPT on the side" habit** — 52% of advisors now use general AI tools (T3
   2026), typically ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo, pasting client context into a consumer
   chatbot with no citations and no audit trail. Lantern's Ask replaces that for
   client work: same intelligence, their own key, cited from their own files, logged.
   This is a *compliance upgrade* disguised as a replacement — for many firms the
   quiet reality is "we know staff paste client data into ChatGPT and we'd rather not."

**Tier B — replaces the need before it's bought (positioning, not cancellation):**
4. **A document-vault/client-portal AI** (FutureVault-style) — the file pile already
   is the vault, on their machine.
5. **Add-on "insights" modules** — Jump Grow's meeting analytics at +$50/mo; Book view
   + whole-practice Ask answer the solo/small-firm version of that question without a
   surveillance dashboard.

**Tier C — explicitly NOT replaced (say this out loud; it builds trust in the rest):**
- **The CRM** (Wealthbox/Redtail/Salesforce). We write *to* it; it stays the system of
  record and the task system. This is a feature of the pitch: Kitces' data says
  integration is the #1 satisfaction driver, and "we make your CRM better instead of
  selling you another database" is the adult sentence in the category.
- **Planning software** (eMoney/RightCapital/MoneyGuide), **portfolio/reporting**
  (Orion etc.), **custodial platforms**, **e-signature**, **email archiving/
  supervision** (our follow-ups ride the advisor's own inbox, so existing archiving
  keeps working — by design).
- **Word itself.** We're Word-native on purpose; Lantern makes Word the AI surface
  instead of replacing it.

## 4.4 The messaging architecture

**The platform sentence (internal north star):**
*Lantern replaces your notetaker, your transcription service, and your side-chatbot —
and gives you back the one thing none of them had: everything you know about the
client, cited, on your own machine.*

**The ladder — what we're allowed to say, when** (this is where the aggressive
directive and the evidence discipline meet; the Codex red-team's strongest point is
that replacement claims from a zero-customer vendor invite a battlecard, so each rung
unlocks on proof):

| Rung | Claim | Unlocked by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — now | "Costs less than Jump and nothing leaves your machine." Comparison-page voice: factual, cited, calm. | Program completion + the kill sheet's facts |
| 2 | "Lantern can replace Jump for desk-based advisors." | 3+ real-data pilots where capture→notes→CRM ran reliably for weeks (the 06-28 memo's funnel) |
| 3 | "Advisors are replacing Jump with Lantern." | The first 3–5 named or anonymized switchers with their stories |
| 4 — the war cry | "Cancel Jump. Keep the intelligence." (and the full replace-campaign of section 6) | ~10 documented switchers + support capacity to absorb them |

Draft copy blocks for each rung live in section 5 (attack angles) and section 6 (GTM),
written to the house voice rules.

**The three proof artifacts the platform needs (build once, reuse everywhere):**
1. **The replacement-math one-pager** (§4.3, designed, printable — an advisor forwards
   it to a partner).
2. **The switch guide** — "Moving from Jump to Lantern in an afternoon": export/keep
   your Jump notes (they already live in your CRM and SharePoint, which Lantern
   reads — their exhaust is our import path), connect calendar + CRM, first capture.
   The guide existing *is itself marketing*: it says switching is safe.
3. **The "what happens to your data when you leave a cloud notetaker" explainer** —
   quietly devastating, entirely factual: with Lantern, leaving means your files are…
   still your files. With any cloud vendor, leaving means hoping the export worked
   before the retention clock ran out.

## 4.5 The honest limits of the claim today (print these inside the package, never bury them)

1. **We can't capture what the machine doesn't hear.** Advisor on a phone away from
   the desk, closed laptop, a colleague's meeting — Jump's bot/cloud/mobile stack
   covers moments we structurally don't. The bridge (phone recording → synced folder →
   auto-transcribed) is real but is a power-user move, not parity. Mobile-first
   advisors are the wrong first customers for the replace pitch — qualify them out
   honestly.
2. **Reliability is unproven until it's proven.** Capture that fails once at a real
   client meeting kills the account. Jump's own reliability complaints (dropped
   recordings, XYPN's 3.5/5 accuracy score) are an attack surface for us *only after*
   our capture has survived real pilots — until then the attack invites "and yours?".
   Rung 2 exists for this reason.
3. **Enterprise replacement is off the table for now.** No SOC 2, no mobile apps, no
   admin console at their depth, no support team; LPL/Osaic/Cetera advisors often
   don't choose their own stack anyway. The replace play targets independent solos and
   small RIAs who self-approve.
4. **A one-person vendor is a fair objection.** The honest answers: the data is the
   advisor's own files in standard formats (vendor failure strands nothing), the
   design-partner case study, and a controlled early-access posture (limited pilots,
   founder support) rather than pretending to be a scaled vendor.
5. **"Replace your CRM's notetaker" is a different fight.** Wealthbox AI at ~$49/mo
   inside the CRM they already pay for is the hardest competitor for the *replace*
   frame (it adds no new vendor at all). Against CRM-bundled AI we win on depth
   (whole-pile, cited, Word-native, private) — not on consolidation. Know which pitch
   you're in.
