# 8. Open questions for Jameson — the decisions only the board can make

*Each question in plain language, with my recommendation and why. None of these are
made for you; the package works under any answer, but several downstream pieces wait
on them.*

## Q1. How aggressive do we go publicly, and when?

**The tension:** your directive says aggressive "we can replace Jump" marketing. The
independent red-team (Codex, arguing the opposite on purpose) says a company with
zero customers attacking a liked incumbent looks insecure, invites Jump's sales team
to define us, and turns every capture bug into a public broken promise.

**My recommendation: adopt the claim ladder (section 4.4) — aggressive in substance
immediately, aggressive in voice on proof.** Concretely: the moment the program
completes, we publish the comparison page, the contract essay, and the replacement
math — all of it names Jump, all of it is confrontational *in content* (their own
contract language, their price history, the bot in the room). But the literal words
"replace Jump" / "cancel Jump" wait for the first documented switchers. This gets you
the aggression you asked for where it's safe (facts they can't rebut) and holds the
one claim that needs witnesses until we have them. The difference between rung 1 and
rung 4 is maybe a few months of pilot time — and skipping it is the one move that
could hand Jump a win.

## Q2. Do we name Jump on the homepage, or only on comparison pages?

**My recommendation: comparison pages, the essay, and outbound conversations — yes.
Homepage — no.** The homepage sells our story ("connect your files, ask cited
questions, nothing leaves your machine"); a homepage that leads with a competitor's
name makes them the reference point and us the accessory. Advisors who are evaluating
Jump will find the comparison page — that's what comparison SEO is for. This is also
the standard practice that keeps legal exposure boxed to pages a lawyer has reviewed.

## Q3. When do real advisors first see this? (The build-first decision, revisited once)

**Context:** you decided all waves (0–4) get built before advisors see the
experience, overriding the "validate waves 0–2 first" recommendation — that decision
stands and this program executes it. **But one thing can start earlier without
showing anyone anything: recruiting.** Discovery interviews (the staged campaign) are
conversations about *their* workflow, not demos of our product — and recruiting
takes weeks of calendar lag anyway.

**My recommendation:** authorize interview recruiting now (Rings 1–3 per the staged
campaign), timed so the first interviews land as Wave 4 wraps. You lose nothing —
no advisor sees an unfinished product — and the parity program ends with a full
demo calendar instead of a cold start. If you'd rather keep even recruiting until
after the merge, the plan still works; it just adds the lag at the end.

## Q4. What's the public name this all ships under?

**Context:** the surfaces currently disagree — the app/repo say Keepance, this
program says Lantern, and the live advisor comparison page is branded "Advisor Prep
Hero" (with matching domain). A renameability analysis already exists
(docs/strategy/2026-06-29-renameability-analysis.md).

**My recommendation:** decide before ANY Jump-facing marketing ships — a brand that
changes mid-campaign burns the trust the campaign exists to build, and the receipts
vault (archived pages, essays, switcher stories) all bake the name in. I don't have
a strong view between the candidates; I have a very strong view that it's one name
everywhere before Stage 1 fires. This is the single longest-lead open item.

## Q5. Do we fix the current public vs-Jump page now, or wait for the full rewrite?

**Context:** the live page has at least one apparently false claim about Jump (a
HIPAA credit no research pass could verify), a stale advisor count, stale "Markdown"
copy, and framing ("isn't a meeting-notes tool") that Wave 3 will flatly contradict.

**My recommendation: fix the falsehoods this week; full rewrite at Wave 3 merge.**
An incorrect claim about a competitor on our own comparison page is exactly the
credibility hole the hard rules exist to prevent — it shouldn't wait for the
program. The full replacement page (both doors, receipts, our-weaknesses-first) is
Stage 0 work.

## Q6. What price does the replacement math run on?

**Context:** list is Solo $468 / Pro $948 / Firm $1,548-per-seat annually; the 06-28
memo recommends testing ~$1,800 solo ("$99 signals toy"); the replacement math (§4.3)
stays compelling up to ~$2,000.

**My recommendation:** test **$1,500–1,800/yr solo** in the first paid pilots, per
the memo, and print the replacement one-pager at whatever survives contact with real
buyers. Don't decide the number today — decide today that *pilots set it*, and kill
any $99-style founding price on advisor surfaces now (it undercuts the "serious tool
that replaces a $2,400 stack" story).

## Q7. Do you authorize the switch-incentive ("we credit your remaining Jump months")?

**Context:** Stage 3's sharpest weapon; costs real dollars per switcher (bounded by a
cap we set); reads as supreme confidence.

**My recommendation: yes, capped** — e.g., up to 3 months / $300 per advisor, first
20 switchers, revisit after. At ~95% gross margin a $300 acquisition cost for a
documented, story-telling switcher is the cheapest distribution we will ever buy.
Board call because it's real money and it sets a public precedent.

## Q8. Do we spend on a lawyer for the comparison assets?

**Context:** hard rule 6 requires a legal pass on anything naming Jump; comparative
advertising is legal when truthful and substantiated, but the burden of proof sits
on us, and we're also quoting their contracts.

**My recommendation: yes — one flat-fee engagement** (review the comparison page,
the contract essay, the one-pager, and our own claims list; likely low four
figures). For a trust product attacking a $105M incumbent, this is the cheapest
insurance in the plan. Fold in a same-pass review of our Wave 4 voiceprint-consent
copy (biometrics law) since the lawyer will already be in the documents.

## Q9. The mobile gap — accept it, or fund a minimal companion app after parity?

**Context:** the honest gap the red-team leaned on hardest: no mobile capture, and
"record on your phone → synced folder" is a power-user bridge. Our ICP (desk-based
solo/small RIA) mostly meets at the desk — but "mostly" will cost us some deals.

**My recommendation: accept it through the first ten switchers.** Qualify
mobile-first advisors out honestly (it builds trust and saves demos), instrument how
often the gap kills a deal, and revisit a minimal companion recorder app (capture +
sync only, no cloud) as a beyond-parity candidate only if the debriefs say it's the
blocker. Building mobile now would delay the flank where we actually win.

---

*One meta-note: questions 1–3 interact. If you take Q1's ladder and Q3's
recruit-now, the aggressive campaign and the proof it needs arrive at nearly the
same time — which is the whole trick of this plan.*
