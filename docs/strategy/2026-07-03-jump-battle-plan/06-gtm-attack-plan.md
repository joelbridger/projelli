# 6. Go-to-market attack plan — reaching Jump's users and evaluators

*Constraint honesty up front: distribution is our weakest asset (no Kitces presence,
no XYPN footprint, no brand, no customers) and Jump's strongest. This plan is built
for a founder-sold, zero-budget motion that converts our architecture story into the
first ten switchers — not a spray campaign we can't staff. Everything here is a
recommendation; nothing fires without Jameson's go.*

## 6.1 The two doors (run both, always)

Every piece of marketing offers the same two entrances:

- **Door A — Replace** ("switch from Jump"): for the fed-up (reliability victims,
  price-resenters), the never-committed (evaluating notetakers right now), and the
  privacy-anxious. This is the aggressive door — sections 4–5 are its ammunition.
- **Door B — Coexist** ("keep your notetaker, add the private brain"): for the 35,000
  who like Jump. Wave 0 built this on purpose: Jump's notes land in Wealthbox and
  SharePoint, which Lantern reads — so a Jump loyalist can adopt Lantern *without
  switching anything*, and Lantern then cites their Jump notes alongside the whole
  file pile. Door B is how we get *inside* Jump accounts and let the product make the
  replacement argument over months. The red-team is right that Door B converts wider;
  Door A is what makes us mean something. The doors don't conflict: A is the message,
  B is the on-ramp.

## 6.2 Sequence — four stages, each gated on evidence, mapped to the claim ladder (§4.4)

### Stage 0 (now → program completion): build the arsenal, fire nothing
1. Rewrite `website/vs/jump.html` — it's stale and partly wrong (27,000 count, an
   apparently false HIPAA credit to Jump, "plain Markdown" copy, "isn't a
   meeting-notes tool" framing that Wave 3 obsoletes, and it's branded "Advisor Prep
   Hero" — see section 8 on naming). Rebuild it as the Rung-1 comparison page: calm,
   dated, cited, both doors, our weaknesses stated first.
2. Produce the three §4.4 proof artifacts (replacement-math one-pager, switch guide,
   leaving-a-cloud-notetaker explainer).
3. Archive every cited Jump page (MSA, privacy policy, methodology, pricing) — the
   receipts vault behind the hard rules.
4. Legal pass on the comparison page + one-pager.

### Stage 1 (at program completion): the discovery campaign, upgraded — PEOPLE FIRST
The staged-but-unfired campaign (`~/keepance/docs/marketing/campaigns/
2026-06-advisor-first-users/` — rings of warmth from the design partner outward,
cal.com booking + recorded Quo calls, Mom-Test scripts) fires **as designed**, with
three Jump-specific additions:
- **Recruit Jump users deliberately** into the interview pool (r/CFP, XYPN forum, and
  peer intros: "you use Jump? I'll pay for 30 minutes of what you love and hate about
  it"). Their answers calibrate the whole battle plan — especially which of our ten
  angles land with real buyers.
- **Add the switch probe** to the interview script: "What would a tool have to do for
  you to cancel one you already pay for? Have you ever switched? What triggered it?"
  (Tests the replace-don't-add thesis on live subjects.)
- **The demo becomes the Jump demo.** The 4-beat demo script gains beat 5: the
  crash-recovery capture demo and the "here's the meeting cited on the Client Map next
  to the will and the 1099" moment — the two things Jump physically can't show.
Scoreboard stays the memo's funnel: 10–15 conversations → 5 demos → 3 real-data
pilots → 3–5 weekly-active → 1–2 paying. **Pilots are what unlock Rung 2 claims and
Angle 8.**

### Stage 2 (first pilots live): the comparison-SEO beachhead
Advisors evaluating notetakers search; comparison queries are cheap to own because
incumbents rarely bid on their own alternatives:
- Pages: "Jump alternatives" · "Jump vs Lantern" · "Zocks vs Jump vs Lantern" ·
  "AI notetaker without a bot" · "local AI for financial advisors" · "what happens to
  your data when you cancel an AI notetaker." Each is Rung-1/2 voiced, cites sources,
  runs both doors.
- One flagship essay, written under the house voice rules, carrying Angle 1: *"I read
  Jump's customer agreement so you don't have to."* Quotes verbatim, links archives,
  states our own asterisks. This is the piece that gets passed around r/CFP — the
  goal is to be *the person who did the homework*, not the competitor who threw mud.
- Kitces AdvisorTech map listing + XYPN vendor directory + T3 survey participation
  the moment we qualify — these are where evaluators actually look (advisors buy
  ~49% from demos and ~43% from peer referral; directories and comparison pages are
  where the demo request comes from).

### Stage 3 (3+ documented switchers): the replace campaign proper
- Named or anonymized switcher stories ("why I canceled Jump"), each tying to one
  angle — never composite, never paid-for, written permission always (campaign
  guardrails already say this).
- The switch-incentive: **"Switch on us" — we credit your remaining Jump months**
  (bounded: up to N months / $X cap) plus white-glove onboarding (the concierge
  motion from the 06-28 memo doing double duty). Cheap at our ~95% margin, loud as a
  message: *we're so sure, we'll buy out your contract.*
- Community presence where Jump's users complain when Jump stumbles (r/CFP threads,
  XYPN forum): helpful-first, receipts-ready. Never ambush; answer questions asked.
- Podcast/guest circuit with the local-first + Reg S-P angle (Kitces guest post is
  the crown target; the FPA/NAPFA local chapters are the warm stage).

### Stage 4 (10+ switchers): the category claim
"The private alternative to Jump" becomes "the practice intelligence Jump can't
build." Beyond-parity Tier 1 (section 7) ships by now — the story graduates from
replacement to superiority ("it caught the stale beneficiary").

## 6.3 What the first 10 switchers are actually worth

Strategically, they are worth more than the next 100 customers:
1. **They unlock the claim ladder.** Rungs 3–4 are gated on them; without switchers
   the whole aggressive posture stays theoretical.
2. **They're the proof Kitces/T3/XYPN coverage needs.** "Local-first vendor says" is
   a pitch; "eleven advisors canceled Jump for this" is a story.
3. **They kill the one-person-vendor objection** better than any document — a
   fiduciary who switched *and said so publicly* did the diligence for the next ten.
4. **They're the objection library.** Every switcher interview = the real reasons
   people leave Jump, in buyer language, feeding every page above.
5. **They set Jump's response clock.** We should assume Jump notices at roughly this
   scale (their sales team will hear us in deals). Everything in Stage 0–2 must be
   finished before the noise starts — you don't build the receipts vault during the
   artillery exchange.

Instrument them: a one-page "switch debrief" per switcher (what triggered it, what
almost stopped them, what they canceled, what they'd tell a peer), with permission
levels recorded.

## 6.5 What we do NOT do

- No outreach of any kind before Jameson fires the campaign (standing rule).
- No enterprise/BD pursuit — Jump's walled garden; wasted founder hours.
- No attacking Jump in a thread we weren't asked into; no astroturfing, ever — one
  sockpuppet discovery would erase the entire trust position.
- No "Jump connector" branded integration (per the 06-29 connector analysis: reading
  their exports via CRM/SharePoint is free and safe; a branded connector legitimizes
  them in our UI and builds on goodwill they can revoke).
- No launch-day stunt against their press cycle. Their $80M buys share of voice;
  ours is earned one advisor at a time. Asymmetric warfare means never fighting where
  the money decides.
