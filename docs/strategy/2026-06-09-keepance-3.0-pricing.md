# Keepance 3.0 Pricing: Recommendation

**Prepared by:** Claude (as CEO), 2026-06-09
**Status:** RATIFIED by Jameson on 2026-06-09 ("Pricing looks good, go with it"). This is the locked Keepance 3.0 pricing. Building it out and deploying.
**Basis:** the deep-research pricing report (run 2026-06-09; sources cited in the appendix) + the 4-source attorney UX study + the existing Keepance pricing.

> The short version: **move Keepance 3.0 from one-time to per-seat annual subscription**, in three tiers (Solo / Professional / Firm), priced well below specialized legal AI and at or above productivity AI. Keep BYOK and turn it into a transparency selling point. Protect the local-first soul with a hard "your files are always yours" guarantee so subscription does not mean lock-in.

---

## 1. The recommendation, in numbers

Per-seat. Billed annually (headline price); month-to-month available at roughly a 25 to 30 percent premium. A 30-day free trial, no card, stays.

| Tier | Annual (per seat) | Month-to-month | Who it is for | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo** | **$39/mo ($468/yr)** | $49/mo | A single attorney | The complete confidential workspace: the Word-native editor, AI redlining (tracked changes), confidential matter-scoped cited recall across email + documents, privilege controls, local/BYOK, the trust layer |
| **Professional** | **$79/mo ($948/yr)** | $99/mo | The serious solo / small-firm litigator | Everything in Solo, plus the litigation associate (deposition contradiction-finder, timelines, discovery triage, privilege-log drafter), version history, all practice packs, priority support |
| **Firm** | **$129/seat/mo ($1,548/seat/yr)**, min 3 seats | $159/seat/mo | Firms (roughly 5 to 50 attorneys) | Everything in Professional, plus shared matters and collaboration, an admin console with SSO and ethical walls, the assured zero-retention option, and the assurance package (DPA, trust center, SOC 2 readiness) |

**Founding rate:** the first cohort (e.g., first 100 Solo/Professional seats and first 10 firms) lock a ~30 percent discount for the life of the subscription. This seeds adoption and rewards the early-trust buyers our UX research said are decisive.

**Existing buyers are grandfathered.** Anyone who bought the current one-time Personal/Lifetime or an annual plan keeps what they paid for, in good faith. 3.0 is a new, repositioned product at new pricing; we do not claw back.

## 2. The billing model: subscription, not one-time (and why)

The current product is one-time ($49 Personal / ~$149/yr Professional / ~$499/yr Practice). The original rationale (Obsidian/Sublime-style one-time, lower billing complexity) was right for a cheap local note tool. **It is wrong for 3.0.** Three reasons, all from the research:

1. **The category prices per-seat subscription, and buyers expect it.** Clio and MyCase, the practice-management tools every small firm already pays for, are per-user/month with an annual discount (Clio $39 to $139/user/mo annual; MyCase $50 to $130). [clio.com/pricing, mycase.com/pricing] A one-time price reads as "toy," not "the tool I run my practice on."
2. **3.0 has real recurring costs.** Continuous AI and legal updates, security, support, and (for the Firm tier) a cloud backend + the assured proxy. One-time pricing cannot fund an evolving, compliance-grade product. The BYOK research is explicit: the customer pays inference; the vendor still pays for everything else. [jetbrains.com BYOK, tianpan.co]
3. **The firm tier is inherently recurring.** Collaboration, multi-seat, admin, and assured hosting are ongoing services. They do not fit a one-time model at all.

**Resolving the tension with our local-first soul.** Our UX research found attorneys value "my files are mine, no lock-in." Subscription can feel like the opposite. We resolve this with a guarantee, stated plainly on the pricing page and in-app:

> **Your files are always yours.** They are real Word and PDF files in your own folders. If you ever cancel, you keep every document, email, and matter you have made. A lapsed subscription turns off the AI features and updates, not your data. Nothing is ever held hostage.

This preserves the anti-lock-in value (data ownership) while moving the platform/AI/updates to a sustainable model. I considered keeping a one-time "local-only Personal" SKU for the purist; I recommend against it for 3.0 (it under-monetizes a recurring-cost product, splits the story, and the data-ownership guarantee already answers the lock-in fear). We can revisit if the market pushes back.

## 3. How BYOK is framed (turn the objection into a win)

Our UX participant's instinct on the old pricing was "and I also pay the AI company separately?" That is a trap if hidden and a differentiator if told first. The research agrees: BYOK is a transparency and savings advantage, not a reason to discount the platform fee. Recommended copy:

> **You bring your own AI key and pay the provider directly** (usually $5 to $15 a month for a solo). We take zero markup on AI usage, and you see exactly what it costs. Prefer that nothing leaves your machine at all? Run a local model with Ollama and pay nothing for AI. Either way, your subscription covers the platform: the Word editor, AI redlining, confidential cited recall, privilege controls, encryption, updates, and support. It does not cover the AI tokens, and we never mark them up.

This is honest, it is the thing no cloud competitor can say, and it pre-empts the "second bill" surprise our research warned "tells thirty other lawyers."

## 5. Key risks and mitigations

1. **Solo budget ceiling.** 74 percent of solos spend under $3,000/yr on software; only 41 percent budget for tech at all. [ABA 2024 Solo & Small Firm TechReport] Mitigation: Solo at $468/yr is one Clio seat and a small slice of that ceiling, leaves room for BYOK spend, and the trial + founding rate lower the entry bar.
2. **The 3x jump from the old $149/yr.** Mitigation: 3.0 is a different, far more capable product; grandfather existing buyers; lead with the value (the editor + redline + recall + associate) and the CoCounsel comparison; founding rate.
3. **Subscription alienating the original one-time/local-first audience.** Mitigation: the data-ownership guarantee; honest framing; the option to revisit a local-only one-time SKU if churn signals demand it.
4. **BYOK "second bill" confusion.** Mitigation: the up-front transparency framing above.
5. **Assured zero-retention may carry real per-customer infra cost.** Open question from the research. Mitigation: bundle it into Firm for now; if infra cost is high, make it a metered Firm add-on rather than discounting the base.
6. **AI adoption is broad but shallow among small firms** (72 percent any-use, only 8 percent wide). [Clio 2025 Legal Trends] Mitigation: keep the Solo entry accessible and onboarding effortless to convert the curious-but-uncommitted majority; capture margin at Firm where wide adoption and WTP concentrate.
