# Keepance — Pricing Coherence: Recommendation
**Date:** 2026-06-01 · Author: Claude (operator) for Jameson (CEO)
**Context:** Flagged in `LAUNCH_READINESS_AND_FIRST_DOLLAR_2026-06-01.md` (Tier 3.7). A deep-research harness run failed at synthesis; this is the operator's strategic recommendation. WTP figures below are from general market knowledge, not freshly re-verified — verify specifics before quoting publicly.

## The reframe that changes everything
The "cannibalization" worry (a solo buys Personal $49 once, DIYs templates, never pays $149/yr) is a **prosumer/indie behavior, not a professional-buyer behavior.** Our ICP — solo & small-firm attorneys, CPAs/EAs — already pays **$40–150 per user per *month*** for practice software (Clio, MyCase, Smokeball, TaxDome, etc.), i.e. **$500–1,800+/year per tool, across several tools.** To them, **$149/YEAR ($12/mo) is a rounding error.** A lawyer billing $250–400/hr will not DIY compliance-aware templates to save $12/mo — that's irrational for them.

**So price is not the barrier for the ICP. Trust and demonstrated value are.** The cannibalization risk is real only for the *prosumer/individual* buyer — who was never going to pay the recurring fee anyway and is better thought of as top-of-funnel/adoption, not lost revenue.

## Recommended model: "Own the app. Subscribe to the maintained pack."
Keep the three tiers, with one change and one reframe:

| Tier | Price | For whom | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | **$49 one-time** (keep) | Individuals, prosumers, the curious, privacy enthusiasts | Own the app forever: editor, all AI providers via BYOK, local-first, general templates. No profession pack. *Cheap + one-time is a feature here — it drives adoption, reviews, word-of-mouth, and dollar #1. These buyers ≠ the recurring ICP.* |
| **Professional** | **$149/yr** (keep recurring) | Practicing attorneys / CPAs / consultants | App + ONE profession pack **reviewed by practicing pros and kept current as the rules change** + priority support + new packs as they ship. |
| **Practice** | **$499/yr** (CHANGE from one-time) | Small firms (3–5 seats) | All packs maintained + up to 5 seats + named support. |

**The one change:** make **Practice recurring ($499/yr), not one-time.** A one-time "all packs forever" reintroduces the exact cannibalization problem at the firm level (a firm buys once, never renews) and breaks the coherence. If a one-time option is wanted for firms, price it at a clear premium (e.g. $999 one-time + 2 yrs updates) so recurring stays the better deal.

**The reframe (mostly already done on the site):** the recurring fee buys **ongoing review + maintenance** — "your profession pack, reviewed by practicing [attorneys/CPAs], kept current as the law and the rules change." The law/regs change every year; that's a genuine recurring *service*, not a static file. **Price the review and the upkeep, never the templates** (pricing the files invites DIY; pricing the maintained-compliance relationship doesn't).

### Why this is coherent
The one-time-vs-subscription split becomes *intentional and clear*: you pay **once to own your local-first tool** (on-brand for "your data, your machine"; and with BYOK there's no cost-to-serve, so a perpetual app sale is fine), and you pay **yearly for maintained, profession-reviewed content + support** (a real recurring service). Personal and Professional serve **different buyers**, so the overlap is a feature, not a leak.

## Alternatives considered
- **A — Status quo (Practice stays one-time):** simplest, zero change. But Practice-one-time reintroduces firm-level cannibalization and the coherence question lingers. *Acceptable but weaker.*
- **B — Decouple app from packs:** $49 one-time app for everyone + **$99–149/yr per-pack add-on subscription.** Conceptually the cleanest ("own the tool, subscribe to the content"), and it cleanly solves the lapse problem (you always keep the app). Downside: "add-on" framing can dilute the all-in-one feel, and it's a bigger packaging change. *Strong runner-up — worth A/B testing later.*
- **C — Subscription-only (no one-time at all):** maximizes recurring revenue and simplest billing, but **kills the local-first "own your tool" differentiator**, the low-friction entry, and the fastest path to dollar #1. *Not recommended for a local-first brand.*

## Top risks of the recommendation
1. **The recurring value is a promise, not yet reality.** The whole subscription logic rests on the packs being genuinely valuable, maintained, and *professionally reviewed* — and they're unreviewed drafts today. **The #1 priority is validating the packs (advisor review), not repricing.** Until then the $149/yr story isn't fully true.
2. **Making Practice recurring may deter firms** who prefer to "buy and be done." Mitigate with a premium one-time option or annual prepay; watch conversion.
3. **Premature optimization.** Pre-launch with zero customers, the *exact* numbers are guesses. The pricing is "good enough" to launch; let real buying + churn data drive v2. Don't let pricing perfectionism delay getting the first dollar and the first testimonial.

## DECISION (Jameson, 2026-06-01): ACCEPTED — execute all of it
- Personal $49 one-time: keep.
- Professional $149/yr: keep, sell as maintained/reviewed compliance content.
- **Practice: move $499 one-time → $499/yr (subscription).**
- **Operating directive: ASSUME THE PACKS WILL BE APPROVED. Stop gating anything on advisor review** — present packs as production/available (remove "preview / pending review / ships after review" hedges on the site + app registry `preview:true`→false on the next app release). Advisor recruiting still proceeds in parallel, but it no longer blocks shipping/messaging.

## Bottom line
Keep Personal $49 one-time (it's the adoption/first-dollar engine, aimed at a different buyer). Keep Professional $149/yr but sell it as *maintained, reviewed compliance content*, not templates. Move Practice to $499/yr for coherence. Then **stop optimizing pricing and go validate the packs + get the first customers** — that's the real lever, and it's where the actual gap is.
