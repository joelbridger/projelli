# Advisor Prep Hero Website vs. the Advisor-Tech Research: Where to Dial In

**Date:** 2026-06-24
**What this is:** A direct comparison of the **current keepance.com** against the **Kitces advisor-technology research** (in `./research/`), to find where Advisor Prep Hero can sharpen to win in today's advisor-tech landscape. Written plainly.
**Method:** My read of the live site (homepage, `/how-keepance-fits/`, `/fits-your-stack/`, security, pricing) against the research library, cross-checked by an independent Codex pass (`./codex-website-vs-research-gap.md`).

---

## TL;DR — the five highest-leverage moves

1. **Lead with the objection, not the category.** The market's #1 mood is "tech bloat / not another tool." The site says that beautifully on the fits pages, but the **hero** still leads with "Secure client intelligence for high-trust work." Bring "you already pay for a dozen tools, this isn't a thirteenth" *up to the hero.*
2. **Sell integration, because it's the #1 thing advisors reward.** The product copy talks about "files, emails, notes." Advisors reward tools that make their *stack* work together. Talk about reading across **the tools they already pay for** (name them), not just files.
3. **Turn citations from a feature into the answer to a fear.** The research's gating barrier is "one wrong AI answer can lose a client or trigger a lawsuit." Frame citations as *"AI you can actually check,"* not a bullet point.
4. **Fix the stale non-advisor examples.** The homepage still shows an **NDA** redline demo, a "delivery delay / contractual window" recall example, and a `keepance-attorney-clients.png` hero image. These are leftovers from the law-firm era and quietly undercut the advisor re-aim.
5. **Add peer proof + get into the places advisors actually research.** Advisors buy via demos, peers, and industry research, not ads. The site has the demo; it's missing a **design-partner advisor quote** and a presence in the **Kitces AdvisorTech map/directory.**

## 1. What the site already gets right (keep and amplify)

The site is genuinely well-aligned with the research on several fronts. These are strengths to protect:

- **"Where Advisor Prep Hero fits" + "How Advisor Prep Hero fits" + "isn't a thirteenth tool" + "switches you away from nothing."** This is a direct, on-target answer to the two biggest research findings: tech bloat and switching inertia. It's recent and strong. **Keep it; push it higher.**
- **Citations everywhere.** The research is clear that citation-grounded AI is what won advisor trust in 2025-2026. The site leans on this correctly.
- **Local-first / BYOK / no-vendor-server + the Reg S-P compliance bar.** This is exactly the privacy + vendor-oversight story the research says is now a real, timely lever (especially after the near-miss where a vendor almost sold millions of client meeting transcripts as AI training data). The three compliance columns are well done and honest ("informational, not compliance advice").
- **The prep + drafting lane** ("Meet with confidence," "Draft with context"). The research says advisors want AI in the *back office* (prep, document review, drafting) with a human in the loop, and explicitly *not* in client service. The site is in the right lane.
- **Advisor Practice Pack** (Reg BI, Reg S-P, Annual Review, Meeting-Prep/Suitability, Books & Records). Advisor-specific and credible.
- **Outcome language** ("serve clients with the context they expect you to remember," "make a client feel known"). The research says ~8 in 10 advisors value tech that improves *advice/client experience* over pure efficiency. This framing is on-point.

## 2. The gaps, and how to dial in (prioritized)

### A. The hero doesn't lead with the market's strongest emotional truth
**Research:** "Tech bloat" is the dominant mood; satisfaction is *falling*; "I don't want another tool" is the default reflex.
**Now:** The hero leads with "Secure client intelligence for high-trust work" (good, but it's a category statement). The "not a thirteenth tool" message is buried mid-page and on the fits pages.
**Dial in:** Work the "you already pay for a dozen tools, Advisor Prep Hero isn't a thirteenth, it reads across them" idea into the hero or the line directly under it. Lead with the objection you overcome, then the category.

### B. "Integration" is the #1 satisfaction driver, but the copy sells "files," not "your stack"
**Research:** Integration (tools sharing data) is the single biggest driver of tech-stack satisfaction; "siloed" is the named AI failure mode.
**Now:** The radial graphic shows tool logos (great), but the core feature copy (Ask / Draft / Meet) talks about "files, emails, notes." It under-claims the thing advisors reward most.
**Dial in:** Frame Advisor Prep Hero as making *the stack you already pay for* finally answer you. Name the real tools in the value copy, and be honest and concrete about *how* it reads them today (Outlook email is built; OneDrive/local client folders; planning/tax/custodian PDFs you export; CRM on the roadmap). Reward = "your tools, finally working together," without overclaiming live integrations.

### C. Citations are a feature, not yet the answer to the fear
**Research:** The gating barrier to AI adoption is trust: "it sometimes takes only one big mistake to lose a client or face a career-ending lawsuit."
**Now:** Citations are listed as a capability.
**Dial in:** Name the fear and answer it. Something like: *"You can't bet a client relationship on an AI you can't check. Every Advisor Prep Hero answer shows its source, so you can verify before you act."* Make "trust, but verify" an explicit pillar, not a bullet.

### D. Stale non-advisor examples undercut the re-aim
**Research:** Resonance comes from advisor-specific, concrete scenarios.
**Now (concrete defects on the homepage):**
- "Draft with context" demo is a **Mutual_NDA.docx** redline (legal, not advisor).
- "Ask about anything" demo is a generic "delivery delay / contractual window / vendor delays" example (consulting/legal, not advisor).
- Hero image file is `keepance-attorney-clients.png` (alt text says "advisor," filename says attorney).
**Dial in:** Swap to advisor scenarios you already use elsewhere on the page: an **Annual Review letter** or **Reg BI note** for the drafting demo; a **Roth conversion / 529 / beneficiary** recall for the Q&A demo; rename/replace the hero image. Small change, real credibility gain.

### E. The "you stay in control / never client-facing" reassurance is missing
**Research:** Advisors want AI to *expedite* (57%) not *automate* (28%), and the one place a majority want AI to stay out of is **client service**.
**Now:** The product is in the right lane but doesn't say this out loud.
**Dial in:** Add an explicit reassurance: *"Advisor Prep Hero never talks to your clients. It makes you more prepared. AI proposes, you decide, and nothing goes out without your review."* This turns a research-backed advisor preference into a selling point.

### F. The most-validated AI use (document review) isn't the hero of the demo
**Research:** The single most common *real* AI use among advisors today is **reviewing client documents** (tax returns, estate docs). It's the proven, low-risk on-ramp.
**Now:** The site leads with broad "client intelligence" and Client Map.
**Dial in:** Make the first thing a prospect can *do* the proven behavior: "Point it at one client's folder and ask a question." Anchor the demo and a hero CTA on document-reading + cited Q&A, then expand to the Client Map and drafting.

### G. The homepage doesn't address the funded competition (the fits page now does)
**Research:** The cross-source AI layer is a contested, well-funded race (Jump's AI Associate, Orion Denali, Advisor360, Microsoft Copilot). "We have AI" is commodity; even tax-doc reading is commodity.
**Now:** The new `/how-keepance-fits/` matrix handles this well. The homepage doesn't acknowledge it.
**Dial in:** A short homepage teaser answering "why not just use Copilot, my CRM's AI, or Jump?" that links to the matrix. The honest wedge: *local-first + cross-source + cited + Word-native, for the advisor who won't pour everything into one cloud platform.*

### H. Distribution + proof: the site is the demo, but peer trust is missing
**Research:** Advisors buy via vendor demos (49%), peers (43%), blogs/podcasts (33%), and industry studies (31%), and only for a clearly superior product. They ignore cold outbound.
**Now:** The `/try/` browser demo exists (good). There's no peer/social proof and (as far as I can tell) no presence in the Kitces AdvisorTech map/directory.
**Dial in:** (1) Add a **design-partner advisor testimonial** (your wife's firm or another real user) near the hero or CTA. (2) Get Advisor Prep Hero **listed on the Kitces AdvisorTech map + fintech directory** (advisors browse it when researching). (3) Make "try it on one real client in five minutes" the primary CTA.

### I. Keep avoiding the "AI assistant" shelf
**Research:** The standalone "agentic AI assistant" category is low-importance and the most distrusted.
**Now:** Mostly avoided (the site leads with "Client Map / layer"), but "private AI workspace" leans slightly assistant-y.
**Dial in:** Keep leading with "the layer over your stack / Client Map," not "AI workspace/assistant." Minor, but worth holding the line.

## 3. The single highest-leverage change

**Lead the hero with the objection you overcome, and prove it with one concrete "annual review prep" demo.**

Two halves, both research-backed:

**(a) Objection-led hero.** The fits pages nailed the message; the hero hasn't caught up. Open with *"You already pay for a dozen tools. Advisor Prep Hero isn't a thirteenth — it reads across the ones you use and answers with citations you can check, on your own machine."* That aligns the top of the funnel with what advisors reward (no new tool, integration, citations/trust, privacy).

**(b) One concrete proof demo (this is the part to actually build).** Advisors buy off demos. Show Advisor Prep Hero answering one realistic question across the real messy stack, with a citation on every fact:

> **"What changed for the Smiths since our last annual review, and what should I bring up?"**
> Sources cited: RightCapital plan export · Holistiplan tax report · Schwab/Tamarac statement · an Outlook thread · a OneDrive estate PDF · a Jump meeting note.
> Output: a "what changed" summary, a "needs your review" list, a **missing-info list**, a draft agenda, and a draft follow-up email — each claim linked to its source, and anything unsupported labeled *"I couldn't find support for that"* rather than guessed.

That single demo ties together every research point at once: tech bloat (works over the stack), integration (connects scattered evidence), back-office prep (not client-facing), document review (the #1 real AI use), trust (cited + refuses to guess), privacy (local/BYOK), switching inertia (change nothing), and demo-led buying (peer-shareable). Pair it with a **"Try it on one household"** CTA. Everything else in this doc supports this.

## 4. Suggested order of work
1. Hero rewrite (objection-led) + advisor demo CTA. *(highest leverage)*
2. Swap the stale legal/generic examples for advisor ones; replace the hero image.
3. Add the "trust, but verify / you stay in control / never client-facing" pillar.
4. Reframe the value copy around "your stack, working together" (integration), honestly scoped.
5. Add a design-partner advisor quote; pursue a Kitces AdvisorTech map/directory listing.
6. Add a homepage "why not just use Copilot / my CRM AI / Jump?" teaser linking to the matrix.

## Independent cross-check (Codex) — agreement, additions, and one correction

I ran an independent Codex pass (`./codex-website-vs-research-gap.md`) and triangulated it against this analysis.

**Strong agreement** on: tech bloat as the dominant mood; "not a thirteenth tool" is the right narrative; integration is the #1 reward; back-office (not client-facing) AI; citations as the trust spine; precise local/BYOK privacy wording; de-emphasize the "AI assistant" category ("use AI as the engine, not the category").

**Additions folded in from Codex (genuinely valuable):**
- The concrete **"annual review prep" demo** as the highest-leverage proof (now §3).
- **"Try it on one household first"** as the CTA + setup framing (lowers switching cost).
- Make **"I couldn't find support for that"** a visible trust feature (a refusal to guess is valuable in regulated work).
- Frame privacy as **"reduce the number of vendors holding copies of client data"** (sharper Reg S-P/vendor-review angle).
- Honesty guardrail: don't imply live API integrations where Advisor Prep Hero reads files/email/exports today.

**⚠️ One correction — a factual error in the Codex pass.** Codex concluded the *live homepage is still "attorney-first"* ("private intelligence layer for your law practice," depositions, privilege logs, Clio, ABA 512) and made that its #1 recommendation. **This is wrong: it fetched a stale/cached copy.** I verified the live homepage directly — the hero is "Secure client intelligence for high-trust work," and there are **zero** legal terms (no "law practice," depositions, ABA, privilege, opposing counsel) in the served HTML, which matches the deployed advisor source. So **disregard Codex's "build an advisor-first homepage because the current one is legal-first" premise** — the homepage is already advisor-first. The real homepage gap is narrower: the hero leads with the *category* ("client intelligence") rather than the *objection* ("not a thirteenth tool"), per §2A above.
