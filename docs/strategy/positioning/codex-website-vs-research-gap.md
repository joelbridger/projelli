# Codex Analysis: Keepance Website vs. AdvisorTech Research

**Date:** 2026-06-24  
**Scope:** Independent comparison of Keepance's current public website against the local Kitces/advisor-tech research library.  
**Audience:** Internal strategy. This is not customer-facing copy.  

## Executive Summary

The advisor-specific pages are much closer to the research than the homepage. The `/how-keepance-fits/` and `/fits-your-stack/` pages already say the right core thing: Keepance is not a CRM, planner, custodian, or notetaker. It is the private layer over the stack advisors already use, and it answers household questions with citations. That aligns strongly with the Kitces research.

The main gap is not the product idea. It is emphasis and entry point.

The research says advisors are tired of tech bloat, reward integration above everything else, distrust broad "agentic AI assistant" promises, and buy software only when a demo, peer, or trusted industry source proves it is clearly superior. The website has many of those ideas, but some are buried on secondary pages. The homepage is still attorney-first, the advisor story does not yet lead with the market's actual pain, and the proof is not yet demo-grade enough for a skeptical solo or small RIA.

The single highest-leverage change: **create an advisor-first homepage or landing entry that leads with "make your existing stack answer you," then immediately shows a real pre-review household answer with citations across CRM, plan export, tax file, email, and statement.** That one change would connect the strongest product truth to the strongest research-backed buyer pain.

## Current Website Snapshot

### Homepage

The homepage is still mostly for attorneys.

The hero says: **"The private intelligence layer for your law practice."** The proof points are legal: opposing counsel, indemnity caps, depositions, contradictions, privilege logs, tracked changes in Word, Clio, ABA Formal Opinion 512, and litigation-heavy solos.

The homepage does include an advisor card:

- Financial advisors and RIAs are named as one audience among attorneys, tax pros, and consultants.
- It says client financials, plans, and account details are sensitive.
- It mentions local models, the Advisor Practice pack, fiduciary work, meeting prep, suitability notes, annual-review packets, and a confidential client-data inventory.

But for an advisor arriving cold, the first 80% of the page says "legal product." The advisor message is present, not dominant.

The homepage's strongest reusable assets for advisors:

- "Every answer carries a citation."
- "Your client files stay on your machine."
- "Bring your own AI key, or run local."
- The honest data-flow section.
- The pricing section's BYOK clarity.
- The "your files are always yours" trust story.

### `/how-keepance-fits/`

This is the strongest current advisor strategy page.

It directly says Keepance is not a CRM, planner, custodian, or notetaker. It maps Keepance against Wealthbox/Redtail/Salesforce, RightCapital/eMoney/MoneyGuide, Holistiplan/FP Alpha, Orion/Tamarac/Black Diamond, Schwab/Fidelity/Pershing, Microsoft 365/Copilot, Jump/Zocks, and ChatGPT.

The page already uses the strongest research-backed framing:

- Jump/Zocks are respected as the breakout meeting-AI category, but Keepance owns the client knowledge outside the meeting.
- The Reg S-P vendor-review angle is present.

This page mostly needs sharper proof, not a different strategy.

### `/fits-your-stack/`

This is the cleanest message.

It says:

This page is very aligned with the research. Its weakness is that it is short and still more like a positioning explainer than a purchase-converting proof page.

### `/security/`

This page is a major strength.

It is unusually honest about:

- Email import staying local.
- Encrypted imported messages.

This matches the research's trust gap better than most marketing pages would. It does not overclaim, which matters because advisors and compliance reviewers punish vague AI/security claims.

## Where The Website Already Aligns With What Advisors Reward

### 1. "Not Another Replacement Tool" Is Already Right

The research says advisors run too many tools and are increasingly dissatisfied with tech bloat. They resist switching because migration, retraining, and workflow disruption are painful. Keepance's advisor pages already answer that correctly:

- It does not replace CRM, planning, tax, custody, reporting, or notetaking.
- It reads across the existing stack.
- It asks advisors to switch away from nothing.

Keep and amplify this. It is the core market fit.

### 2. Integration Is The Right Lead Benefit

Kitces says integration is the number-one driver of tech-stack satisfaction. Keepance's "reads across all of it" framing is exactly pointed at that pain.

The current advisor pages correctly name the real stack: Wealthbox, Redtail, Salesforce, RightCapital, eMoney, MoneyGuide, Holistiplan, FP Alpha, Orion, Tamarac, Black Diamond, Schwab, Fidelity, Pershing, Microsoft 365, Jump, and Zocks.

That specificity matters. Advisors need to see their real world, not a generic "documents and apps" claim.

### 3. Back-Office AI, Not Client-Service AI

The research says advisors want AI for prep, document review, drafting, and data gathering. They do not want AI replacing client service or advice judgment.

Keepance's current advisor pages fit this lane:

- Pre-review prep.
- Follow-up drafting.
- Finding missing information.
- Summarizing what changed.
- Answering questions from the client record.

The product is framed as advisor-facing, not client-facing. That is correct.

### 4. Citations Are A Real Trust Wedge

The research repeatedly says citations and verifiability are how advisors start trusting AI. The website already leads with cited answers in several places.

This is not a small feature. It is the buyer's safety mechanism. The site should keep making citations feel like the product's spine, not a feature bullet.

### 5. Privacy Wording Is Strong And Mostly Precise

The site clearly separates:

- Local model: nothing leaves the machine.
- BYOK cloud model: prompts go directly to Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google under the advisor's account, not through Keepance.

That nuance is important. Overclaiming "nothing ever leaves" would break trust. The current security page gets this mostly right.

### 6. Human-In-The-Loop Is Implied Correctly

The website repeatedly says the advisor verifies and decides. That aligns with the research: advisors want AI to expedite work, not autonomously act.

This should be made even more explicit in advisor contexts: Keepance drafts, cites, and prepares. The advisor reviews, edits, and decides.

## Biggest Gaps

### Gap 1: The Homepage Is Still Attorney-First

**Why it matters:** If Keepance is trying to win solo and small RIAs, the first page currently tells many of them "this is not for me." The page navigation includes Advisors, but the hero, examples, proof, pricing comparisons, and founder story are legal-first.

**Research tie:** Advisors buy through demos, peers, blogs/podcasts, and industry research. When a warm referral or Kitces-style mention sends them to the site, the first page has to confirm immediately that Keepance understands advisory work.

**Specific issue:** The homepage has the right universal claims, but the examples are opposing counsel, depositions, privilege logs, Clio, and law-practice pricing comparisons. Advisors have to mentally translate the product into their world.

**Recommendation:** Add an advisor-first route that can serve as the primary page for advisor traffic. It should not feel like a vertical card under a legal product. At minimum:

- Hero: "Make your existing advisor stack answer you."
- Subhead: "Ask across client files, email, planning exports, tax docs, statements, and notes. Get cited answers and drafts without putting client data on a Keepance server."
- Primary proof: one household review-prep example with citations from Outlook, OneDrive, RightCapital, Holistiplan, and Schwab/Tamarac export.
- CTA: "Try it on one household."

### Gap 2: "Tech Bloat" Is Present, But Not Sharp Enough

The site says advisors run a dozen tools and Keepance is not a thirteenth. That is good. But it does not yet fully use the market's emotional truth: advisors are tired of software.

**Research tie:** The Kitces report names tech bloat as the dominant mood. Satisfaction is falling. Advisors feel forced into juggling too many tools.

**What to change:** Lead with the pain before the AI:

- Current feel: "Keepance reads across your stack."
- Stronger feel: "Your stack has the answer. It is just scattered across twelve places."

Recommended section:

> You do not need another place to maintain client records. You need the tools you already pay for to answer one simple question: what do we know about this household, and where is it written down?

Use this idea high on advisor pages.

### Gap 3: Integration Is The #1 Satisfaction Driver, But The Site Does Not Name It As The Strategic Reason To Care

The pages show integrations conceptually, but they do not make the research-backed point: integration is the thing advisors reward most.

**Research tie:** Integration is the top driver of advisor tech-stack satisfaction. Fewer than one-third say data flows automatically across main applications.

**What to change:** Add a "why this matters" proof band:

- "Advisor software is not failing because advisors lack tools. It is failing because the tools do not talk cleanly."
- "Keepance is the private answer layer over the messy middle."

Do not overclaim live API integrations if the product is reading exports/files/email first. Say "reads from folders, email, exports, and connected sources as available."

### Gap 4: The Site Under-Uses Document Review As The #1 Real AI Use Today

Keepance talks about reading documents, but the advisor pages do not strongly say: this is already the AI job advisors are most comfortable with.

**Research tie:** The Kitces report says reviewing client documents is the highest current advisor AI use case, around 20%. The market is also full of document AI: Holistiplan, FP Alpha, TaxStatus, RightCapital Smart Import, Wealth.com, Vanilla, RISR.

**What to change:** Make "review the messy client file before a meeting" the hero demo.

Concrete demo:

- Ask: "What changed for the Smith household since our last annual review?"
- Sources: RightCapital PDF, Holistiplan tax report, Schwab/Tamarac statement, Outlook thread, estate PDF, Jump meeting note.
- Output: cited answer, missing-data list, and draft follow-up email.

This uses the real current AI adoption wedge without claiming Keepance replaces specialist tax/planning tools.

### Gap 5: The Trust Gap Is Not Dramatic Enough On Advisor Pages

The security page is excellent, but the advisor pages could make the trust problem more vivid.

**Research tie:** One AI mistake can mean a lost client or lawsuit. Kitces repeatedly warns that AI must be verified, especially when it affects advice, client communications, or records.

**What to change:** Add a short trust section near the first demo:

- "Every claim has a source."
- "Unsupported facts are labeled as missing, not guessed."
- "Drafts are for advisor review before client use."
- "The audit trail stays with the workspace."

The site should make "I could not find support for that" feel like a feature, because in regulated work a refusal to guess is valuable.

### Gap 6: Switching Inertia Could Be Used More Aggressively

The site says Keepance replaces nothing, but it does not fully exploit the research point that switching is the barrier.

**Research tie:** Advisors do switch, but mostly only for something clearly superior. They resist because the perceived switching cost is high.

**What to change:** Add copy and product proof around "one household first."

Recommended CTA:

> Try Keepance on one household before you change anything else.

Recommended setup proof:

- "Choose one client folder."
- "Connect or import email."
- "Ask one review-prep question."
- "Keep using your CRM, planner, custodian, and notetaker."

This lowers the mental price of adoption.

### Gap 7: The Site Does Not Yet Reflect How Advisors Buy

The current CTAs are "download free trial" and "try it in your browser." Those are useful, but advisor-tech buying is not mostly ad-click self-serve.

**Research tie:** Advisors buy through vendor demos, peers, blogs/podcasts, and industry studies. Cold ads and exhibit halls rank low.

**What to change:** Add buying-path proof:

- "See a household review demo."
- "Bring one anonymized or sample household."
- "Advisor design partner notes."
- "Built with practicing advisors" needs specifics if true. If not yet backed by named proof, keep it modest.

Add a demo page or page section that is built for peer sharing. A skeptical advisor should be able to send it to a partner or compliance consultant and say, "This is what it does."

### Gap 8: The Cross-Source AI Race Is Underplayed

The advisor pages compare against Jump, Copilot, eMoney, and ChatGPT. That is good. But the site does not yet fully explain the category Keepance is entering: the cross-source AI layer is now a funded race.

**Research tie:** Jump, Zocks, Orion, Advisor360, Envestnet, Dispatch, Milemarker, Advyzon, Microsoft, Vega, and others are trying to become the advisor AI home base.

**What to change:** Be honest but sharp:

- "Every tool is adding AI inside its own walls."
- "Keepance is for the facts that live between those walls."

This is safer than "we are the only AI for advisors." It also turns competitor AI into the setup for Keepance's role.

### Gap 9: Standalone "AI Assistant" Language Should Be De-Emphasized

The site mostly avoids this already, but phrases like "private AI that actually knows your clients" can still drift toward the low-trust assistant category if not anchored in documents, citations, and stack fit.

**Research tie:** Agentic AI Assistants have low adoption and below-median importance. Advisors distrust autonomous client-service and advice automation.

**What to change:** Use "private client intelligence" or "cited client-memory layer" more than "AI assistant."

Use "AI" as the engine, not the category.

Better:

- "Private client intelligence for advisors."
- "Cited answers from the client record."
- "The private answer layer over your stack."

Riskier:

- "Your AI associate."
- "Agentic AI for your practice."
- "Autopilot for financial advice."

### Gap 10: Proof Is Still Mostly Conceptual

The current site explains well, but advisors need to see it work on a familiar messy workflow.

**Research tie:** Advisors buy when they believe something is clearly superior, and vendor demos are the top procurement input.

**What to change:** Add one very concrete, repeatable advisor demo:

**Demo name:** Annual Review Prep  
**Question:** "What changed for the Smiths since our last review?"  
**Input sources shown:** CRM note, RightCapital plan export, Holistiplan tax report, Schwab statement, Outlook email thread, OneDrive estate PDF, Jump meeting note.  
**Output:**  

- "What changed" summary.
- "What needs advisor review."
- "Missing or stale information."
- "Draft review agenda."
- "Draft follow-up email."
- Citations beside every factual claim.

This is the fastest way to make the strategy tangible.

## Prioritized Dial-In Recommendations

### Priority 1: Build An Advisor-First Homepage Or Landing Page

**Reason:** The current homepage is legal-first. Advisor traffic needs immediate recognition.

**Action:**

- Create `/advisors/` or make the Advisors nav target a full advisor landing page.
- Lead with tech bloat and stack fit, not generic AI.
- Show one household answer with citations above the fold.
- Use advisor-specific sources and language: household, review meeting, RIA, CRM, planning export, tax report, custodian statement, Outlook, OneDrive.

**Research behind it:** Tech bloat, integration as top satisfaction driver, switching inertia, demo-led buying.

### Priority 2: Make "Not A Thirteenth Tool" The Main Narrative

**Reason:** This is already the site's best line and the research's clearest pain.

**Action:**

- Put it near the top of every advisor page.
- Support it with a simple source map: existing stack → Keepance answer layer → drafts back to existing tools.
- Avoid making Keepance look like a new dashboard to maintain.

**Research behind it:** Advisors use about 12 tools, satisfaction is falling, and the dominant mood is tech bloat.

### Priority 3: Turn Citations Into The Product's Main Proof, Not A Feature Bullet

**Reason:** Citations are the bridge across the trust gap.

**Action:**

- Show claim-level citations in the main demo.
- Include exact source type, date, and excerpt/page when possible.
- Add "I could not find support for..." as a positive trust behavior.

**Research behind it:** Advisors distrust black-box AI. Trusted tools show their work through citations, curated data, or verified sources.

### Priority 4: Reframe Privacy Around Vendor-Risk Reduction

**Reason:** Privacy is not just "nice." It answers Reg S-P/vendor-review anxiety.

**Action:**

- On advisor pages, say: "Reduce the number of vendors holding copies of client data."
- Keep BYOK/local wording precise.
- Link early to the security page's data-flow table.
- Avoid implying compliance is guaranteed.

**Research behind it:** Reg S-P, vendor oversight, Mobile Assistant transcript near-miss, client discomfort with undisclosed AI use.

### Priority 5: Own The Back-Office Prep Workflow

**Reason:** Advisors want AI in prep, review, drafting, and document analysis. Not client-facing automation.

**Action:**

- Name the first three advisor jobs:
  - Annual review prep.
  - Missing-info check.
  - Follow-up drafting from verified facts.
- De-emphasize open-ended "ask anything" until after those jobs are clear.

**Research behind it:** Human-in-the-loop preference, document review as top current AI use, AI should expedite rather than automate.

### Priority 6: Be More Direct About Competitor Boundaries

**Reason:** Advisors will ask, "Doesn't Wealthbox/RightCapital/Jump/Copilot already do this?"

**Action:**

- Add a concise comparison section:
  - CRM AI knows CRM data.
  - Planning AI knows plan data.
  - Tax AI knows tax uploads.
  - Meeting AI knows meetings.
  - Copilot knows Microsoft data.
  - Keepance connects the messy household record across files, email, exports, and tools, with local-first control.

**Research behind it:** AI is now in every stack category. The cross-source layer is a funded race.

### Priority 7: Add A Peer/Demo Buying Path

**Reason:** Advisors buy through demos and peers more than ads.

**Action:**

- Add "See the 5-minute household review demo."
- Build a page that a peer can share.
- Add design-partner proof only if it can be stated honestly.
- Eventually add advisor quotes, even anonymized at first if needed.

**Research behind it:** Vendor demos 49%, peers 43%, blogs/podcasts 33%, industry studies 31%.

## What To De-Emphasize

### De-Emphasize Broad "AI Assistant" Claims

The category is low-importance and distrusted. Use "private client intelligence," "cited answer layer," or "client-memory layer" instead.

### De-Emphasize Automation For Its Own Sake

Do not sound like Keepance autonomously runs the practice. The research says advisors want supervision and review.

### De-Emphasize Tax-Document Reading As A Standalone Wedge

Document reading is important, but tax/planning specialists already own parts of it. Keepance's wedge is cross-source context plus citations, not "we read a tax return."

### De-Emphasize Replacement Language

Any hint that Keepance replaces CRM, planning, custody, tax, or notetaking fights the research. Replacement increases switching fear.

### De-Emphasize Legal Examples On Advisor Entry Paths

The attorney positioning is strong for legal buyers, but it weakens advisor conversion if it is the first thing an advisor sees.

## The Highest-Leverage Change

**Build an advisor-first landing experience around one demo: "Walk into the annual review already knowing the whole household."**

The page should show Keepance answering one realistic question across the exact messy stack:

> "What changed for the Smiths since our last annual review, and what should I bring up?"

The answer should cite:

- A planning export.
- A tax report.
- A custodian or portfolio statement.
- An Outlook email thread.
- A document in OneDrive.
- A CRM or meeting-note export.

Then Keepance should produce:

- A review-prep summary.
- A missing-information list.
- A draft agenda.
- A draft follow-up email.

This one page would tie together every important research point:

- Tech bloat: it works over the stack.
- Integration: it connects scattered evidence.
- Back-office AI: it helps prep, not replace advice.
- Document review: it uses the leading real AI use case.
- Trust: every fact is cited.
- Privacy: the working copy stays local, with BYOK/local-model clarity.
- Switching inertia: it asks the advisor to change nothing.
- Buying behavior: it is demo-ready and peer-shareable.

## Uncertainties And Guardrails

- I did not verify current advisor conversion data, traffic sources, or which page advisors land on first. If most advisor traffic already goes directly to `/fits-your-stack/`, the homepage mismatch is less urgent but still strategically messy.
- I did not verify the current product's exact connector depth. Messaging should avoid implying live API integrations where Keepance currently reads local files, email imports, exports, or manually supplied documents.
- Some competitor features are announced or in beta, not necessarily deeply adopted. The report treats them as market direction and buyer perception, not proof of mature usage.
- Security/compliance language should stay careful. Keepance can reduce vendor data exposure. It should not claim to eliminate compliance duties or guarantee Reg S-P compliance.

## Bottom Line

The website's advisor positioning is directionally right. The issue is that the strongest strategic idea is still split across secondary pages while the main site remains legal-first.

To win in the current advisor-tech landscape, Keepance should sound less like "a private AI app" and more like **the private, cited answer layer that makes the advisor's existing stack usable**.

That is the research-backed opening.
