Notes for Jameson

  1. A write is a stamp you press, never an auto-send. The card sits quietly in the timeline until you open it. One Approve button writes everything at once, and the label counts down as you uncheck rows (“Approve 4 changes” → “Approve 3 changes”). Nothing reaches Wealthbox on its own.
  2. The green is Word’s redline, reused. Each proposed note and task is shown as a tracked-change insertion, the same green-addition language advisors already know from tracked changes in a document. Every task carries an inferred due date and a chip back to the exact transcript moment it came from.
  3. Failures stay attached to the thing that failed. If one task can’t reach Wealthbox, the amber “Retry” lives on that row while the others show a quiet checkmark. No toast, no banner, nothing to chase across the screen. And when a client maps to two Wealthbox households, Approve stays disabled until you pick which one.
