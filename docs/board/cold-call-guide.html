Keepance Cold-Calling Guide

Scripts, discovery questions & objection answers · plan for the week of 2026-06-22

The mindset (read this first). You are NOT selling. You're a curious product designer doing research — warm, relaxed, low pressure. Every call is practice, and every "no" is totally fine. The goal is a real 15–20 minute conversation now, or a booked time this week. Expect mostly voicemail. That's normal. It's a numbers game.

Goal of each call

  1. Reach a solo / small-firm family-law or injury attorney (or get a callback time or email).
  2. Have a short research conversation now, OR book the 20-minute call: cal.com/jamesondaines/research-call
  3. Learn where AI helps, what feels risky, and what's scattered or painful in their workflow.

When to call

  • Best days: Tuesday–Thursday. Monday afternoon is fine to start.
  • Best windows (Mountain Time): 10:00–11:30 AM and 1:30–4:00 PM.
  • Avoid: before 9 AM, 12–1 PM (lunch), after 4:30 PM, and Friday afternoons.
  • Start small: 8–12 dials per session so you stay sharp and take good notes.
Always ask before recording. Utah is one-party-consent (you may record your own calls), but some people may be elsewhere, and lawyers expect to be asked. The moment they agree to talk:
"Quick thing before we dive in — do you mind if I record this just for my own notes? … [if no] No problem at all, I'll just take notes."
If they say no, do not record. Take notes instead.

The scripts

Script A — the attorney answers

"Hi, is this [First name]? … Great. My name's Jameson Daines, I'm a product designer here in Utah. I'll be upfront: I'm not a salesperson and I'm not pitching anything. I'm doing some research on how solo and small-firm attorneys are actually using AI tools like ChatGPT in real casework, and I'm trying to learn from a few people before building too much. Do you have 15 or 20 minutes right now, or could I grab a quick time with you later this week?"

If YES now → ask the recording question, then go to Discovery. If BUSY → "Totally understand. Could I send you a link to grab 15 minutes whenever's easy? cal.com/jamesondaines/research-call — or what's the best email for you?"

Script B — a receptionist or paralegal answers

"Hi, my name's Jameson Daines. I'm doing research on how small firms are using AI in their work — I'm not selling anything. Is [Attorney] the right person to ask about that, and is there a good time to catch them for 15 minutes? … If it's easier, could I grab their direct email or a good callback time?"

Be friendly and brief. Gatekeepers decide whether you get through.

Script C — voicemail (under 25 seconds)

"Hi [First name], this is Jameson Daines, a product designer in Utah. I'm doing some quick research — not selling anything — on how solo attorneys are using AI like ChatGPT in real casework, and I'd really value 15 minutes of your perspective. You can reach me back at [your number], or grab a time at cal.com/jamesondaines/research-call. Thanks so much, and no worries either way."

Leave a voicemail every time. It warms up a later email or callback.

Discovery — what to actually talk about

Let THEM do the talking. Open with: "Please don't share any confidential client details — I only care about your workflow. Made-up examples are perfect."

  1. Have you used ChatGPT, Claude, or legal tools like CoCounsel / Lexis+ / Westlaw in your work?
  2. Walk me through the last time you used AI on a real matter.
  3. What did you deliberately keep OUT of the tool? (confidentiality)
  4. Where do your case facts and documents actually live day to day?
  5. Do you reuse prior briefs, demand letters, clauses, or summaries?
  6. On a case with lots of records or testimony, how do you keep track of what's been said and catch contradictions?
  7. What feels useful but risky about AI?
  8. What would make an AI workspace feel safe enough to use on a real case?
  9. What would be an instant dealbreaker?
  10. Would you ever try a private pilot on a fake or cleaned-up matter?

Close with: "Who else should I be talking to?" (referrals) and "I'll send you a short anonymized summary of what I learn across all these calls."

Pain points to listen for (the gold)

Strong signals real pain

  • "I use ChatGPT but I'm nervous about what I put in."
  • "My prompts / notes / docs are scattered everywhere."
  • "I wish I had a case-specific AI workspace."
  • "I'd try that on a sanitized matter."
  • "How does it handle confidentiality / security?" or "How much would it cost?"

Litigation-specific pains to probe: re-reading huge record sets to find one fact · catching contradictions across depositions / statements · drafting demand letters / briefs from scratch every time · fear of putting client facts into ChatGPT · verifying AI output (made-up cases, hallucinations).

Weak signals politeness, not pain — "Sounds interesting," "AI's the future," "younger lawyers would use that," "just send me something."

Answers to likely questions / objections

"Are you selling something?"
"Honestly, no. I'm a designer trying to understand the real workflow before I build. If something comes of it I'd love to show you later, but today I just want to learn."
"What are you building?"
"I'm the founder of an early product called Keepance — a private AI workspace for law firms. I'm not pitching it today, I'm trying to understand how firms really work first."
"I don't use AI / I'm not techy."
"That's genuinely useful to hear. I'd love to understand why, and what would have to be true for it to ever be worth your time."
"How did you get my number?"
"Your firm's website — it's public. I'm reaching out to a handful of Utah family and injury attorneys."
"I can't discuss my cases."
"Completely understand, and I don't want any client details — just how you work. Made-up examples are perfect."
"I'm not interested."
"No problem at all, I appreciate you picking up. Have a good one."
"Just email me."
"Happy to — what's the best address?" (then send the research email + cal link)
"What do I get out of it?"
"I'll send you a short anonymized summary of what I learn across all these calls — basically a peek at how your peers are handling AI."

After every call

  • Keep the recording / transcript (automatic if you use a calling tool like OpenPhone).
  • Jot the outcome: reached / voicemail / booked / not interested / callback time, plus 1–2 lines of what they said.
  • Each outcome gets logged in the CRM against that contact, and recordings get analyzed for patterns.

Realistic numbers

~10 dials → ~3–4 conversations → ~1–2 real interviews or booked calls. Lots of voicemail. That's the job.
