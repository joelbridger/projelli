01The research

First real target-user session: a practicing XYPN-network RIA advisor (yes, your wife) thinking aloud through onboarding. ~49 minutes, transcribed entirely on-device. This is the foundation everything else is built on.

Her mental model: two gates, in order

1 · "Can we use it?"

Compliance. A hard, binary gate — and it comes first. She'd have quit at the API-key screen. No advisor adopts software solo; it goes through a compliance officer who can be personally sued.

2 · "Is it useful?"

Product value — only evaluated if gate 1 passes. We'd been over-weighting this one. Any tool has to nail both.

The bombshell: we are the answer to her #1 pain

"It's part of my job to protect client information from going into ChatGPT — I don't even use clients' first names… we could benefit from AI so much, but there are these blockers." — and when asked if a private, secure AI would be valuable: "Of course."

She's forbidden from using AI on client data and has no compliant option. Our local-first / nothing-leaves-the-machine / BYOK product is precisely that. She didn't know it was our thesis — she just described our product as the thing she's been missing.

The GTM playbook she handed us

  • XYPN is the master key. The RIA network's recommended-software list is trusted implicitly — "if XYPN recommends it, it'd be shocking if it weren't safe." This is exactly how Jump got in. Top priority.
  • Herd safety. No explicit SEC rule for AI software → approval is a judgment call → "everyone else uses it" is the defense. We need a wall of firm logos.
  • Microsoft-adjacency = instant trust — leans right into our M365 integration.
  • Court the outside compliance consultants (Synergy Compliance / Eric; XYPN's compliance arm). Her advice: "just call and ask what you need."
  • A CCO compliance pack — hand them exactly what unblocks the gate. (Built tonight — section 04.)

04The compliance pack

The thing that unblocks the sale. Eric at Synergy needs a specific packet to greenlight us — we now know exactly what, mapped to the real SEC rules. And our local-first design makes most of it easy to satisfy. Here it is, as graphics.

Where your client data goes (and doesn't)

Three modes a firm can approve

Strongest

Local-only

AI runs on the advisor's machine. No prompt or file ever reaches a cloud provider. No AI vendor receives client information at all.

Common

Direct cloud

The machine sends the prompt straight to the firm's approved AI, using the firm's own key. We're not in the path — the AI provider is the vendor to approve.

Firm tier

Firm Assured

Optional: routes through our zero-retention relay with a firm-managed key. Requires a signed DPA before approval.
