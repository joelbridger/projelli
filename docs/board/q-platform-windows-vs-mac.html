Do our target users use Mac or Windows more?

A research note · 2026-06-20 · multi-source, fact-checked (ABA surveys + corroborating data)

Bottom line

Windows, by a wide margin. Among U.S. solo and small-firm lawyers — exactly our target — roughly 4 out of 5 are on Windows (~81%) and only about 1 in 9 are on Mac (~11%), per the ABA's authoritative survey.

But Mac is concentrated in our slice: solo lawyers are the most Mac-friendly group of all (~16%, about 1 in 6), and Mac use drops as firms get bigger. Mac rose into 2021 but has been flat and choppy since — there's no rising-Mac trend to bet on.

What to do: polish Windows first (it covers ~80% of users), support Mac well as a strong second (1 in 6 solos, plus a loud, influential Mac-lawyer community), and keep Linux as a low-priority long tail. This matches the priorities already in the Testing tab.

The numbers

The gold-standard source is the ABA Legal Technology Survey Report / TechReport (the 2023 Solo & Small Firm edition). It asks lawyers their primary operating system:

Operating systemShare of solo/small-firm lawyers (2023)
Windows (all versions)~81%
— of which Windows 1071%
Mac OS11%
Chrome OS0.2%

Mac is concentrated in our exact target (solos)

Mac usage isn't even across firm sizes — it's highest among solos, which is precisely Keepance's core user. By primary OS = Mac (ABA 2023):

Firm sizeOn Mac
Solo16% (highest of any size)
2–9 attorneys12%
100+ attorneys8%
10–49 attorneys5%

So for our litigation-solo focus, Mac realistically runs around 1 in 6 — small, but not negligible, and worth supporting well.

Is Mac on the rise? Not reliably.

Mac adoption among solos climbed through the mid-2010s and peaked near 21% in 2021 — but it's been volatile, not a steady climb:

  • 2020: 16% → 2021: 21% (peak) → 2022: 8% → 2023: 16%
  • Net over that window: roughly flat. These are small, noisy survey samples.

Implication: you can't bet on Mac overtaking Windows in this segment. Windows is the safe, durable priority.

Independent corroboration. A Florida Bar tech-helpline consultant's ~400 real solo/small-firm interactions broke down ~78% Windows / 16% Mac / 5% mixed — almost exactly matching the ABA survey. (Notably this came from a pro-Mac podcast, which makes the Windows figure more credible, not less.)

A nuance worth knowing

Lawyers love their iPhones (61% of solos use one), and laptops have overtaken desktops as the main work computer. But iPhone affinity does not translate into desktop Mac use — it's a weak signal at best. The desktop split above is what matters for a desktop app like Keepance.

One honest gap. We have no first-party data on what OS our own visitors/users run — our traffic is too low and mostly direct (i.e., largely us testing). Recommendation: turn on operating-system tracking in our analytics so that, as real prospects arrive, we can confirm this industry picture against our actual audience.

What this means for Keepance

  • Windows = priority. ~80% of target users. This is already where the recent testing focus is — keep it there. Getting Windows genuinely solid covers the large majority of the market.
  • Mac = strong second tier, not optional. ~1 in 6 solos, and the Mac-using-lawyer community (MILOFest, "Macs in Law") is small but vocal and influential — good word-of-mouth per Mac user. Support it well; don't lead with it.
  • Linux = low-priority long tail. Keep building it (it's our dev platform anyway), but it's a rounding error among lawyers.
