Showing Keepance to your wife: the session plan

How to run the first demo so it gives real signal · June 25, 2026

The goal (read this twice)

You are NOT trying to sell her. You are trying to learn two things: 1. Would she actually use this every week on her real clients? 2. What would stop her, or her firm, from using it?

And if it goes well, you want one outcome: she becomes user #1 — meaning she puts a real client's files into Keepance and uses it before a real meeting, within about a week.

That's it. A "wow, this is so cool" with no follow-up action is a failure, not a success.

The one trap to avoid: she loves you

Your wife will want to be encouraging. That kindness is the single biggest risk to getting useful information. People are nice about ideas and honest about their own behavior. So the whole technique below is built to get past politeness by asking about what she actually does today, and by getting her to drive it herself on a real client — not just watch you and say nice things.

Say this out loud at the start, and mean it:

"I need you to be brutally honest, even harsh. You won't hurt my feelings. The most valuable thing you can do for me is tell me what's confusing, useless, or annoying. Compliments don't help me — problems do."

Before the session (the prep checklist)

  • [ ] Use the polished build, not the developer build. (The dev version silently breaks the file-reading step. Confirm with the demo-build instance which build to run.)
  • [ ] Disconnect the test email account. There's a real developer mailbox connected to the test setup right now — disconnect it so nothing off-topic appears on screen.
  • [ ] Clear old stuff: remove old questions, old "recent workspaces," and anything from the earlier law-firm version, so the screen looks clean and advisor-native.
  • [ ] Load one believable client. Use the "Hendricks" sample household, OR — much stronger — ask her ahead of time if you can use one of her real clients' files (with her permission). A real client makes the whole thing land.
  • [ ] Run the whole 4-step demo yourself once, start to finish, the day before. Confirm the Client Map builds and the answer shows its sources (those are the parts that broke recently).
  • [ ] Ask her to come with a real, upcoming client meeting in mind. "Pick a client you're meeting soon" gives the demo a real target.

Running the demo (the 4 steps — but watch, don't perform)

Drive the four steps, but after each one, stop and let her react. Watch her face. Note exactly where she leans in, where she looks confused, and what she says unprompted.

  1. Sign in as an advisor, pick a folder + an AI option. (Watch: does the privacy/"stays on your machine" idea land, or does she not care?)
  2. Import the client's pile of files. (Watch: does she recognize this mess as her real life?)
  3. The Client Map builds itself. This is the "wow" moment. (Watch: does she say something like "oh, that would save me time"? Where does her eye go first?)
  4. Ask the files a real pre-meeting question, then click the source. (Watch: does the cited-source/"I can verify it" thing matter to her? Does she trust it?)

The real test: hand her the keyboard

After the guided tour, give her the mouse and keyboard and say: "Now you try — ask it something about this client you'd actually want to know before a meeting."

This is the most important 10 minutes. Watching a real advisor try to use it herself tells you more than an hour of talking. Stay quiet. Let her struggle a little. Note every place she gets stuck.

The questions to ask (and the ones to never ask)

Ask these — they're about her real behavior and the real world, so the answers are trustworthy:

  • "Walk me through how you prep for a client meeting today, step by step."
  • "When was the last time you wasted time hunting for something in a client's file? What happened?"
  • "What's the most annoying part of keeping track of everything you know about a client?"
  • "If you used this for real, whose files would you point it at first?"
  • "What would your compliance person want to know before you could use this?"
  • "What would stop you — or your firm — from actually using it?"
  • "Who else, besides you, would have to say yes?"

Never ask these — they invite polite, useless answers:

  • ❌ "Do you like it?"
  • ❌ "Would you use this?" (hypothetical — people say yes to be nice)
  • ❌ "Would you pay $X for this?" (hypothetical — watch for real commitment instead)

How to read the result

Green flags (it's working): - She asks the price without you bringing it up. - She asks "can I use this on my real clients now?" - She wants to show a specific colleague. - She gets frustrated when something is slow or missing — frustration means she cares. - She follows up the next day, unprompted.

Red flags (be honest with yourself): - Warm, encouraging, but no specific action. - "This is cool!" with no concrete example of when it would've helped her. - She can't name a single real moment from her own work where this would have saved her.

What "she becomes user #1" actually looks like

Don't settle for enthusiasm. The real win is a small, concrete commitment:

Within ~1 week, she imports one real client's files and uses Keepance to prep for one real meeting — then tells you what happened (what worked, what was wrong).

If she does that, you have your first true user. If she won't, that's the most important signal of all, and you want to understand why (too clunky? compliance worry? not actually a real pain?).

Right after the session (don't skip this)

  • Write your notes within the hour, while it's fresh. Use the template below.
  • Pick the single most important thing to fix before the next advisor sees it.
  • Schedule the next touch with her (when she'll try it on a real client).

The path beyond her: her firm

Your wife is also your scout into her firm. Use her to learn: - How does her firm actually buy software? Who decides? Is there a compliance gatekeeper or an "approved vendor list"? - What tools do they already use (CRM, planning, custodian)? Where would Keepance fit, and what would it need to not fight? - Then ask for one warm intro — to a colleague at her firm, or to one advisor friend at another firm. One good intro beats fifty cold emails.

The 60-day scoreboard (pin this somewhere)

Metric Target by ~day 60
Advisors who watched a real demo 5–8
Advisors using it weekly on real clients 3–5
Paying (even a small pilot) 1–2
Warm intros to new advisors 3+

If you hit these, the advisor bet is real and the thrashing era is over. If you don't, you'll know exactly why — cheaply — and that's the whole point.
