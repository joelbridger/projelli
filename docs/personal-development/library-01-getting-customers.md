# Library 1 — Getting Customers

**Priority: highest (tied with The Advisor World).**

This is the big one. You've said it yourself: getting customers — "distribution" — is the
thing that's been hardest, and it's what makes or breaks most startups. The good news is
that it's a learnable skill, not a personality trait. You don't need to become a pushy
salesperson. You need to get genuinely good at two things:

1. **Honest conversations** with potential customers, so you learn what they'll actually pay
   for (not what they politely say they like).
2. **Getting inside the advisor world's "watering holes"** — the small, well-mapped set of
   blogs, communities, and events where financial advisors already gather and decide what
   software to trust.

> **Plain-language terms used below:**
> - **RIA** = Registered Investment Adviser = a financial advisory firm (like your wife's)
>   that's legally registered and must act in the client's best interest.
> - **Distribution** = the whole job of getting your product in front of the right people
>   and getting them to buy.
> - **Customer discovery** = talking to potential customers to learn their real problems
>   *before* you build, so you build the right thing.
> - **Positioning** = deciding, in the customer's mind, what your product *is* and who it's
>   for — so they instantly "get it."

**The one big idea:** you don't win this market with broad advertising. You win it by
becoming a known, trusted name inside *one* advisor community, getting onto the maps
advisors already use to shop for software, and earning a few reference customers who vouch
for you. Everything below serves that.

## (A) Essentials — do these first, in this order

1. **The Mom Test** — Rob Fitzpatrick. *Book, ~$15.* [momtestbook.com](https://www.momtestbook.com/)
   A short (130-page) manual on how to talk to potential customers so they tell you the
   *truth* instead of being nice. This is your single highest-leverage read, because you have
   a live design partner (your wife's firm) and your #1 risk is building the wrong thing.

2. **Get inside the advisor world via Kitces.** *Free.*
   [Nerd's Eye View blog](https://www.kitces.com/blog/category/20-financial-advisor-marketing/) ·
   [Kitces AdvisorTech Map](https://www.kitces.com/fintechmap/) · [Directory](https://fintech.kitces.com/)
   Michael Kitces is the most influential voice advisors read (200k+ monthly readers), and his
   "AdvisorTech Map" (380+ tools) is literally how advisors discover software. Study where
   Keepance would slot, then make getting listed there a goal.

3. **Obviously Awesome** — April Dunford. *Book, ~$20.* [aprildunford.com/books](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)
   A step-by-step process for **positioning**. Your phrase "private client intelligence layer"
   is evocative, but advisors won't recognize it as a category yet. This book fixes exactly that.

4. **Founding Sales** — Pete Kazanjy. *Free online; print ~$50.* [foundingsales.com](https://www.foundingsales.com/)
   The definitive handbook for a *founder who is not a salesperson* learning to sell business
   software for the first time. Free, complete, and written for precisely your situation.

5. **Apply to the XYPN LIVE AdvisorTech Expo (the FinTech competition).** *Application low-cost.*
   [XYPN LIVE](https://info.xyplanningnetwork.com/xypn-live-fintech)
   A speed-pitch competition built specifically for early advisor-tech startups (you qualify:
   launched within 12 months, or under $1M revenue). The most concrete fast path to put
   Keepance in front of hundreds of next-generation RIAs at once.

## (B) The full library, by sub-topic

### 1. Customer discovery — talking to advisors and learning what they need
- **The Mom Test** — Rob Fitzpatrick. *Book, ~$15.* [link](https://www.momtestbook.com/)
  The foundation for every conversation: ask about their real life and past behavior, never
  pitch your idea.
- **Talking to Humans** — Giff Constable (foreword by Steve Blank). *Free PDF.* [talkingtohumans.com](https://www.talkingtohumans.com/)
  The practical "how-to" companion: how to find people to interview, how many, and how to turn
  what you hear into decisions.
- **"How to Talk to Users"** — Eric Migicovsky, Y Combinator. *Free 30-min talk.* [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-1-recap-kevin-hale-and-eric-migicovsky/)
  The exact questions to ask (and the hypotheticals to never ask), from Pebble's founder.
- **Deploy Empathy** — Michele Hansen. *Book, ~$10-30.* [deployempathy.com](https://deployempathy.com/)
  The best follow-up to *The Mom Test*: it hands you exact phrases for getting advisors to open
  up about their messy real workflows. (Independently flagged by both research passes.)
- **Continuous Discovery Habits** — Teresa Torres. *Book, ~$20.* [link](https://www.producttalk.org/2021/05/continuous-discovery-habits/)
  Especially good for a designer: it turns "talk to customers" into a small *weekly habit* so you
  keep learning from advisors instead of guessing — fits your weekend rhythm.
- **What Customers Want** (or the shorter *Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice*) — Tony Ulwick.
  *Book ~$30; free framework at* [strategyn.com](https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/)
  The "Jobs to be Done" lens: focus on the *outcome* an advisor wants ("never get caught
  flat-footed by a client question"), not features. Helps turn interview notes into a roadmap.

### 2. Positioning & messaging — making advisors instantly "get" Keepance
- **Obviously Awesome** — April Dunford. *Book, ~$20.* [link](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)
  The 10-step process and Positioning Canvas to choose Keepance's category, best-fit buyer,
  and true differentiator.
- **Sales Pitch** — April Dunford. *Book, ~$20.* [link](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)
  The follow-up: an 8-step structure for the actual sales conversation, including the insight
  that your biggest competitor is often the advisor choosing to "do nothing."

### 3. B2B / SaaS sales fundamentals — for someone who is not a natural salesperson
- **Founding Sales** — Pete Kazanjy. *Free online.* [foundingsales.com](https://www.foundingsales.com/)
  Full early go-to-market handbook for first-time founder-sellers. Used in Wharton's curriculum.
- **Gap Selling** — Keenan. *Book, ~$25.* [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Gap-Selling-Problem-Centric-Everything-Relationships/dp/1732891001)
  A "problem-centric" method that suits a designer's instincts: diagnose the advisor's broken
  current state, and the sale becomes obvious. Selling by helping, not pushing.
- **YC's free founder-sales modules** — Y Combinator. *Free videos.*
  [The Sales Playbook for Founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Mo-the-sales-playbook-for-founders) ·
  [Enterprise Sales for Founders](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/LF-enterprise-sales-for-founders)
  Short, free weekend modules on running early pilots and closing your first customers — handy when
  several people at a firm (advisor, owner, compliance) all influence the buy.

### 4. Pricing basics
- **The Pricing Roadmap** — Ulrik Lehrskov-Schmidt. *Book, ~$25.* [willingnesstopay.com](https://www.willingnesstopay.com/product/the-pricing-roadmap)
  A better first pricing read than the classics here because it's specifically about *B2B SaaS*
  pricing — pilots, tiers, and willingness-to-pay — which is exactly your situation.
- **Monetizing Innovation** — Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke. *Book, ~$30.* [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Monetizing-Innovation-Companies-Design-Product/dp/1119240867)
  Ask about willingness-to-pay *during* discovery, before you finish building. Nine concrete
  rules from a firm that has run 10,000+ pricing projects.
- **OpenView SaaS Pricing Guide for early startups.** *Free.* [openviewpartners.com/saas-pricing](https://openviewpartners.com/saas-pricing/)
  Practical enough to make your first pricing decision without hiring a consultant.

### 5. How to reach and win financial advisors specifically (the real doors)
This is where generic startup advice fails. The advisor market is small and clubby. These are
the actual entry points.

**The maps & influencer — start here:**
- **Kitces Nerd's Eye View + AdvisorTech Map.** *Free.*
  [Marketing blog](https://www.kitces.com/blog/category/20-financial-advisor-marketing/) ·
  [FinTech Map](https://www.kitces.com/fintechmap/) · [Directory](https://fintech.kitces.com/)
  The map is how advisors shop for tools; the blog is what they read. Getting Keepance accurately
  categorized (and eventually reviewed) here is a top distribution goal. The marketing section is
  also a free course in how advisors get *their* clients — so you learn their language.

**Communities to embed in (where next-generation RIAs gather):**
- **XY Planning Network (XYPN).** *Free blog/podcast; paid membership for advisors.* [xyplanningnetwork.com](https://www.xyplanningnetwork.com/)
  The leading network for small, fee-only, independent RIAs — firms exactly like your wife's.
  They curate a recommended member tech stack; getting Keepance adopted or discounted into that
  stack is a real distribution channel.
- **NAPFA** ([napfa.org](https://www.napfa.org)) and the **FPA** (Financial Planning Association).
  The two large professional bodies; their regional study groups and conferences are where
  word-of-mouth is built.
- **r/CFP on Reddit.** *Free.* [reddit.com/r/CFP](https://www.reddit.com/r/CFP/)
  Where working advisors candidly discuss tools and gripes. A free, ongoing customer-discovery
  feed — read it regularly.

**Prospecting & research tools (to find real advisors to talk to):**
- **XYPN "Find an Advisor"** ([directory](https://connect.xyplanningnetwork.com/find-an-advisor)) and
  **NAPFA "Find an Advisor"** ([directory](https://www.napfa.org/find-an-advisor)). *Free.*
  The best manual sources of small, fee-only, independent advisors — the firms that look most like
  your likely first buyers. Use them to build your interview list beyond your wife's network.
- **SEC adviser database (Form ADV / IAPD).** *Free.* [adviserinfo.sec.gov](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/)
  Look up any firm's size, registration, and profile before you reach out, so you spend time on the
  right ones.
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator.** *~$120/month.* [link](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans)
  Worth one paid month to build a tight list of RIA owners and operations leads for outreach. (See
  the outreach exercise note below — pair it with language from your real interviews.)
- **T3 / Inside Information Advisor Software Survey.** *Free PDF, published yearly.* [t3technologyhub.com](https://t3technologyhub.com/)
  Shows what software advisors actually use and rate highly — so you're not selling in a vacuum and
  you know your real competitors.

**Conferences (highest-density way to meet advisors and tech buyers):**
- **XYPN LIVE — AdvisorTech Expo & FinTech Competition.** *Apply: low cost.* [link](https://info.xyplanningnetwork.com/xypn-live-fintech)
  Built for early advisor-tech startups; you qualify. **Your single best near-term event.**
- **T3 (Technology Tools for Today) Advisor Conference.** *Vendor registration paid.* [t3conferences.com](https://t3conferences.com/)
  The largest event focused purely on advisor technology — a trade show where firms demo tools.
- **Future Proof Festival** and others — see Kitces' annually-updated, ranked guide:
  ["The Best Financial Advisor Conferences To Attend in 2026"](https://www.kitces.com/blog/2026-best-financial-advisor-conferences-list-fpa-futureproof-sway-rts-heckerling-t3/). *Free.*
  Use it to pick 1-2 events a year by audience fit, not hype.

**Partnership/distribution angle to keep in mind:** winning advisor-tech startups rarely replace
an advisor's whole system — they *integrate* into it (CRMs like Wealthbox/Redtail, custodians/
platforms like Altruist, Schwab, Orion). As Keepance matures, an integration or a custodian/
network partnership can become a bigger distribution lever than any ad. Note it now; act on it
after you have a few happy reference firms.

**General distribution sharpening (optional, not advisor-specific):**
- **Lenny's Newsletter.** *Free tier + paid ~$150/yr.* [lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/)
  The best single source on product, growth, and go-to-market for software founders. Always
  translate its advice through the advisor-specific channels above.

### What to SKIP right now (just as important)
Both research passes independently flagged the same traps. Until you have a handful of paying or
clearly-committed advisors, do **not**:
- **Pay for ads.** Too early, too little proof — you'd be paying to amplify a message you haven't
  validated yet.
- **Buy a conference booth.** An XYPN LIVE booth runs ~$14,000, and big-festival passes run
  hundreds to a few thousand dollars. Great channels *later*; a bad first move now. Go as an
  attendee to learn, not as an exhibitor to sell.
- **Chase "growth hacking" content.** It feels productive but dodges the hard, slow work of real
  conversations.
- **Build more features before ~10 advisor conversations.** Your bottleneck is trust and
  distribution, not another workflow template.
