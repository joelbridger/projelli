# Library 3 — Founder & Startup Craft

**Priority: third** (after Getting Customers and The Advisor World).

This is the "how to actually run a company" toolkit for someone who is a brilliant
product designer but new to the business side. The goal here is not to turn you into
a finance person. It is to give you three things designers usually lack:

1. **A method** for finding real demand before you over-build (so you don't pour
   months into something nobody will pay for).
2. **Positioning and story** (turning "a private client intelligence layer" into a
   sharp sentence an advisor instantly understands).
3. **Pricing sense** (for a one-person business, the price you pick is the difference
   between a hobby and a real company).

> **Plain-language note on a few terms you'll see below:**
> - **Product-market fit** = the moment you have something a real group of people
>   clearly wants and keeps pulling out of your hands. The whole early game is finding it.
> - **MVP (minimum viable product)** = the smallest version you can build that
>   actually tests whether people want the thing.
> - **SaaS** = software people pay for monthly or yearly (like Netflix, but for a tool).
> - **Bootstrapping** = funding the company from your own money and early sales,
>   instead of raising money from investors.

## (A) Essentials — the 5 highest-impact, in order

1. **Y Combinator Startup School** — *free online course.* [startupschool.org](https://www.startupschool.org/)
   The single best modern, free, structured on-ramp. It covers the whole job in one
   place (finding the idea, talking to users, pricing, a sober take on raising money,
   growth), taught by the people who funded Stripe and Airbnb. It's organized into
   modules, which suits your deep-focus weekends perfectly. **This is your "business
   school in a weekend or two."**

2. **The Lean Startup** — Eric Ries. *Book, ~$15.* [theleanstartup.com](https://theleanstartup.com/)
   Gives you the core mental model: build the smallest thing that teaches you
   something, measure, learn, repeat. For a designer whose instinct is "make it
   polished," this reframes the job to "make it *learn* first" — the exact discipline
   a feature-rich, pre-launch product needs.

3. **Obviously Awesome** — April Dunford. *Book, ~$20.* [aprildunford.com/books](https://www.aprildunford.com/books)
   The best practical book on **positioning** (deciding what category you're in and who
   you beat). This directly answers your hardest open question — *what is Advisor Prep Hero to an
   advisor, and what does it replace?* (Also appears in the Getting Customers library —
   that's how important it is.)

4. **Zero to One** — Peter Thiel with Blake Masters. *Book, ~$15.* [Publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/234730/zero-to-one-by-peter-thiel-with-blake-masters/)
   The best short read on *strategy* — why a company deserves to exist, and why you
   should own a small market completely before expanding. Helps you choose the narrow
   slice of advisors to dominate first instead of trying to serve everyone.

5. **Paul Graham's essays** — *free.* [paulgraham.com/articles.html](https://paulgraham.com/articles.html)
   The founder-mindset canon, and ideal deep-focus weekend reading. Start with these four:
   [How to Get Startup Ideas](https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html) ·
   [Do Things that Don't Scale](https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html) ·
   [Startup = Growth](https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html) ·
   [How to Start a Startup](https://www.paulgraham.com/start.html).
   *"Do Things that Don't Scale"* is the one to read first: it's your permission slip to
   win your first advisors by hand, one personal relationship at a time.

## (B) The broader library, by sub-topic

### 1. The startup method — how startups actually find what works
- **"The only thing that matters"** — Marc Andreessen. *Free essay.* [pmarchive.com](https://pmarchive.com/guide_to_startups_part4.html)
  The clearest explanation of product-market fit and why being in a real market beats a
  clever product. Sets your north star for the next 12 months.
- **The Startup Owner's Manual** — Steve Blank & Bob Dorf. *Book, ~$30-40.* [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Four-Steps-Epiphany-Steve-Blank/dp/0989200507)
  The thick operating manual for "customer development" (searching for your business
  model before scaling). Use it as a reference/checklist, not a weekend read.

### 2. The foundational canon (only the genuinely high-signal ones)
- **How to Start a Startup (Stanford CS183B)** — Sam Altman & guests. *Free, 20 video lectures.* [startupclass.samaltman.com](https://startupclass.samaltman.com/)
  A free "one-class business school" with lectures from Altman, Thiel, and Andreessen.
  Great deep-focus video for weekends; pairs with YC Startup School.
- **Zero to One** — see Essentials #4.
- **Paul Graham essays** — see Essentials #5.
- **Lenny's Newsletter** — Lenny Rachitsky. *Free tier; paid ~$200/yr.* [lennysnewsletter.com](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/about)
  The best current practitioner writing on product and growth. A steady drip once you're
  past the foundational books.

### 3. Pricing and the few numbers that matter
- **Monetizing Innovation** — Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke. *Book, ~$25-30.* [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Monetizing-Innovation-Companies-Design-Product/dp/1119240867)
  The best book on pricing a *new* product: decide what people will pay *before* you
  finish building, and shape the product around it. You're pre-launch — perfect timing.
- **"Why Your Customers Would Be Happier If You Charged More"** — Patrick McKenzie. *Free essay.* [kalzumeus.com](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2012/09/21/ramit-sethi-and-patrick-mckenzie-on-why-your-customers-would-be-happier-if-you-charged-more/)
  The antidote to the #1 first-timer mistake: charging far too little. Advisors are a
  high-value buyer who won't respect a $9/month tool.
- **SaaS Metrics 2.0** — David Skok. *Free guide.* [forentrepreneurs.com](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/saas-metrics-2/)
  Plain-language definitions of the handful of numbers that decide a subscription
  business: cost to win a customer, lifetime value, churn (cancellations), recurring
  revenue. Skim now to learn the vocabulary; you'll use it later.

### 4. Raising money vs. funding it yourself
- **The SaaS Playbook** — Rob Walling. *Book, ~$25.* [saasplaybook.com](https://saasplaybook.com/)
  The clearest how-to for building a profitable software business *without* investors —
  the most realistic path for a solo, niche, compliance-focused product like Advisor Prep Hero.
- **Venture Deals** — Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson. *Book, ~$25-30.* [venturedeals.com](https://venturedeals.com/the-book/)
  If you ever consider raising money, this teaches the language of the deal so you're not
  negotiating blind. Read it *only if/when* you decide to fundraise.
- **My honest take for Advisor Prep Hero:** default to bootstrapping. A solo designer, a niche
  advisor market, and a trust/compliance-sensitive sale all point toward a focused,
  profitable, self-funded business — not the swing-for-the-fences bet that investor
  money requires. Read *The SaaS Playbook* first; keep *Venture Deals* "in case."

### 5. Storytelling — how to explain the company
- **"The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen"** — Andy Raskin. *Free essay.* [Medium](https://medium.com/the-mission/the-greatest-sales-deck-ive-ever-seen-4f4ef3391ba0)
  A 5-part template (name the big shift in the world → winners vs. losers → the promised
  land → your product as the bridge → proof) — the best framework for explaining *why
  Advisor Prep Hero, why now*. Your design skills make you unusually able to execute this.
- **Building a StoryBrand** — Donald Miller. *Book, ~$20.*
  "Make the customer the hero, your product the guide." Useful for website and onboarding
  copy once your positioning is set. Optional — Dunford + Raskin are the higher-signal pair.

### 6. Operating as a solo founder
- **The Minimalist Entrepreneur** — Sahil Lavingia (founder of Gumroad). *Book, ~$20.* [Publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/652764/the-minimalist-entrepreneur-by-sahil--lavingia/)
  A manifesto for building a great *small* company: start small, charge early, stay
  sustainable. Matches your reality as a mostly-solo designer-founder.
- **Zero to Sold** — Arvid Kahl. *Free sample; ~$25-30 full.* [zerotosold.com](https://zerotosold.com/)
  The most complete solo/bootstrapped operating manual — what to actually *do* day to day,
  from idea to selling the business — by a founder who built a niche B2B tool much like yours.
