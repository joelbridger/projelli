# Library 2 — The Financial Advisor / RIA World

**Priority: highest (tied with Getting Customers).**

To build and sell Advisor Prep Hero well, you need to understand your customer's world almost as well
as they do. The faster you do that, the more Advisor Prep Hero will feel — to an advisor — like it was
built by someone who *gets it*.

> **Plain-language terms used below:**
> - **RIA** = Registered Investment Adviser = a financial advisory firm that's legally
>   registered and owes its clients a "fiduciary duty" (the highest legal standard: act in the
>   client's best interest).
> - **AUM** = "assets under management" = the total pile of client money a firm looks after.
>   Most firms charge about **1% of that per year** as their fee. So their income, budget, and
>   priorities all flow from AUM.
> - **Custodian** = the big institution (Schwab, Fidelity) that actually *holds* the client's
>   money. The advisor manages it but can't hold it themselves.
> - **Compliance** = following the financial regulators' rules. For a document product like
>   Advisor Prep Hero, this is the whole ballgame (see below).

**The one big idea:** an RIA is a small business that lives or dies on *trust* and *records*.
Two facts should anchor everything you build and say:
1. Advisors are **legally required to keep complete, findable records** of client
   communications and advice for at least five years (the SEC "books-and-records" rule).
2. As of 2024-2026 they are **newly required to protect client data**, watch for breaches, and
   notify clients when data leaks (the updated "Reg S-P" rule).

Advisor Prep Hero sells into the exact gap those two rules create: advisors are drowning in scattered
documents and emails they're *obligated* to keep, organize, and protect — but their existing
tools were never built to be the firm's organized, compliance-aware memory. If you understand
the advisor's day, their software, and these two rules, you can position Advisor Prep Hero not as "nice
document software" but as "the thing that keeps you organized *and* keeps the regulator happy."

   Nothing teaches you faster than watching one real advisor's actual workday and mapping where
   their files, emails, and "where did I put that?" pain live. (Details in `exercises.md`,

   firms, and choose software. His tech map shows the whole landscape and the exact "document

3. **Read the two regulations that justify Advisor Prep Hero — the actual text.** *Free.*
   - **Books-and-Records Rule (Advisers Act Rule 204-2)** — firms must make and keep findable
     records of advice and client communications, generally 5+ years.
     [Rule text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.204-2)
   - **Regulation S-P, 2024 amendments** — now requires written data-safeguard policies, an
     incident-response plan, and client breach notification. Smaller firms must comply by
     **June 3, 2026** (i.e., right now). [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2024/06/s7-05-23) ·
     [Plain-English FINRA summary](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/cybersecurity-advisory-sec-amends-regulation-s-p)
   Quoting these correctly is the difference between sounding like a designer and sounding like
   someone advisors trust with their records.

4. **Subscribe to Bob Veres' Inside Information.** *Paid, ~a few hundred $/yr.* [bobveres.com](https://bobveres.com/)
   Veres is the profession's sharpest independent insider. His service is the deep, opinionated
   read on where advisory firms are heading and what tools win — in the language of firm owners,
   who are exactly your buyer.

5. **Attend ONE big advisor conference this year — to immerse and to sell.** *Paid.*

## (B) The broader library, by sub-topic

### 1. How RIAs operate and make money
- **Kitces, "How Financial Advisors Actually Charge For Their Services."** *Free article.* [link](https://www.kitces.com/blog/financial-advisors-charge-services-fee-structure-advisory-firm-profession-aum-pricing-insight/)
  The clearest explainer of the dominant AUM model (~1% of assets per year). Knowing your
  customer earns a slice of AUM tells you their budget and their priorities: keep clients,
  work efficiently, avoid compliance blowups.
- **Practice Made (More) Perfect** — Mark Tibergien & Rebecca Pomering. *Book, ~$30.* [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Practice-Made-More-Perfect-Transforming/dp/1118019313)
  The standard text on how an advisory *firm* runs as a business: staffing, workflow,
  profitability, scaling. Shows the operational pains ("tribal knowledge," capacity, handoffs)
  that Advisor Prep Hero's "knowledge layer" can relieve.
- **Form ADV via the SEC's adviser lookup.** *Free database.* [adviserinfo.sec.gov](https://adviserinfo.sec.gov/)
  Every RIA publicly files a "Form ADV" describing its business, clients, fees, and conflicts.
  A free X-ray of any prospect (including your wife's firm) before you pitch.

### 2. Their software stack (where Advisor Prep Hero must fit)
- **The Kitces AdvisorTech Map + Directory.** *Free.* [Map](https://www.kitces.com/fintechmap/) · [Directory](https://fintech.kitces.com/)
  The whole landscape; "document management" sits in *operations* alongside CRM and e-signature.
  Shows which box Advisor Prep Hero competes in and which tools it must integrate with, not replace.
- **CRMs — Wealthbox & Redtail.** *~$45-80/user/month.* [Comparison](https://www.wealthbox.com/compare/redtail-crm-vs-wealthbox/)
  These two hold ~55% of the independent-RIA CRM market. The CRM is the system advisors live in;
  Advisor Prep Hero must complement it, and integrating with these is table stakes.
- **Custodians — Schwab Advisor Services & Fidelity Institutional.** *No direct fee to the RIA.*
  [Schwab Advisor Services](https://advisorservices.schwab.com/)
  A custodian holds the client's money and pushes a lot of statements and forms to the firm —
  a huge share of the documents Advisor Prep Hero would organize originate here.
- **Financial planning software — RightCapital, eMoney, MoneyGuidePro.** *~$100-250/mo per advisor.*
  These build the client's actual financial *plan*. Plans and their supporting docs are core
  "client intelligence" — understand what these produce so Advisor Prep Hero can organize, not duplicate.
- **Document management — the incumbents you're up against.**
  [InvestmentNews roundup](https://www.investmentnews.com/goria/technology/top-financial-document-management-software-solutions-for-rias/262260)
  Most RIAs today store files in SharePoint/OneDrive, Egnyte, or a CRM's built-in storage —
  usually a messy folder tree, not "intelligence." That status quo is what Advisor Prep Hero must visibly beat.
- **Docupace.** *Contact sales for pricing.* [docupace.com](https://www.docupace.com/)
  The tool living closest to Advisor Prep Hero's lane: document workflows and back-office processing for
  advisory firms. Worth studying closely — it's the clearest picture of who you'll be compared to.
- **Portfolio & reporting suites — Orion, Envestnet Tamarac, Black Diamond.** *Contact sales.*
  [Tamarac](https://www.envestnet.com/tamarac)
  These run the investment-operations layer (trading, reporting, billing). You don't compete here,
  but a lot of the documents and data Advisor Prep Hero organizes pass through them.

### 3. The regulatory / compliance landscape (the heart of the pitch)
- **Fiduciary duty basics.** RIAs register with the SEC (larger firms) or their state and owe a
  fiduciary duty — the highest standard. Background via [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/form-adv).
  "Fiduciary" is the emotional core of an advisor's identity; speak to it.
- **Books-and-Records: Advisers Act Rule 204-2.** *Free.* [Rule text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/17/275.204-2) ·
  [Plain-English overview](https://www.smarsh.com/regulations/investment-advisers-act-of-1940-rule-204-2/)
  Requires creating and *findably* keeping records of advice and client communications, generally
  5 years. This is Advisor Prep Hero's reason to exist — frame the product as "the rule, handled."
- **Regulation S-P (2024 amendments).** *Free.* [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2024/06/s7-05-23) ·
  [FINRA summary](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/cybersecurity-advisory-sec-amends-regulation-s-p)
  New written safeguard policies, a breach response program, vendor oversight, and client
  notification — smaller-firm deadline **June 3, 2026.** The freshest, most urgent compliance pain.
- **SEC Reg S-P "Small Entity Compliance Guide."** *Free PDF.* [sec.gov](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/regulation-s-p-small-entity-compliance-guide.pdf)
  The plain-English version of the rule above, aimed at small firms. Read this *before* the dense
  legal text — it's the fastest way to understand what a small RIA actually has to do.
- **SEC Examination Priorities (published yearly).** *Free PDF.* [sec.gov/exams](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-examinations)
  A short annual document telling you what regulators are focused on right now (fiduciary duty,
  conflicts, cybersecurity, AI, Reg S-P). It's a cheat sheet for the fears your buyer feels this year.
- **Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) + Form CRS.** *Free.* [FINRA overview](https://www.finra.org/article/regulation-best-interest-(reg-bi)-overview)
  Useful nuance: Reg BI's standard governs *broker-dealers*, not pure RIAs (held to the higher
  fiduciary duty); Form CRS is a short relationship-summary both must give clients. Many real
  firms are "hybrid" (both), so you'll meet Reg BI in the wild — getting this right signals credibility.
- **SEC Rule 17a-4 (broker-dealer electronic recordkeeping), 2022 amendments.** *Free.* [SEC summary](https://www.sec.gov/investment/amendments-electronic-recordkeeping-requirements-broker-dealers)
  The broker-dealer cousin of 204-2; relevant if Advisor Prep Hero ever touches hybrid firms.
- **Cybersecurity expectations.** Reg S-P's safeguards rule is now the practical baseline.
  "Secure by design" is a buying criterion for anything holding client data, not a nice-to-have.

### One thing to keep in mind (the skeptical take)
You do **not** need to become a financial advisor. Skip the certifications, the CFA materials, and
the thick finance textbooks — that's wasted time. What you need is to understand the advisor's
*operating system*: how their day works, where their information lives, and what the regulator
makes them do. A clean test for whether any resource above is worth your weekend:

> When an advisor asks, *"What do I know about this household, what did I promise, what changed,
> what documents prove it, and what do I need to do next?"* — Advisor Prep Hero should answer instantly,
> with the proof attached, and leave a clean record behind.

That sentence is the whole product. Judge everything you read and build by whether it helps you
deliver it.
