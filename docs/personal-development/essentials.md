# Start Here — The Essentials

Open this on a Saturday morning. It's the short, ranked list of the few things that beat
everything else, in the order I'd actually do them. The four big libraries (one per focus area)
are there when you want to go deeper — but this page is the spine.

---

## The single most important idea

Your bottleneck is **getting customers**, and the fastest fix is not more reading — it's **real
conversations with advisors, then a sharp story, then a direct ask for a paid pilot.** Reading
makes you *better* at those moves. The moves themselves are what move Keepance forward.

Two separate research passes — my own researchers and a fully independent second AI (Codex) —
landed on the *same* path without talking to each other. That's a good sign it's right:

> **Talk to advisors → nail the story → learn founder-led selling → map the advisor channels →
> ask for a paid pilot.**

---

## If you do nothing else — the top 3 (all hands-on)

These three, done well, would put you ahead of most founders. Everything else just supports them.
Full instructions are in `exercises.md`.

1. **Shadow your wife's firm and map how documents and compliance actually work** (Exercises 1-2).
   This is your product spec and your first customer conversation in one.
2. **Run a 10-advisor discovery sprint** (Exercise 3) — read *The Mom Test* first.
3. **Make the paid design-partner ask** (Exercise 6) — the only honest proof you're building
   something sellable.

## The essential reading & courses, in priority order

### Priority 1 — Getting customers *(tied for most important)*
1. **The Mom Test** — Rob Fitzpatrick *(book, ~$15-20)* — how to get the truth from advisors.
2. **Obviously Awesome** (and its follow-up **Sales Pitch**) — April Dunford *(books, ~$20 each)* —
   make advisors instantly "get" what Keepance is.
3. **Founding Sales** — Pete Kazanjy *(free online)* — selling for a founder who isn't a salesperson.
4. **Live inside Kitces** *(free)* — the blog advisors read + the AdvisorTech map they shop from.
5. **Apply to the XYPN LIVE FinTech competition** *(low cost)* — pitch to a room full of small RIAs.

### Priority 1 — The advisor world *(tied for most important)*
1. **Immerse at your wife's firm** — the shadow + mapping exercises above.
2. **Live inside Kitces** *(free)* — same source, now for understanding their world.
3. **Read the two rules Keepance exists for** *(free)* — Books-and-Records (Rule 204-2) and Reg S-P;
   start with the plain-English **Reg S-P Small Entity Compliance Guide**.
4. **Bob Veres** *(paid)* — start with his short *History of the Financial Planning Profession* book;
   subscribe to *Inside Information* if you want the ongoing insider read.
5. **Attend one advisor conference this year** *(paid)* — Wealth Management EDGE or Future Proof.

### Priority 2 — Founder & startup craft
1. **Y Combinator Startup School** *(free course)* — your "business school in a weekend or two."
2. **The Lean Startup** — Eric Ries *(book, ~$15)* — build to *learn* first, not to polish.
3. **Zero to One** — Peter Thiel *(book, ~$15)* — own a small market completely before expanding.
4. **Paul Graham's essays** *(free)* — start with *"Do Things that Don't Scale."*

### Priority 3 — Focus, energy & mindset
1. **Deep Work** — Cal Newport *(book, ~$15)* — turn weekend blocks into real focus.
2. **Indistractable** — Nir Eyal *(book, ~$15)* — the direct fix for the YouTube pull.
3. **Atomic Habits** — James Clear *(book, ~$15)* — keep showing up between the big blocks.
4. **Set your weekly weekend rhythm** (Exercise 7) — so the time actually gets used.

## A suggested first six weekends

A concrete plan so you always know what to open. It front-loads your two top priorities
(customers + the advisor world), because those are where the leverage is.

| Weekend | Do this |
|---|---|
| **1** | Read *The Mom Test* (it's short) and skim Kitces' "Start Here" + the AdvisorTech map. Set up your weekly rhythm (Exercise 7). |
| **2** | Shadow your wife's firm; do the document/compliance map and software inventory (Exercises 1-2). |
| **3** | Read *Obviously Awesome*; draft Keepance's positioning and one-page story (Exercise 4). |
| **4** | Read the Reg S-P Small Entity guide and skim the Books-and-Records rule; build your interview list from the XYPN/NAPFA directories. |
| **5** | Run the first 5 discovery interviews (Exercise 3). Read *Founding Sales* alongside. |
| **6** | Finish interviews; do the reverse-demo test (Exercise 5) and make the paid design-partner ask to your 3 warmest advisors (Exercise 6). |

After that: keep talking to advisors every couple of weekends, and let *YC Startup School* and the
founder-craft reading fill the blocks in between.

---

## A note on money and time

Most of the highest-value material here is **free or about $15-25 a book**. Buying every essential
book would cost roughly what a few months of streaming services do. The expensive moves — conference
booths, ads, big exhibit spaces — should wait until you have paying advisors. (See the "What to skip
right now" note at the bottom of the Getting Customers library.)

The point isn't to read all of it. It's to do the top 3 hands-on moves, read the few essentials that
make you better at them, and keep a steady rhythm. Trade some of the aimless YouTube for this, and in
a few months you'll know far more about your customer — and be far closer to your first paying one —
than almost any founder at your stage.
