## The core idea

The page is trying to sell too many things at once. Make one idea unavoidable:

> **Keepance gives high-trust professionals a private AI memory for every client.**

Lead with the **Client Map** as the product's main mental model, not "AI features." Move detailed legal/tax/compliance explanations to the persona pages. On the homepage, those make the buyer think "this is legally complicated" instead of "this solves my problem."

## Final message hierarchy (use everywhere)

1. **Memory:** Keepance gives your firm a private AI memory for every client.
2. **Client Map:** That memory lives in a Client Map: people, history, goals, risks, documents, preferences, and open questions.
3. **Cited work:** Ask questions, draft, review, prepare, and hand off work with citations back to the source.
4. **Privacy:** Your files stay in folders you control, and every AI action shows where the request goes.
5. **Team continuity:** The client context no longer lives only in one person's head.

## Section: AI for people, not just facts

> AI for people, not just facts.
> Most AI tools know the world. Keepance knows your clients.
> Generic AI can summarize a document. Keepance builds context across the whole relationship: emails, files, notes, interviews, timelines, preferences, risks, and open questions. It helps your team understand the client behind the paperwork, then use that context to answer, draft, review, prepare, and hand off work.

Three cards:
- **Know the client** — See the people, history, goals, risks, preferences, and unresolved questions in one living map.
- **Find the source** — Ask plain-English questions and get answers tied to the exact file, email, page, or note.
- **Act with context** — Draft replies, documents, summaries, meeting notes, and follow-ups using the client's real history.

## Section: The Client Map (the heart)

> The Client Map
> Every client has a story. Keepance keeps it usable.
> A Client Map is a living profile built from the materials you already have: files, emails, notes, intake forms, meeting summaries, and guided interviews. It captures what matters, what changed, what is missing, and where each detail came from.

Feature blocks:
- **People & relationships** — Know the family members, companies, decision-makers, beneficiaries, opposing parties, advisors, and other people who shape the work.
- **History & timeline** — See what happened, when it happened, who said it, and which document or email proves it.
- **Goals & preferences** — Remember what the client cares about, how they like to communicate, what they worry about, and what good service means to them.
- **Risks & open questions** — See contradictions, missing details, incomplete intake, unsigned documents, stale information, and issues worth reviewing.
- **Firm context** — Apply your firm's philosophy, preferred language, templates, tone, and review standards across client work.

End: **The result is not just a better search box. It is a shared memory your team can actually use.**

## Section: Ask cited questions

> Ask questions across the full client file.
> Search makes you remember the file name. Keepance lets you ask the question you actually have.

Example questions: "What did the client agree to?" / "When did they first mention the issue?" / "Which version changed the indemnity clause?" / "What are the open questions before our next meeting?" / "What did we promise to send after the last call?"

> Every answer includes citations, so you can open the exact email, document, page, or note and verify it yourself.

Bullets: Search across imported files, emails, notes, and interviews · Scope every question to one client, matter, or household · Get citations to the exact source · Open the source in one click · Keep unrelated client files separated.

Example card (less litigation-specific):
> Your question: "When did the client first mention the delivery delay?"
> Keepance: The first mention appears in a March 4 email, where the client wrote that the shipment "missed the contractual window." The issue appears again in the March 18 meeting notes under "vendor delays."
> Sources: Email · Re: Q1 logistics · Mar 4 / Meeting_Notes.pdf · p. 3

## Section: Draft with context

> Professional work does not end in a chat window.
> Keepance helps you draft, review, redline, and export real documents. Work in Word and PDF, preserve tracked changes, and keep the client's context beside the work from first draft to final version.

Bullets: Draft documents, replies, summaries, and follow-ups · Review and redline Word documents with tracked changes · Export real .docx and PDF files · Preserve styles, numbering, tables, and comments · Use client-specific context instead of generic AI output.

(Avoid "If it isn't in Word with your letterhead, it isn't a real document." It sounds dismissive.)

## Section: Find gaps, risks, and contradictions

> See what does not line up.
> Keepance can read across a client's file and flag the things worth checking: inconsistent dates, missing signatures, stale information, open promises, contradictory statements, and facts that appear in one place but not another.
> It proposes. You decide. Every flag points back to a source.

Bullets: Build timelines from emails, notes, and documents · Find inconsistencies across the client file · Surface missing information before meetings or deadlines · Flag issues for professional review · Tie every suggestion to a source.

Example card:
> Flagged for review: The signing date may not match the project timeline.
> In the intake notes, the client said the agreement was signed before work began. The signed agreement is dated three days after the first invoice.
> Sources: Intake notes · p. 2 / Agreement_signed.pdf · p. 1 / Invoice_001.pdf · p. 1

## Section: Keep the team aligned (NEW)

> Onboard new staff without retelling the whole story.
> The most important client knowledge is often trapped in one person's head. Keepance turns that knowledge into a shared Client Map, so new team members can understand the relationship, the work, the risks, and the next step without interrupting the person who already knows.

Bullets: Give new team members a cited client overview · Preserve meeting notes, decisions, and promises · Track what changed since the last review · Keep client-specific preferences visible · Reduce repeated explanations and missed context.

## Section: Privacy / data path

> Privacy you can explain.
> Keepance shows where your data lives, where each AI request goes, and which mode you are using before you run it.
> Keepance is local-first. Your files live in folders you control, and every AI action shows where the request is going before it runs. For sensitive work, run a local model and keep the request on your machine. For stronger cloud models, bring your own AI key and send the prompt directly from your computer to your chosen provider. Either way, Keepance does not keep a copy of your client files in a Keepance cloud.

Three cards:
- **Local model** — Run with Ollama for work that should stay entirely on your machine.
- **Bring your own AI key** — Use Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google directly from your own account. The provider sees the prompt; Keepance does not route it through our server.
- **Firm deployment** — For teams that need admin controls, shared client files, ethical walls, and a more formal review path.

Then: **No hidden data path.** Every action shows a clear egress indicator: your machine only, or your chosen AI provider. You can see what is being sent, where it is going, and which client or matter it belongs to.

(REMOVE the combative line: "Microsoft 365 Copilot and the other cloud assistants cannot honestly promise they never see your data..." — competitors make nuanced enterprise privacy claims; this can backfire. Replace "The whole truth, told first, by us" with "Privacy you can explain." Replace "The paranoid mode for your most sensitive client work" with "For your most sensitive client work.")
