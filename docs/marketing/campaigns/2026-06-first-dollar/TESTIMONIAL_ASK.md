# Testimonial ask: Step 3

**Owner: Jameson.** The moment 1-3 people buy or actively use Keepance, ask for a short testimonial and permission to name them. This is the thing that unlocks the law/tax launch (zero social proof is the #1 conversion blocker right now). Don't rush it: let them actually use the app for a few days first, then ask.

---

## After someone bought it or has clearly been using it

> Hey [name], thank you for actually giving it a shot, it means a lot this early. Quick ask, no pressure: if it's been useful, would you be up for a sentence or two I could put on the site? With your name and what you do, or just your role, whichever you're comfortable with. And if it's been frustrating, I want to hear that even more, because you're one of the very first people to really use this. Either way I owe you one.

---

## If they say yes but freeze on what to write

People often want to help but don't know what to say. Make it a one-line answer:

> Honestly, whatever's true. Something like "what made you try it," "what surprised you," or "who you'd tell to use it." A single line is perfect. I'll never edit it into something you didn't say.

---

## Permission, plainly

Always get explicit, specific permission before anything goes live:

> Two quick things so I don't overstep: are you good with me using your first name and [role/company], or would you rather I keep it to just the role? And is the exact wording above okay to quote, or do you want to tweak it first?

---

## Notes

- A real first name and a real role beats a glowing anonymous quote. "Independent strategy consultant" with a first name is worth ten "★★★★★ amazing tool" lines.
- If they're in a regulated field and can't be named, an anonymized but specific quote ("a solo attorney in [region]") still works, with their okay.
- Log every testimonial and its permission status somewhere durable. A quote on the site without written permission is a liability, not proof.
