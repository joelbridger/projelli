# Battlecards: Condensed (Reviewer Kit)

**Purpose:** One-liners and when-to-concede statements for use during reviewer outreach and the review process itself. Tightened from `docs/marketing/competitive-battlecards.md`. Use these when a prospective reviewer asks how Keepance compares to what they already use, or when prepping a reviewer to field questions from their peers.

**Regulatory note (say it once, then move on):** Every regulatory reference below is informational only, not legal, tax, or compliance advice.

**Local-vs-cloud note (say it once):** "Nothing leaves your machine" is only true with a local model (Ollama, LM Studio). A cloud BYOK key sends the prompt to the provider. Different things.

---

## Legal

**vs Clio Duo**
One-liner: "Clio Duo knows your matters and your billing. Keepance is for the confidential drafting you'd rather not keep on any vendor's cloud, and it can run fully local."
When to concede: If their AI need is entirely around Clio workflow context (matters, billing, deadlines), Clio Duo is already there. Keepance is an add-on for the drafting work, not a replacement.

**vs CoCounsel**
One-liner: "CoCounsel is the right tool for Westlaw-grounded citation research. Keepance is for drafting the privilege log, the memo, the engagement letter, at $149/yr and with no vendor in the data path."
When to concede: Any workflow that requires citation-verified primary-authority research needs CoCounsel or a comparable tool. Keepance does not compete on legal research databases. Tell them plainly.

## Tax

**vs Intuit Assist**
One-liner: "Intuit Assist is good at advisory planning from data already in Intuit's system, and it's free. Keepance is for §7216-clean work where return data should stay on your machine, and it's the AI companion Drake never gave you."
When to concede: If they're on Lacerte and only need advisory planning summaries, Intuit Assist is free and already there. Keepance adds value for drafting, §7216 consent management, and WISP work.

**vs Blue J**
One-liner: "Blue J is excellent for primary-authority research with confidence scores. Keepance handles the drafting side: the memos, the engagement letters, the client correspondence, and it keeps the data local."
When to concede: If their primary pain is tax research that cites the Code and regulations, Blue J is purpose-built for it. Keepance is for the written deliverables that come after the research.

## Consulting

**vs M365 Copilot**
One-liner: "Use Copilot for the deck and the Office work. Use Keepance for the client-specific research and drafting a strict NDA no-upload clause would prohibit putting in any cloud, including Microsoft's."
When to concede: If they're on M365 Enterprise, Copilot is already paid for, and their deliverable is primarily a polished deck with no sensitive data constraints, Copilot is the right tool. Keepance adds the upstream private workspace.

**vs Gamma**
One-liner: "Use Gamma for the final deck. It's better at that than Keepance is. Use Keepance for the discovery synthesis, the research memos, and the confidential analysis you can't put in a cloud."
When to concede: Gamma produces better decks faster than Keepance does. If the primary deliverable is a visually polished presentation and there's no NDA restriction, Gamma is the right tool. Keepance and Gamma complement each other.

## Advisor

**vs Jump**
One-liner: "Jump is the category leader for meeting notes and CRM sync. Keepance is for the written work those notes feed into, and it's the only option where there's no AI vendor to vet for Reg S-P."
When to concede: If the primary pain is meeting-notes capture and CRM sync, Jump does that better than anything else in the market. Keepance doesn't compete on meeting notes. An advisor who uses both has a sensible stack.

**vs Zocks**
One-liner: "Zocks' no-recording approach is a genuine step up for privacy within meeting tools. The notes still reach their cloud. Keepance on a local model is where nothing reaches any cloud, and it covers the written work Zocks doesn't."
When to concede: If the advisor needs meeting-notes capture + eMoney sync, Zocks is a thoughtful option. Keepance handles the written deliverables. No reason to choose between them.

## Cross-vertical

**vs "I already use ChatGPT"**
One-liner: "ChatGPT Plus trains on your inputs unless you've opted out, and your work lives on OpenAI's servers. Keepance puts the conversation on your hard drive as a real file, with profession-specific templates, and a local model option where nothing leaves your machine."
When to concede: If their work doesn't touch confidential client data, they don't have NDA restrictions, and they just want a capable general-purpose AI, ChatGPT Business or Enterprise is hard to beat on raw capability. ChatGPT also has better mobile apps and image/video generation that Keepance doesn't offer.

---

## What not to say

- Never say a competitor "steals data." Say "cloud SaaS with a contractual no-training policy" for the ones that have it.
- Never imply that a cloud BYOK key equals zero egress. It doesn't.
- Never present Heppner as settled law. It's a leading case; later courts are diverging to fact-specific analysis.
- Never claim Keepance is a research tool. It's not. Acknowledge the database gap clearly and don't try to paper over it.
- Never promise SOC 2 or a signed DPA. We don't have them yet. Honest answer: "local-first architecture means your data doesn't reach us, but we haven't done a formal certification yet."
