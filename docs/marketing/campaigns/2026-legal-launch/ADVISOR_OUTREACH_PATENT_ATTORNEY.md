## The email

**Subject:** AI and patent confidentiality — building a local-first tool, would you review a template?

Hey [Name],

I'm Jameson Daines. I'm a product designer building a desktop app called Keepance, and I'm working on a template specifically for patent attorneys that I want to get in front of someone who actually does prosecution work before I publish it.

The product is a local-first AI workspace. Everything runs on your machine. No cloud, no server I control, API keys go direct from your computer to the provider. The confidentiality angle for patent work is actually more acute than for any other legal specialty, I think, because of how the EU absolute-novelty rule works. If you upload a draft application to a cloud AI tool before filing, you've potentially made a prior-art disclosure that can kill the patent internationally. That's a pretty serious thing to get wrong.

I'm building a Patent Disclosure Draft template that helps attorneys think through what they can and can't share with an AI tool at each stage of prosecution. But I'm not a patent attorney, and I want someone who is to tell me whether the framing is realistic or whether I'm missing how this actually plays out in practice.

The ask is small: one template, 10-15 minutes to read, a "this is right / this is how you'd actually get burned" conversation. I'd love to show you the product on a 15-minute call first.

If you're not the right person for this, I'd really appreciate a referral to someone doing prosecution work who is comfortable with legal tech conversations.

Thanks,
Jameson
keepance.com

---

## Notes

- **Target profile:** solo IP attorney or small IP boutique doing patent prosecution (not just licensing or litigation). AIPLA member is a good signal.
- **Where to find them:** AIPLA member directory, IPWatchdog comments section (practitioners who comment there are self-selected tech-savvy), LinkedIn search for "patent prosecution solo."
- **The citation to verify before any public use:** EU absolute-novelty rule is real doctrine but the specific framing ("premature cloud AI use destroys patentability worldwide") needs an actual patent attorney to confirm accuracy and nuance before it goes anywhere public.
