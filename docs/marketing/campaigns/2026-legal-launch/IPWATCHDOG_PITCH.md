## Pitch email

**Subject:** Guest post pitch: AI tools and prior art disclosure risk for patent practitioners

Hi Gene,

I'm Jameson Daines. I build software tools for attorneys and other professionals who work under confidentiality obligations. I'd like to pitch a guest post.

**Working title:** "The prior art problem nobody's talking about: patent attorneys, AI tools, and the absolute-novelty rule"

**The angle:**

There's a specific risk in the way most patent attorneys are using AI tools right now that I haven't seen addressed directly in the IP press. When a practitioner uploads a draft application, a technical disclosure, or even detailed client notes to a cloud-based AI service before filing, there's a real question about whether that upload constitutes a prior-art disclosure under the EU's absolute-novelty rule. Domestically the analysis is different (12-month grace period), but internationally the exposure is genuine and, for clients seeking broad protection, potentially catastrophic.

The piece would explain the technical mechanics of what happens when data goes to a cloud AI service, map that against the absolute-novelty doctrine, and cover what practitioners should be asking before they use any AI tool on pre-filing material. I'd also cover local-first tools as the cleanest solution to the problem: if the data never leaves your machine, the disclosure question doesn't arise.

I'm the developer of Advisor Prep Hero, one of those local-first tools. I'll be completely transparent about that. But the article would be genuinely educational, not a product pitch, and I'm happy to have you vet the legal framing before anything goes live. I'll be working with a patent attorney co-author [name TBD] to make sure the practice-side analysis is accurate.

Happy to send a full draft if the angle sounds worth pursuing.
