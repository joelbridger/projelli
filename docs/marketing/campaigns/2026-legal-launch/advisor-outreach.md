# Legal Advisor Outreach — 2026-05-27

Two variants below. **Variant A** goes to a solo or small-firm generalist attorney.
**Variant B** goes to a patent or IP attorney (the absolute-novelty hook is a different conversation).

Send one or the other, not both. They're cold outreach, so keep it exactly this length.

---

## Variant A — solo / small-firm generalist attorney

**Subject:** Quick ask — want a lawyer's eyes on something I'm building

Hi [Name],

I build software. For the last year and a half I've been working on something called Keepance, a local-first AI workspace that stores everything on your own hard drive, not on my servers. The AI calls go directly from your machine to Claude or OpenAI, never through me. Zero telemetry, no training pipeline, nothing leaves your office.

I'm building a Legal Practice template pack for it, aimed specifically at solo and small-firm attorneys who genuinely can't pipe client files into ChatGPT but also aren't in the market for a Harvey license. The product is actually designed for this use case. But I'm a designer, not a lawyer, and I need someone to read what I've built and tell me where I got it wrong.

The templates I have so far: a Deposition Contradiction Finder, an Evidence Gap Analyzer, a Case Timeline Builder, a Privilege Log Drafter, a Discovery Document Triage, and a Client Intake Synthesizer. I know what they should produce. I don't know whether they reflect how your workflow actually works, or whether the language would make a real practitioner close the tab immediately.

The ask is honest: read through the drafts, tell me what a solo attorney would actually use and what sounds like it was written by someone who has never billed an hour, and flag anything that looks legally careless in how it describes its own purpose. I'm estimating 2-3 hours total, split across however many weeks fits your schedule.

What I can offer: lifetime access for you and anyone at your firm, advisor credit on the legal-facing page, and I want to pay you for the time. Just tell me what makes sense.

If this sounds interesting, I'm happy to share the draft templates over email to start, no call needed. And if you know someone who'd be a better fit for this than you are, I'd genuinely appreciate the introduction.

## Variant B — patent / IP attorney

**Subject:** Quick ask — want a patent attorney's eyes on something I'm building

Hi [Name],

I build software. For the last year and a half I've been working on something called Keepance, a local-first AI workspace that keeps everything on your machine. No servers, no training pipeline, no third-party eyes on your client files. The AI calls route directly from your computer to whatever model you use.

I'm building a template pack specifically for IP attorneys. The reason I think it's actually worth something to patent practitioners is a thing I didn't fully understand until recently: using a cloud AI with an invention disclosure before filing might constitute prior disclosure in jurisdictions that require absolute novelty. Europe. Japan. Most of the world outside the US. That's not a hypothetical risk. It's the kind of thing that ends a filing.

The template I'm most focused on is a Patent Disclosure Draft tool, designed to let an attorney or inventor develop a disclosure completely offline, on local hardware, before any external API call ever happens. I also have a Prior Art Search Workspace, a Claims Drafting Assistant, and a cross-reference to the main litigation templates.

What I need from you: read through the drafts and tell me where the framing is legally careless, where the workflow doesn't reflect how you actually work with inventors, and where an IP attorney would close the tab and never come back. I'm a designer who got deep into the regulatory argument for local-first AI. I'm not a practitioner, and I know it shows.

This would be maybe 2-3 hours total, across however many weeks fits. What I can offer: lifetime access for you and your firm, advisor credit on the IP-facing page, and I want to pay you for the time.

If any of this sounds interesting, happy to share the drafts over email. And if you know someone at AIPLA or in the solo IP space who would find this more useful than you do, I'd genuinely value the introduction.

## Send order + notes

**Send Variant A first** (larger TAM, faster to find contacts). Target the ABA Solo & Small Firm Section member directory, Lawyerist community, r/LawFirm, state bar referral service.

**Send Variant B second** (or in parallel if you have an IP attorney in your existing network). AIPLA member directory is the fastest cold-contact path. IPWatchdog community has practitioners who post publicly and respond to founder outreach.

**One additional note on the Heppner citation:** Both variants eventually need to reference U.S. v. Heppner (Feb 2026 SDNY, ruling that consumer AI usage waives attorney-client privilege) in the landing page copy and the product description. That citation came from Claude's research report. It needs to be verified against Westlaw or CourtListener before it goes anywhere public. This is the most important pre-launch check.

The same caution applies to the EU absolute-novelty framing in Variant B. A patent attorney advisor reviewing the templates will either confirm the framing or correct it. Do not put the absolute-novelty argument in marketing copy before Variant B is reviewed.
