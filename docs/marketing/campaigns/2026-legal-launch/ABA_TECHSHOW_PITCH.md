## Session abstract (600 words, suitable for submission form)

Attorney-client privilege is one of the most foundational protections in the practice of law. It's also one of the most quietly vulnerable to the current generation of AI tools.

When an attorney uploads a client memo, a deposition transcript, or a draft contract to a cloud-based AI service, that document leaves the attorney's machine and travels to a server owned by a third party. Most practitioners assume their subscription agreement handles this. Most subscription agreements do not say what practitioners think they say.

ABA Formal Opinion 512, issued in July 2024, gave the profession its first formal guidance on AI use. The opinion was actually quite practical and, for the most part, technology-neutral: it focused on competence, confidentiality, and supervision of AI output. What it made clear is that the confidentiality analysis isn't just about the subscription's terms of service. It's about what happens to the data, where it goes, and whether the attorney has meaningfully evaluated that.

This session is for solo and small-firm practitioners who are using AI tools right now, want to keep using them, and want to make sure they're doing it in a way that is defensible if a client ever asks.

We'll cover:

**What the confidentiality risk actually is.** Not a theoretical exercise. Specifically: what happens when you paste a client document into a cloud AI tool, which providers offer what data protections, and what the Terms of Service actually say versus what the sales team says.

**The local-first alternative.** A category of AI tools has emerged that runs entirely on your machine. Your documents never leave your computer. Your API requests go direct from your machine to the AI provider. Nothing passes through a third-party server. We'll look at how these tools work technically, at a level of detail that is useful to practitioners without requiring a computer science background.

**The patent attorney's specific exposure.** For IP practitioners, there's an additional layer. Under the EU's absolute-novelty rule, premature disclosure of a patent application to a third party can destroy international patentability. The question of whether uploading a draft application to a cloud AI tool constitutes prior-art disclosure is not fully settled. Local-first tools sidestep the question entirely.

**Practical workflow comparisons.** We'll look at three common attorney workflows: research, document drafting, and deposition prep. For each one, we'll compare what a cloud tool interaction looks like versus a local-first tool interaction, and talk through the confidentiality exposure at each step.

**What to actually do.** Concrete guidance: what questions to ask before adopting any AI tool, what to look for in a Terms of Service, how to document your evaluation process, and how to talk to clients about your AI use policy.

The goal is not to make practitioners afraid of AI. It's the opposite. The attorneys who will use AI most effectively over the next decade are the ones who understand what's actually happening when they use it. This session is about building that understanding.
