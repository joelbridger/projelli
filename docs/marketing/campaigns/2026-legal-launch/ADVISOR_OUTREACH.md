## Email 1: Solo / small-firm generalist attorney

**To/context:** Solo practitioner or attorney at a 1-5 person firm, general practice or litigation. Probably has heard of ChatGPT for law but has real concerns about client confidentiality. Cold outreach. Found via r/LawFirm, Lawyerist community, state bar listservs, or lawyer LinkedIn.

**Subject:** Attorney review question: I'm building legal AI templates and need a real practitioner's feedback

Hi [Name],

I'm a product designer, not a lawyer. I've been building a desktop app called Advisor Prep Hero, and I'm about to ship a Legal Practice template pack for it. I need a practicing attorney to read the templates before they go out.

The short version of the app: Advisor Prep Hero runs AI models locally on your machine or through your own API keys. Nothing goes to my servers. Client files stay where they are. The ABA issued Formal Opinion 512 last July specifically blessing local-first AI tools like this one. Additionally, an SDNY ruling from February 2026 confirms that consumer cloud AI usage without attorney direction may waive attorney-client privilege: *United States v. Heppner*, No. 1:25-cr-00503-JSR (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 17, 2026), Dkt. No. 27 (Rakoff, J.). Those two data points are part of why I think this tool has a real place in solo and small-firm practice. But I'm not a lawyer, and I could be wrong about how it all fits together.

The templates are: Deposition Contradiction Finder, Evidence Gap Analyzer, Case Timeline Builder, Privilege Log Drafter, Discovery Document Triage, and Client Intake Synthesizer. Seven total. I know what I want them to do. I don't know whether they reflect how a real practice actually operates, or whether the language would make a practitioner close the tab in two seconds.

What I'm asking for is 2-3 hours of async work over however many weeks fits your schedule. Read the drafts, tell me what's wrong with them, mark up anything that looks legally careless in how it describes its own purpose or workflow. No calls required. Just email and a shared doc.

In return: free Professional lifetime license for you and anyone at your firm, a named acknowledgment in the app's credits on the legal page, and I want to pay you for the time. The going rate I've heard is $75-150/hr. You invoice me at whatever feels right, and I pay it.

If this sounds interesting, I can send the draft templates over email today. If you're not the right person but know a solo or small-firm attorney who cares about client confidentiality and follows the ABA tech standards closely, I'd genuinely appreciate the pointer.

jamesondaines@outlook.com

## Email 2: Patent / IP attorney (prosecution focus)

**To/context:** Patent attorney who handles prosecution, not just litigation. May work at a firm or solo. The absolute-novelty hook is the lead angle here. Found via AIPLA member directory, IPWatchdog community, LinkedIn search for "patent prosecution" + "solo" or small firm.

**Subject:** Quick question for an IP attorney: local-first AI and the absolute novelty problem

Hi [Name],

I'm a product designer building a desktop app called Advisor Prep Hero. It runs AI models locally on your machine or through your own API keys. Nothing goes to my servers, nothing is logged, client files stay wherever you put them.

I'm building a template pack specifically for IP attorneys, and there's one angle I keep coming back to that I haven't seen anyone talk about clearly: using a cloud AI with an invention disclosure before filing might constitute prior disclosure under the absolute novelty rule. Europe, Japan, Korea, most of the PCT world. If an inventor describes their invention to ChatGPT or Claude.ai to work through a patent disclosure draft, and that conversation is stored on a third-party server, the question of whether that's a public disclosure is genuinely unsettled. The safe answer is to not find out the hard way. The template I care most about is a Patent Disclosure Draft tool built to run completely offline before any external API call happens.

The rest of the legal pack has a Privilege Log Drafter, Discovery Document Triage, and Case Timeline Builder that overlap with prosecution support work. There's also a confirmed ruling on attorney-client privilege: *United States v. Heppner*, No. 1:25-cr-00503-JSR (S.D.N.Y. Feb. 17, 2026), Dkt. No. 27 (Rakoff, J.), holding that consumer AI usage without attorney direction can waive attorney-client privilege.

The ABA issued Formal Opinion 512 last July and it's notably friendly to local-first tools. That's the compliance angle I'm leading with in the product, but I need someone who actually does this work to read the templates and tell me where I've gotten the IP-specific framing wrong.

The ask is 2-3 hours total, async. Read through seven template drafts, mark up anything that doesn't reflect how prosecution actually works or that looks legally careless in how it describes its own function. No calls required.

What I can offer: free Professional lifetime license for you and your firm, a named acknowledgment in the app's credits on the IP page, and I want to pay you for your time. Invoice me at whatever feels right, $75-150/hr is the range I have in mind. You set the number.

Happy to send the drafts over email if any of this sounds worth a look. And if you know someone in the solo IP or AIPLA community who would find this more interesting than you do, I'd appreciate the introduction.

Jameson Daines
jamesondaines@outlook.com
