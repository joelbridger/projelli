## Guest column pitch (editorial — unpaid)

**To:** Kathryn Rubino or Joe Patrice (via atl.tips or DM on X/LinkedIn)

**Subject:** Column pitch: "The data-flow question your client hasn't asked about your AI use — yet"

Hi Kathryn,

I'm Jameson Daines. I build software for solo and small-firm attorneys, and I've been watching ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) get underread by the practitioners it was designed for.

Opinion 512 is the first formal ABA guidance on lawyers using AI tools. It's actually fairly permissive — it doesn't prohibit cloud AI use, it just says attorneys need to understand where their data goes and take competent precautions. Most solo and small-firm practitioners I talk to haven't read it. They're using ChatGPT or Claude Projects with client files, not because they're cutting corners, but because the guidance was released quietly and there's no plain-language version for practitioners who aren't already tracking ABA tech ethics.

The column I'd propose is roughly 900 words:

1. What Opinion 512 actually says (in plain terms, not the ABA committee summary)
2. The specific data-flow question it implies — where does your client's information go when you use a cloud AI tool, and is that disclosure documented anywhere?
3. What "competent precautions" looks like in practice for a solo attorney with no IT staff
4. Local-first AI as one option in the decision tree (disclosure: I make one)

I'd disclose my connection to Advisor Prep Hero at the top and keep the piece neutral on the product question — the argument stands regardless of what tool any attorney uses.

Is this the kind of column ATL runs in the legal tech track? Happy to send a draft.

Jameson Daines
keepance.com
