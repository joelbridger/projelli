# Why I built Keepance for financial advisors

*A one-pager you can read in two minutes or forward to a colleague. Customer-facing draft, written in Jameson's voice. No compliance claims. Send only with Jameson's go.*

---

You already pay for a dozen tools. Keepance isn't a thirteenth.

Here's what I kept seeing. A single client's story is scattered across everything you own. The notes live in Wealthbox. The plan is in RightCapital. Last year's return sits in Holistiplan. The statements come from Schwab. Years of back-and-forth are buried in Outlook, and the documents are somewhere in OneDrive. No one tool holds the whole picture of a household, and the one tool that could actually answer you, ChatGPT, is the one place you can't safely paste a client's private financial life.

So before a review you do the thing you always do. You open six tabs and you hunt.

I built Keepance for that hunt.

### What it actually does

It's a private workspace that runs on your own computer. You point it at one client's pile of files, and it reads across all of it and builds a single picture of the household: the goals, the accounts, the open questions, what's changed since last time. Then you can just ask it things. "What's still open before the Smith review?" "Did we ever finish the Roth conversion conversation?" And it answers.

The part I'm actually proud of is the next bit. Every answer shows its work. Each claim links straight to the source file, the date, the exact line it came from. You click it and you're looking at the real document. You're not trusting a black box, you're supervising it, which is the only way a fiduciary should ever use one of these.

And when you're ready to write the review letter or the follow-up, it drafts it from the real file, in Word, not from a blank page.

### Where your client data actually goes

This is the question every advisor asks me, so let me be straight about it.

Your client files, the search it builds, the whole client picture: all of it stays on your machine. There's no Keepance server in the middle holding any of it. I can't see your clients' names, your files, or the questions you ask. None of it reaches me.

The one thing that can leave is the AI itself, and you choose how. Run a local model and genuinely nothing leaves your computer at all. Or bring your own key from Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google, and the question goes straight from your machine to the provider you already have an account with, on your terms with them, never through me. In that mode the provider does see the text of your question, the way any cloud AI does. I'd rather tell you that plainly than pretend otherwise.

Why it matters for you: there's no outside vendor sitting on your client data, so there's one fewer company for you to vet and monitor under the rules you live by. It lowers your surface. It doesn't erase your duties, and I'll never tell you it does.

### What it is not

It's not your CRM. It's not your planner. It's not your custodian, and it's not your meeting notetaker. Keep all of those. Keepance sits quietly underneath them and reads from the work you already do.

It's the one place everything about a client finally comes together and answers back.

### Who I am

I'm Jameson. I'm a product designer, and I built this because the most useful AI out there keeps asking people to hand over exactly the data they're not allowed to hand over. Advisors feel that harder than almost anyone.

I'm looking for a few advisors to use it on real clients and tell me honestly where it falls short. If you handle sensitive client work and you've ever felt weird about what you paste into a chat window, I think this is for you.

If you want to try it, or you just think I'm getting something wrong, please let me know.
