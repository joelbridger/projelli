*The advisor version of the lawyer discovery campaign that's already live (`~/keepance-lawyer-interviews/`). Same machine, re-aimed. Honest researcher, not a sales pitch. Keepance is named only if they ask. Owner: Jameson runs the calls. Customer-facing pieces in his voice, no em dashes. Contact nobody until Jameson's go.*

This is for **ring 3** in the plan: widening past the warm circle into peer and community advisors. It does two jobs at once. It teaches you what advisors actually struggle with, and it warms up a few of them into a demo. Remember the weighting: for advisors, warm intros and demos beat cold email, so run this as a small, personal motion, not a 40-email blast.

## The motion (mirrors the lawyer build)

1. **Find them warm-ish.** XY Planning Network forum, the Kitces community, advisor podcasts and AdvisorTech roundups. People who've posted about AI or about keeping client data private are the best first asks.
2. **Invite by personal email.** The honest-researcher note below, one at a time, real detail in line one. Booking link included.
3. **They book a phone time** at the cal.com research-call link (cal collects their number).
4. **Jameson calls through the Quo app** at the booked time. The call records itself, transcribes, writes a summary, and files into the CRM automatically. Same plumbing as the lawyer campaign.
5. **Follow-ups are one-off and manual.** No auto-sequences. Nudge a non-replier about four days later, custom.

Funnel to expect, roughly: a small list of warm-ish advisors gives you a handful of replies, a few calls, and one or two who are pilot-curious. Quality over volume here.

## On the call

### How to open (say this out loud)

> Thanks for the time. Quick ground rule before we start: please don't share any real client details. I only care about how you work, not the specifics. Made-up examples are perfect. I'm not here to sell you anything, I'm honestly just trying to learn.

### The questions (let them do the talking)

Ask about what they actually do, not what they'd hypothetically like. The whole point is to get past politeness and hear real behavior.

1. Walk me through how you prep for a client meeting today, step by step. Where does everything live?
2. Have you used ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot in your real client work? Tell me about the last time.
3. What did you deliberately keep out of the tool? *(the privacy probe, the most important answer of the call)*
4. When you need to pull a client's whole picture together, the plan, the statements, the tax stuff, the email, how do you actually do that today?
5. When was the last time you wasted real time hunting for something in a client's file? What happened?
6. Do you reuse prior review letters, summaries, or notes across clients, or rebuild them each time?
7. How do you keep track of everything you know about a household, and what's still open, between meetings?
8. What parts of AI feel useful but risky to you?
9. What would make an AI workspace feel safe enough to use on a real client?
10. What would be an instant dealbreaker? And would you ever try a private pilot on a cleaned-up or sample client?

### How to close

> This was really helpful, thank you. I'll send you a short write-up of what I learn across all these conversations. I won't use your name or your firm in anything without asking you first.

If they leaned in hard and asked real questions (price, "can I try it"), that's the moment to offer a look at what you're building. Not before.

## Reading the signals

**Strong (write these down word for word):**
- "I use ChatGPT but I'm nervous about what I put in it."
- "Everything's scattered across six tools, it's a pain to pull together."
- "I rebuild the same review letter every quarter."
- "I'd try that on a sample client."
- "How does it handle the data side?" or "What would this cost?"

**Weak (polite, not real pain):**
- "Sounds interesting." / "AI's the future." / "Younger advisors would love that." / "Send me something later."

The strong signals are people to demo. The weak ones are people to thank and move on from.
