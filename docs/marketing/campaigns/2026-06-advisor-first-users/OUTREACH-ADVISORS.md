# Advisor outreach messages

*Customer-facing drafts in Jameson's voice. Owner: Jameson. One person at a time, personal, never a blast. Swap a real detail about the person into the first line every time, or it reads like a template. No compliance claims. No em dashes. Send only with Jameson's go.*

These are the advisor-specific versions. The older design-partner `OUTREACH.md` and `JOURNEY_BEYOND_WEALTH.md` were written attorney-generic. Use these for advisors. They map to the rings in the plan: forwardable intro (ring 1), Jameson-direct (ring 1 to 2), warm network (ring 2), community peer (ring 3).

---

## 1. The note the wife forwards (ring 1, warmest)

For a colleague at her firm, or her own advisor friend. She sends it, so it's in her voice, short.

> Hi [name], my husband Jameson builds software, and he's made something I actually think is up your alley. It runs entirely on your own computer, so client information never goes up to anyone's cloud. You point it at one client's files and it reads across all of them and answers questions about the household, with the source for everything it says. He's looking for a few advisors to try it on real work and tell him honestly what's useful and what isn't. No cost, no pitch. Want me to introduce you two?

## 2. Jameson direct to a warm advisor (ring 1 to 2)

When he has the contact and there's a real relationship to name. Built from the Journey Beyond Wealth note, tightened.

**Subject:** [referrer]'s husband, built a private tool for advisors

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm Jameson, [referrer]'s husband. She speaks really highly of working with you, so I hope it's alright I'm reaching out directly.
>
> I build software, and I spent the last few months on something I think fits your world well. It's called Keepance. It runs entirely on your own machine. You point it at one client's pile of files, the statements, the plan, the email, all of it, and it reads across the whole thing and answers questions about that household. Every answer links back to the exact file and line it came from, so you can check it. Nothing goes to my servers, and you bring your own AI key, so client financial details never leave your computer.
>
> I built it because so much of the useful AI out there asks you to hand it exactly the data you're not allowed to hand over. Advisors feel that more than almost anyone.
>
> I'm putting together a small group of advisors to use it on real clients and tell me honestly where it falls short. It's yours, free, for as long as you're helping me. The only thing I'm after is your honest read, the annoying parts most of all.
>
> Want to try it? I'm happy to get on a quick call and set you up, or just send you the download and stay out of your way.
>
> Thanks either way,
> Jameson

## 3. Warm network (ring 2)

Any advisor Jameson already knows, or a one-hop intro. Personal, low-pressure.

> Hey [name], I built a thing and you're exactly the kind of person I want using it before I make any noise about it.
>
> It's called Keepance. It's a private workspace that runs on your own computer. You point it at one client's files and it reads across all of them, builds a single picture of the household, and answers questions about it, with the source for every answer so you can check its work. Nothing routes through my servers, and you bring your own AI key. I built it for the client stuff you can't really paste into someone else's cloud, which is basically your whole job.
>
> I'm getting a small group of advisors who actually do this work to use it for real and tell me what's wrong with it. Full app, free, direct line to me, real say in what ships next. Honest feedback is all I want back.
>
> Want in? I'll get you set up.
>
> Jameson

## 4. Community peer (ring 3, honest-researcher)

For an advisor met through XY Planning Network, the Kitces community, or an advisor podcast or forum. They don't know Jameson. Lead with research, not a pitch, the way the lawyer campaign does. Name a real, specific thing about them in the first line.

**Subject:** quick research question for a solo advisor

> Hi [name], I saw [the specific thing: your post in the XYPN forum about AI / your episode on [podcast] / your write-up on keeping client data private]. I'm a product designer doing some research and I'd genuinely value your perspective.
>
> I'm trying to understand how solo and small-firm advisors actually use AI like ChatGPT in real client work, and especially what you deliberately keep out of it. I'm talking to a handful of advisors before I build too much more.
>
> Would you be up for 15 or 20 minutes? You can grab a time that works here: [cal.com link]. I'm not selling anything, I just want the honest read from someone who handles this data every day.
>
> Thanks so much, and no worries either way.
>
> Jameson

## After they say yes (onboarding note)

> Awesome, thank you. Here's the download: https://keepance.com. Grab the trial build and I'll flip your license to a comped one so there's no clock. When you first open it, pick "financial advisor" on the welcome screen and it sets you up with the right starting point. Try one real thing this week, something you'd actually do before a client meeting, and tell me where it annoyed you. The annoyances are the most useful thing you can give me.

---

## Notes

- One person at a time. The whole value of warm outreach is that it isn't a campaign.
- Comp the license the moment a design partner is in. Don't make them sit behind a trial clock.
- The testimonial ask is a separate, later message, only after they've gotten real value. Use `../2026-06-first-dollar/TESTIMONIAL_ASK.md`. Don't ask on day one.
- Track each advisor: who, firm, date onboarded, last check-in, testimonial status and permission. Keep it durable.
- No compliance claims even one-to-one. Local-first plus bring-your-own-key plus cited answers is the true story. Keep it there.
