# NAEA / AICPA Newsletter + Community Pitch

> **When:** Q3 2026. Target a placement that lands in member inboxes in October or November,
> right as tax-prep season ramps. The §7216 angle is seasonal: it's most relevant when practitioners
> are actively onboarding new clients and running engagement letters.
>
> **NAEA:** National Association of Enrolled Agents. ea.naea.org. Smaller, tighter community than AICPA.
> "The EA Journal" and member newsletter are the main written channels. Conference is annual (usually August).
>
> **AICPA:** Much larger. Tax Section newsletter, Journal of Accountancy. Guest submissions exist but
> editorial bar is higher. Start with NAEA, use traction there as proof for AICPA.
>
> **§7216 framing update:** Lead with civil §6713 (strict liability, $250/disclosure, no intent required, $10,000/year cap) rather than criminal §7216 (requires knowing or reckless conduct). Example updated framing: "IRC §6713 imposes strict-liability civil penalties of $250 per unauthorized disclosure of tax return information. For practitioners using cloud AI tools without written client consent, every session is a potential disclosure event." Criminal §7216 can be mentioned as a reinforcing note but should not be the headline.

## NAEA Annual Conference speaker pitch

> NAEA Annual Conference is typically in August. Speaker submissions open in the spring.
> The talk title and abstract are the same shape as the ABA TECHSHOW pitch but shorter (30-45 min).
> Recruit your CPA/EA advisor as co-presenter before submitting.

**Proposed title:** "§7216, AI tools, and the consent question your engagement letter doesn't answer"

**Abstract (200 words):**

Most enrolled agents are already using AI tools in their practice. Most of them haven't thought through whether their current engagement letter covers it.

IRC §7216 requires written consent before disclosing client tax return information to third parties. Cloud-based AI tools receive data from your machine and process it on their servers. Whether that constitutes a "disclosure" under §7216, and whether the tool's terms of service constitute adequate authorization, is not a question most practitioners have asked.

This session covers three things: what §7216 actually requires in the context of AI tool use, what practitioners should add to their engagement letters before the next tax season, and what "local-first" AI tools are and why they sidestep the disclosure analysis entirely.

The goal is not to make you afraid of AI. The goal is to make sure you are using it in a way that you can defend if a client ever asks. Attendees will leave with a model consent clause for their engagement letter, a checklist for evaluating any AI tool, and a clear picture of where the exposure is.

Presented by Jameson Daines (Advisor Prep Hero) and [CPA/EA advisor name, credentials].
