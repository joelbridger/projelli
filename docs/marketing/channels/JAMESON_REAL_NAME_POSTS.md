## Post type 3: Learning post (behavioral science / building things pillar)

### Use when
Something Projelli taught Jameson that's relevant to the personal brand's pillars. Projelli is the example, not the subject. Should sit comfortably in the 5% project-mention slot of the personal brand strategy.

### Template

> [Hook with a specific time anchor and observation, e.g., "Three weeks into running Projelli's onboarding emails, the data is clear about what's actually moving the needle and what isn't."]
>
> [The behavioral / building / health-tech relevant insight in 2-4 sentences. The insight is the point; Projelli is the source of the insight, not the topic.]
>
> [The reflection or implication for other founders / designers / health-tech people who follow you for the personal brand pillar.]
>
> [If naturally relevant: a one-line link to projelli.com. Often better to leave it off entirely and let the post stand on its own.]

### Example (hypothetical: post about activation rates)

> Three weeks into running Projelli's onboarding emails, the data is clear about what's actually moving the needle.
>
> Of the seven emails in the sequence, the day-one "how was your first session?" reply rate is 3x higher than every other email combined. Not because of subject line tweaks. Because the question itself invites a real answer instead of a click.
>
> The behavioral piece: most lifecycle emails are designed for the marketer (open rate, click rate). One question, sent at the moment a buyer is most uncertain, designed for the buyer (do they need help). That's the one that moved the metric I actually cared about.
>
> Worth a re-read for anyone designing onboarding sequences right now.

### Voice profile check
- Time anchor ("three weeks into") ✓
- Specific number (3x) ✓
- Observational, not authoritative ✓
- Honest about the alternative explanation ("Not because of subject line tweaks") ✓
- The personal brand pillar (behavioral science, design) is the actual subject ✓
- Projelli is the example, not the topic ✓
- No em dashes ✓

## Post type 4: "What I'm Learning" pillar (about shipping)

### Use when
A specific lesson from shipping Projelli that's interesting to other founders / makers. This is the personal brand strategy's pillar 5 ("What I'm Learning") slot.

### Template

> [The lesson framed as a single direct sentence, e.g., "Shipping a paid desktop app as a non-developer in 2026 is genuinely possible, but the path is not what the marketing makes it look like."]
>
> [2-4 sentences expanding on what the lesson actually was, with specific details. Names of tools, time estimates, real obstacles.]
>
> [The non-obvious part. The thing you'd tell yourself if you were starting over.]
>
> [Optional: a one-line "more here" with a link. Often better as a comment after the post lands.]

### Example (hypothetical: post about Tauri)

> Shipping a paid desktop app as a non-developer in 2026 is genuinely possible, but the path is not what the marketing makes it look like.
>
> Eight weeks of evenings to ship Projelli on Tauri 2 + React + Claude Code. The architecture and most of the React was AI-assisted. The Tauri part, less so. Cross-platform code signing, NSIS installer hooks, native filesystem permissions on macOS. Each took a day to figure out the first time.
>
> The non-obvious lesson: the AI is great at writing the feature. It's bad at the fifty operational pieces that turn a working feature into a downloadable installer. Plan for those to be a third of the project, not 5%.
>
> If you're building one of these, the path I took is mostly documented at projelli.com/blog.

### Voice profile check
- Specific tools named (Tauri, React, Claude Code, NSIS) ✓
- Specific timing ("eight weeks of evenings", "a day to figure out") ✓
- Honest about the hard part ✓
- Useful to the audience (other founders shipping software) ✓
- Anchors to the personal brand's "What I'm Learning" pillar ✓
