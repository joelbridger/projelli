# 10: Creative Experiments

_Written: 2026-04-29 by Claude (CEO mode), in response to Jameson's question: "any really creative unique ways to spread the word?"_
_Re-review: quarterly. Add new experiments as they're tested. Kill ones that didn't work._

This doc enumerates creative / unconventional marketing tactics for Projelli that don't fit cleanly into the standard channel menu in `09-non-paid-exposure-channels.md`. These are higher-variance ideas: novel framings, experimental formats, or tactics that punch above their weight when they hit but require taste to execute well.

**Read this in conjunction with:**
- `09-non-paid-exposure-channels.md` — the canonical distribution menu (boring tactics that compound)
- `07-anti-patterns.md` — the 22 traps to NOT fall into when feeling creative
- `00-master-strategy.md` § 8 — the test for every creative idea: "Does this move us toward being the answer when an indie founder asks 'what's a local-first AI workspace I can actually buy and own', faster?"

---

## 1. Honest framing

**Most "creative marketing" advice is bad.** The best marketing is usually consistent execution of boring tactics (SEO content, newsletter pitches, build-in-public). What looks creative often takes 10x the time for 1/10th the result.

**But a small number of creative tactics genuinely punch above their weight when:**
- They compound (audience asset, SEO, recurring artifacts) rather than being one-shot stunts
- They're tightly targeted to the actual ICP (not "everyone who might use AI")
- They cost time we have, not money we don't have
- They produce a content/social-proof byproduct in addition to direct attention
- They feel native to the product (not bolt-on marketing theater)

**Most fail one of those tests.** The 7 ideas in this doc pass them.

## 2. Tier A creative experiments (genuinely worth doing)

### 2.1 "Local-First Tools" podcast (Jameson as HOST)

**The idea:** Start a 30-minute weekly podcast where Jameson interviews ONE indie founder building local-first software. Don't promote Projelli in the show.

**Why it works:**
- Compounding audience asset (different from being a podcast guest, which is a one-shot spike)
- Establishes Jameson as a category voice in local-first AI tools
- Each episode is SEO content + searchable podcast directory entries forever
- Each guest becomes an ally + cross-promoter
- "Local-first" is small enough that there are 50+ founders worth interviewing in year 1; they all want exposure

**Effort:** ~3 hr/week (1 hr interview, 1 hr edit/produce, 1 hr promote)

**Cost:** ~$30/mo (Riverside.fm or similar) + one-time mic setup ~$200

**Compounding effect:** Every episode builds on the last. Audience grows monotonically. Three months in, Jameson is "the local-first guy" in the indie-tools community.

**Risks:**
- Sustainability — 3 hr/week is real time. Don't start unless committed for 6+ months minimum.
- Quality bar — bad podcast is worse than no podcast.

**When to start:** Phase 4-5 (post-launch), once there's a story arc + 30+ days of revenue data to discuss in the intro episode.

### 2.2 Annotated public Projelli workspace

**The idea:** Curate Jameson's actual Projelli workspace ("How I Built Projelli, in Projelli") into a public, browsable artifact at a URL like `projelli.com/workspace-tour/`. Real files: Vision.md, Pricing.md, Customer Persona.md, Launch Plan.md, MVP Scope.md — all interlinked, all annotated with notes explaining what was happening at the time.

**Why it works:**
- Live product demo at scale — beats any made-up demo because it's actually 18 months of work
- Social proof (you DOGFOOD your product publicly)
- SEO content (each file becomes a long-tail keyword target)
- Meta proof (Projelli built itself in itself)

**Effort:** 1 weekend to curate + redact sensitive details + ongoing 1 hr/quarter to refresh

**Cost:** $0

**When to ship:** Phase 4 (post-launch). Don't surface pre-launch — let the actual launch story land first.

### 2.3 Sticker packs (the indie-tool classic)

**The idea:** $200 buys 1,000 high-quality vinyl stickers with the Projelli coral mark. Form on the website: "Send me a free sticker." Mail one to every signup who provides an address.

**Why it works:**
- Postman, Linear, Vercel, 37signals, Stripe, Cloudflare ALL do this. The playbook is proven.
- Cost-per-impression is absurdly low — each sticker generates dozens of impressions over its life (laptop lid, coffee shop, conference, Zoom background)
- Visible to OTHER indie founders (highly targeted secondary distribution)
- The signup form itself is a lead-gen tool (collect email + address)
- Fan loyalty conversion: people who request stickers self-identified as fans

**Effort:** Setup ~2 hr (design + Sticker Mule order + form). Then ~5 min/week mailing.

**Cost:** ~$200 for 1,000 stickers. Postage ~$0.50 per envelope domestic, $1.50 international. So ~$700 total to mail 1,000 stickers. Or do batch shipping with first 100 buyers.

**Pricing model:** Free with email signup. Optionally: first 100 paying customers get a "Founder's Launch sticker pack" mailed to them automatically (collects address at checkout via LemonSqueezy custom field).

**When to ship:** Phase 3 (launch week). Sticker form goes live alongside the launch.

### 2.4 Reverse `/alternatives` page (12 competitors honestly compared)

**The idea:** Build a page at `projelli.com/alternatives/` that says "Here are the 12 alternatives to Projelli. Use the one that fits you best." Honestly compare them. Recommend competitors when their fit is genuinely better.

**Why it works:**
- Counter-intuitive positioning — most pages bash competitors; ours endorses them where appropriate
- Builds massive trust ("they recommended their competitor when it fit better, so they must really know the space")
- Ranks for every competitor's keyword (someone searches "Notion AI alternative", we appear; "Reflect alternative", we appear; "Mem.ai alternative", we appear)
- The "we're confident enough to recommend our competitors" message is shockingly rare and signals product-grade confidence
- We already have `docs/reference/COMPETITIVE_LANDSCAPE.md` (~12 competitors with honest analysis) — the content already exists internally, just needs to be web-rendered

**Effort:** 1 day to write the page. Ongoing 30 min/quarter to keep current.

**Cost:** $0

**When to ship:** Phase 4 (Engine 1 SEO push). Ideally month 2-3 post-launch.

### 2.5 The "100 Days of AI Files" public challenge

**The idea:** Public commitment from Jameson: 100 consecutive days, every meaningful AI conversation goes through Projelli. Daily tweet on @projelliproject with one screenshot of a real workspace file you created. Aggregate becomes a content series + dogfooding proof + 100 distinct social posts.

**Why it works:**
- Content gold mine — 100 posts in a single themed series
- Authentic dogfooding proof (the screenshots are real, time-stamped, verifiable)
- The "challenge" framing creates anticipation + social accountability + return visits
- Builds @projelliproject follower base over the course of the challenge
- Day-100 post can be a roundup with metrics ("100 days, 247 files created, here's the breakdown") — instant viral candidate

**Effort:** 5-10 min/day for 100 days. High discipline cost.

**Cost:** $0

**When to start:** Phase 3 or Phase 4. Could start earlier if Jameson has discipline confidence. Could also be 30-day or 60-day if 100 feels too much.

**Risk:** If Jameson breaks the streak after 30 days publicly, the negative signal is worse than not starting. Be honest with yourself about discipline before starting.

### 2.6 MicroConf physical presence (one event)

**The idea:** Attend MicroConf (the canonical indie founder conference, ~600 attendees, almost all indie SaaS founders, held annually in Austin or similar). Bring stickers + a small printed zine ("How I Built Projelli in 18 Weekends, the Honest Numbers"). Have 1-on-1 conversations. Don't pitch — listen.

**Why it works:**
- Highest-density ICP audience anywhere on the planet
- One in-person conversation = 100x the trust of a DM (or arguably even more — researched in B2B sales literature)
- The zine artifact persists past the conference (people take it home, share with their network)
- Founder relationships built at MicroConf compound for years

**Effort:** 3 days at the conference + travel time + ~2 hr to design and print the zine

**Cost:** ~$1.5K all-in (registration $599-1099, hotel $400, flights $300, zine printing ~$100, stickers ~$50)

**When to attend:** Phase 5 / Year 2. Gated on M3 ($500+ MRR sustained) per the financial framework — don't spend $1.5K on a side project that hasn't yet validated buyer interest.

### 2.7 "AI Conversation Archaeology" free standalone web tool

**The idea:** Build a tiny standalone web tool (separate from the Projelli desktop app) at `projelli.com/archaeology/` (or a separate domain) where people paste their ChatGPT export JSON and the tool shows them: "Here's everything you've talked to AI about in the past 6 months, categorized by topic." Show topic count, time spent, signal-to-noise ratio. Conclude: "Imagine if these were real files you could organize and search. That's Projelli."

**Why it works:**
- Free tool = lead generation magnet (no install required, immediate value)
- The output itself is shareable on social (people share their "AI usage profile")
- Demonstrates the problem Projelli solves WITHOUT requiring people to install anything
- Viral mechanics built in (curiosity-driven, share-driven)
- Sets up the natural CTA: "Want this organized into real files going forward? Download Projelli."

**Effort:** 2-3 days of engineering (Jameson has the muscle; can also delegate to me to draft)

**Cost:** $0 (runs on existing Projelli web infra, no AI inference required — this is just JSON parsing + categorization heuristics)

**When to ship:** Phase 4 (post-launch). Could ship anytime, but the launch week is a better moment to surface it.

## 3. Tier B stretch ideas (experimental, lower probability)

### 3.1 Custom Chrome extension: "ChatGPT Conversation Saver Reminder"

Small Chrome extension that detects long ChatGPT sessions and shows a non-intrusive notification: "This is a 30-min conversation. Worth saving as a file? Try Projelli."

**Effort:** 3-5 days engineering for a polished extension + Chrome Web Store submission process (~1 week review).

**Risk:** Extensions can feel intrusive. Must be opt-in, easily dismissible, and genuinely non-annoying. Also — Chrome Web Store has strict policies about referrer/affiliate behavior.

**When:** Phase 5+ if engineering bandwidth allows. Don't prioritize.

### 3.2 "Notion AI Subscription Receipt" graphic generator

Free web tool: paste your Notion AI subscription cost ($X/year), get a shareable graphic showing 3-year cost vs Projelli + BYOK. Funny, shareable, conversion-driving.

**Effort:** 1 day of engineering.

**Risk:** Could read as bashing competitor. Must be tonally neutral / informational. Per anti-pattern #15, "honest observation, not contempt."

**When:** Phase 4. Pair with the Notion AI math blog post (or replaces the original BYOK math tweet that we deferred).

### 3.3 Sponsor ONE deep-dive video by a respected local-first creator

Pay $500-1500 for a thoughtful video by Tom MacWright, Linus Lee, Anne Helen Petersen-tier writers/creators who genuinely care about local-first. NOT a paid sponsorship slot — a deep treatment in their voice. They retain full editorial control (including the ability to publish criticism).

**Effort:** Outreach + brief + payment ~5 hr.

**Cost:** $500-1500 per video.

**Risk:** Easy to confuse with influencer marketing (anti-pattern #7). The distinction: paying for SUBSTANTIVE editorial coverage from a respected voice ≠ paying for promotional placement. Critic must retain editorial independence.

**When:** Phase 5+. Gated on M3 ($500+ MRR sustained) and on identifying the right creator (don't force this if no obvious fit emerges).

### 3.4 "Build With Me" weekly challenge

Pick 1 random IndieHackers user per week with a real problem. Volunteer to use Projelli with them on Zoom (60 minutes, recorded with permission). They get free help, you get content + their amplification + product validation.

**Effort:** ~2 hr/week (1 hr session, 1 hr post-production / publish)

**Cost:** $0

**Risk:** Sustainability + finding willing participants. Stop if no good leads after 4 weeks of trying.

**When:** Phase 4-5.

## 4. Things that LOOK creative but don't work

### 4.1 ❌ Flyers (houses, apartment lobbies, coffee shop bulletin boards)
Wrong audience targeting. Indie founders aren't physically located in any one neighborhood. Even in tech-dense cities, hitting random houses produces ~99% non-ICP impressions. Conversion zero.

### 4.2 ❌ Billboards / outdoor advertising
Mass-market play. We can't compete on attention with companies that have $1M+ ad budgets. The signal-to-noise from a billboard for a $49 indie tool is laughably bad.

### 4.3 ❌ "Surprise and delight" swag boxes to random users
Feels creative but expensive and hard to scale. Most recipients don't convert because they weren't in the buying intent moment when they received the surprise. Better: stickers via opt-in (idea 2.3) which is cheaper AND targeted to people who self-identified as interested.

### 4.4 ❌ Skywriting, public art installations, viral stunts
The "creative agency portfolio" tier of marketing. None of it converts for indie tools. Looks fun, costs a lot, generates social media chatter that doesn't equal sales.

### 4.5 ❌ Mass cold outreach disguised as "personalized" (templates with first name swap)
Detected as spam, kills sender reputation, anti-pattern #6. Even if the wrapper is creative, the underlying mechanic is wrong.

### 4.6 ❌ Bartering / referral schemes that get gimmicky
"Get $20 off if you refer 5 friends" → most people refer no one, the few who do degrade the brand. Affiliate program (which is in the Phase 5 plan) is different: ~10-20% revenue share, only activates if there's enough volume.

### 4.7 ❌ "Hack" / drama / controversy marketing
Some founders launch with controversy on purpose. Sometimes works, more often blows up. Not worth the risk for an indie tool with a reputation to build.

## 6. Decision rule for new creative experiments

When a new creative idea surfaces (yours, mine, or a community suggestion), run it through these 5 questions:

1. **Does this compound, or is it a one-shot stunt?**
   - Compounds = audience asset, SEO content, recurring artifact = consider seriously
   - One-shot = burn time once, get attention briefly, no follow-on = usually skip

2. **Is the targeting tight to the indie founder ICP?**
   - Tight = specific channels, specific audiences = consider
   - Broad = "everyone who might use AI" = skip

3. **Does it cost time we have, or money we don't?**
   - Time within the 5-10 hr/week budget = consider
   - Money beyond the M3-gated newsletter sponsorship budget = skip until M3

4. **Does it produce a content / social-proof byproduct?**
   - Yes = double-counts as marketing artifact + content engine = consider
   - No = single-purpose effort = lower priority

5. **Does it feel native to the product, or bolt-on theater?**
   - Native = makes the product MORE itself (dogfood, public workspace, etc.) = consider
   - Theater = generic guerrilla marketing tropes = skip

**Pass all 5: ship it.** Fail any: think harder before spending time.
