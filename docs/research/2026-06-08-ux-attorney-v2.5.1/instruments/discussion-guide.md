# Discussion Guide: Generative Interview (Session 1, ~60 min)

**Study:** Keepance v2.5.1, attorney segment
**Moderator:** Dr. Lena Whitfield
**Format:** Remote, recorded with consent. Semi-structured. No product shown until Section 6 (concept reaction is verbal only; the interface is reserved for Session 2).
**Golden rules:** Open questions only. Behavior and stories over opinions. Embrace silence. Never sell, never defend, never lead. When she gives an opinion, ask for the story behind it ("Tell me about the last time that happened").

Timings are guides, not gates. Total ~60 minutes.

---

## Section 0: Welcome and consent (0:00-0:04)

- Thank her. Reiterate: research not sales, nothing shared with clients/firm, recording with consent, can stop anytime, no wrong answers.
- "I'm going to spend most of our time just understanding how you actually work. I'll show you a little about what we're building near the end, and I want your honest reaction, including the unflattering parts. The unflattering parts are the most useful."
- Confirm recording consent on the record.

---

## Section 1: Warm-up and context (0:04-0:12)

*Objective: establish her practice, firm, and a concrete picture of a normal day.*

- Tell me about your practice. What kind of law, who are your clients, how big is the firm?
- How long have you been practicing? How did the firm get to its current shape?
- Walk me through yesterday, or the last normal workday, from when you sat down to when you left. *(Let her narrate. This surfaces real workflow and real pain without prompting.)*
- Probes: What took longer than it should have? What's the part of the day you dread? When are you at your best?

---

## Section 2: Tools and workflows (0:12-0:22)

*Objective: map her actual stack and how work flows through it.*

- What software do you live in during a normal day? *(Let her list; don't prompt names.)*
- For each major one she names: What do you use it for? What do you love, what makes you want to throw the laptop?
- Walk me through how a document goes from "I need to write this" to "it's out the door to the client or the court." *(Format, tools, handoffs.)*
- How does email fit into your work? Is it a tool, a filing cabinet, a to-do list, all of it?
- When you need to find something from the past, a document or an email or a decision, how do you do that today?
- Probes: Where do things live? What's your backup situation? Who else touches your files? What happens when you can't find something?

---

## Section 3: Pain points, deep dive (0:22-0:34)

*Objective: get the ranked, visceral pains via critical-incident technique. This is the heart of the generative session.*

- You mentioned [pain from earlier]. Tell me about the last time that bit you. What happened, start to finish?
- **Email/search specifically:** Tell me about the last time you needed something from your email and couldn't find it. What were you looking for? What did you do? How long did it take? How did it feel? *(Probe the cost: time, money, client perception, missed deadline risk.)*
- **Document-heavy work:** Tell me about the most painful document slog you've had recently. Discovery, a deposition, a big exhibit set. How do you get through it today?
- **Drafting:** Which documents do you wish you didn't have to start from scratch? What's the most repetitive writing in your week?
- If you could wave a wand and make one part of your week disappear, which part, and why that one?
- Probes throughout: How often does this happen? What does it cost you (hours, dollars, sleep, client trust)? What have you tried? Why didn't it work?

---

## Section 4: AI: use, attitudes, and the confidentiality line (0:34-0:46)

*Objective: her real relationship with AI and her mental model of confidentiality and "the cloud." Probe the model carefully without correcting it.*

- Have you used any AI tools for work? Tell me about that. *(If yes: what for, what happened, do you still? If no: what's held you back?)*
- *(If she tried and stopped)* What made you stop or pull back? Tell me the moment.
- When you think about using AI on actual client matters, what goes through your mind?
- I've heard lawyers talk about confidentiality with these tools. What does that mean to you, in practical terms? *(Let her define it. Do not supply the answer.)*
- When you use a tool "in the cloud," what's your understanding of where your information goes and who can see it? *(This is the key mental-model probe. Listen for whether she distinguishes "the app stores my data" from "my question is sent to an AI company." Do not correct her; note the gap.)*
- What have you heard, from the bar, from colleagues, from the news, about AI and your professional duties? *(Listen for Avianca, Heppner, ABA 512, CLEs, war stories.)*
- What would have to be true about an AI tool for you to trust it with a real client's information?
- Probes: Who do you trust to tell you something is safe? What would make you distrust it instantly?

---

## Section 5: Adoption, trust, and money (0:46-0:52)

*Objective: how she decides to adopt professional software, and her real reaction to cost structure (without yet pitching Keepance's price).*

- Think about the last new tool you actually adopted for your practice. How did that decision happen? Who or what convinced you?
- What makes you abandon a tool in the first ten minutes?
- When you evaluate professional software, what signals tell you it's legit versus a toy?
- How do you think about paying for tools like this? What feels fair, what feels like a trap?
- Probes: Subscriptions versus one-time? What's a tool worth if it saves you three hours a week? How much does "other lawyers use it" matter?

---

## Section 6: Concept reaction (verbal only) (0:52-0:58)

*Objective: unled reaction to Keepance's core idea, BEFORE seeing the interface. Present plainly, in one breath, then stop talking and listen. Do not oversell. Do not show screens.*

Present, in plain language and only once:

> "Here's the idea I want your gut reaction to. It's a desktop app for confidential work. Your files stay on your own computer, in your own folders, never on the company's servers. You connect your own account with an AI provider like Claude or OpenAI, so your questions go straight from your machine to them and never through us. And the newest piece: it can pull in your email, keep it encrypted on your machine, and let you search all of it and ask the AI questions about it, like 'what did my client say about the deadline,' and get the actual email back. Nothing leaves your computer except the question you choose to send to the AI."

Then:
- What's your honest first reaction?
- What's appealing, if anything? What are you skeptical about?
- *(Probe each pillar separately, in her words):*
  - The "your files stay on your machine" part: does that matter to you? Why or why not?
  - The "your questions go straight to the AI, not through us" part: does that change anything for you? *(Check comprehension.)*
  - The email part: if that worked, what would it be worth? What would you need to believe to trust it?
- What's missing? What's the catch you're looking for?
- Who in your world is this for, and who is it not for?

---

## Section 7: Wrap (0:58-1:00)

- If you had a magic wand and could add one thing to what I described, what would it be?
- Is there anything I should have asked you about how you work that I didn't?
- Anything you want to ask me?
- Thank her. Explain Session 2 (hands-on with the actual app, think-aloud). Confirm logistics.

---

## Facilitator reminders
- If she asks "does it do X?" during the interview, deflect gently: "I want your expectation first. What would you hope?" Save the real answer for Session 2.
- Note exact phrases verbatim; her language is the deliverable.
- Flag every moment her mental model of "cloud" / "confidentiality" diverges from how Keepance actually works. That gap is a primary finding.

## Addendum (2026-06-08): probes added after deep-research integration

These probes were added after synthesizing the two deep-research reports (S3, S4). Weave them into the sections noted; do not read them as a separate block.

**Into Section 4 (AI, confidentiality):**
- When you send a question to an AI tool, what do you think the AI company itself does with your question? Do they keep it? Could they train on it? (Probes the provider-exposure gap. Most users do not distinguish "the app's servers" from "the AI provider." Do not correct; note the model.)
- Suppose every AI conversation you had was saved as a permanent file on your computer. In a matter that later goes into discovery, how would you think about those files? (Probes the discoverable-ESI / work-product concern that S3 raised. Critical for litigators.)
- When you write to an AI, are you ever putting your own strategy or theory of the case into the question itself? How would you feel about that being saved? (Probes work-product exposure.)

**Into Section 5 (adoption, trust, money):**
- If you wanted to use a tool like this across your whole firm, not just yourself, who would have to approve it, and what would they ask for? (Surfaces the firm-versus-solo question and the risk-committee / IT-diligence threshold. Informs the customer-definition fork.)
- How do your associate and paralegal fit into the tools you choose? Does a tool that lives only on your machine help the team or fracture it?
- Have you ever been asked by a client, or asked a vendor yourself, for a security or data-handling document (a SOC 2, a DPA, a data-flow diagram)? What would you need to see to trust a small or new vendor with client data?

**Into Section 6 (concept reaction):**
- After the concept description, ask directly: in your own words, when you ask the AI about your email, what leaves your computer and what stays? (Tests whether the honest data story is understood.)
- If your files were plain files on your disk that you could open in any other program, would you want them locked or encrypted, or do you prefer the openness? (Tests the plaintext-portability versus encryption tension before showing the UI.)
